## Overreaches

Ordered by severity. Each item is a place where the adjudication may have overcalled mandatory inclusion, smuggled scope widening past the comparison standard, or treated contested single-lane claims with more weight than their standing supports.

### 1. `HIGH` — Three new governing heuristics were installed as standing doctrine without adversarial challenge

- [o:c+r:i] `R5.17d3` installs the "governing authority vs. must-edit-now" distinction as standing doctrine (`.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d3-governance-exclusion-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:57`): "Without it, R5.17d would have no principled way to reconcile 'non-propagation is a real failure' with 'the doctrine files do not themselves need rewriting.'" `R5.17d4` cross-vendor elevates this to "standing adjudication rule for future exclusion/adjudication lanes" (`.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d4-operational-consequences-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:79`). The ratification is circular: both lanes depend on it; therefore it survives. That is not post-falsificationist validation. It is usefulness-as-ratification, which the doctrine specifically rejects at `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:147` ("one elegant criticism gets sovereign status by itself" — the twin failure mode is one elegant distinction getting sovereign status by itself).
- [o:c+r:i] `R5.17d4` installs the "intersection-not-union" rule as scope construction for `R5.18`: "intersection of what both lanes call invalid plus the cross-vendor Mandatory Disposition Targets that the internal lane does not contradict" (`.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d3-governance-exclusion-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:15`; carried forward at `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d4-operational-consequences-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:17,73-77`). The construction is not a clean intersection — "does not contradict" is silent-assent, closer to union-via-silence. The rule is adopted as governing without being tested against anti-regret: why is "silent non-contradiction" a sufficient basis for promotion into presumptive edit scope? Under the same standard that `R5.17a` applied to `thin wrapper` or `secondary surface`, "does not contradict" would itself be a burden-bearing exclusion-heuristic candidate.
- [o:c+r:i] `R5.17d1` / `R5.17d4` install "mandatory conditional disposition" as the governing framing for `gsd-verify-work` and `gsd-execute-phase`: wrapper edit-worthiness is tied to Track C trunk landing (`.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d1-wrapper-exclusion-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:63-67`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/REVIEWS/checkpoint-5-r5-17d4-operational-consequences-adjudication-cross-vendor-opus-r1.md:39,83`). The move converts an exclusion judgment ("safe to exclude now") into a conditional inclusion ("include when upstream lands"), which buys time without resolving whether the wrapper's current misframing is itself ground for edit. Under anti-regret, conditional inclusion is a scope claim that needs justification — specifically, why isn't the wrapper's current advertisement of a state model it cannot actually carry (`.codex/skills/gsd-verify-work/SKILL.md:48-54` two-state vs live three-state workflow) the reason to edit now? The framing is load-bearing but not itself audited.
- [o:c+r:i] All three heuristics solve real problems. But `R5.17` was commissioned precisely because prior exclusion heuristics — also useful, also solving real problems — were not defended under direct file-line scrutiny. Promoting three new governing heuristics through the same adjudication that is supposed to challenge that exact pattern is an overreach by construction, even if each individual heuristic turns out to be correct.
