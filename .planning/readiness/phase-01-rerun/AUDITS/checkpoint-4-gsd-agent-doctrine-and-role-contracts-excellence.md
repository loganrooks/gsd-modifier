## Current Strengths

- [e:c:i] The phase-critical GSD chain is not thin by default. `discuss-phase` is explicitly tasked with reducing avoidable ambiguity, capturing assumptions/open questions/future awareness, and keeping downstream agents from reopening preventable ambiguity. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/discuss-phase.md:2-7,15-29,48-79`.
- [e:c:i] `gsd-phase-researcher` is the strongest current claim-discipline surface in the stack. It requires source tagging for every factual claim, treats training knowledge as hypothesis, forces uncertainty reporting, and carries an assumptions log forward for planner/discuss use. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:18-33,84-113,354-363`.
- [e:c:i] `gsd-planner` meaningfully resists silent scope reduction. It treats locked decisions as non-negotiable, forbids placeholder/simplification language, and requires a phase split rather than quiet degradation when full-fidelity implementation will not fit. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:27-34,59-127`.
- [e:c:i] The workflow-injected planning contract is future-aware in a real way, not just rhetorically. `plan-phase` forces canonical refs into downstream reads, tells planner/checker to preserve future-awareness items explicitly, and requires auditable `future_preservation` frontmatter when future-awareness exists. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:295-305,672-716,747-758,820-849`.
- [e:c:i] `gsd-plan-checker` and `gsd-verifier` both reject mere completion theater. The checker uses goal-backward reasoning before execution, and the verifier explicitly distrusts `SUMMARY.md`, verifies roadmap success criteria plus must-haves, and checks existence/substance/wiring rather than file presence alone. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:13-31,70-90,100-220`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:13-30,46-58,99-143,730-746`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/verification-patterns.md:5-15`.
- [e:c:i] Review pressure is at least explicit in the harness. There is a dedicated cross-AI review workflow for plans, a planner reviews mode, and a scoped code reviewer with explicit file lists, severity buckets, and line-number requirements. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:1-9,70-135,188-248`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/planner-reviews.md:1-39`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/code-review.md:119-233,315-416`; `.codex/agents/gsd-code-reviewer.md:237-358`.

## Where Role Contracts Carry The Quality Bar Well

- [e:c:i] `Decision fidelity` is a real shared doctrine, not a vibe. Researcher, planner, and plan-checker all treat CONTEXT decisions as locked, deferred ideas as out of scope, and user intent as something to preserve rather than reinterpret. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:53-80`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:59-127`; `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:55-68`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/discuss-phase.md:89-115`.
- [e:c:i] `AGENTS reach` is materially present on the modernized `.md` chain. Researcher must extract AGENTS constraints into `RESEARCH.md`, the checker has a full AGENTS-compliance dimension, and executor treats AGENTS directives as hard constraints that override contradictory plan instructions. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:39-50`; `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:454-496`; `.codex/agents/gsd-executor.md:31-46`.
- [e:c:i] `Future-aware planning` is carried more by prompt injection than by standalone agent files, but it is still operative doctrine. The planner is told to map future-awareness items to preserved seams, sequencing choices, validation tasks, or explicit non-action rationale, and the checker is told to fail generic or missing `future_preservation` entries. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:694-703,711-716,757-758,840-845`.
- [e:c:i] `Anti-shallow execution` is not absent. The planner prompt forces concrete action text, mandatory `read_first`, grep-verifiable acceptance criteria, automated verification commands, and task sizing that tries to keep execution within a quality-preserving context budget. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:718-758`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:214-257`.
- [e:c:i] `Post-execution skepticism` is genuinely strong. The verifier is asked to verify roadmap truths regardless of what the plans claimed, distrust summary narratives, keep gaps structured for replanning, and refuse to commit closure on its own. Sources: `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:21-30,103-143,697-745`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/gates.md:18-34`.

## Where Role Contracts Still Encourage Thinness

- [e:c+r:i] The single biggest thinness risk is contract drift on the surfaces most likely to be live. The repo registry points named agents at `.toml` files, and those `.toml` contracts still tell workers to read `CLAUDE.md`, use `Claude`-specific discretion language, and look in legacy skill paths, while the `.md` siblings and workflow injections carry the newer AGENTS-oriented doctrine. That means the stack has not yet earned a clean claim that repo doctrine reliably reaches the actual launched workers. Sources: `.codex/config.toml:20-114`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.toml:29-44`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.toml:37-50,124-130`; `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.toml:36-45,447-465`; `.codex/agents/gsd-executor.toml:27-38`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.toml:14-33`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDITS/checkpoint-3-gsd-agent-doctrine-and-role-contracts.md:42-47,68,73-75`.
- [e:c+r:i] Claim discipline is sharply uneven. The researcher requires explicit provenance tags and an assumptions log, but the planner, checker, verifier, code reviewer, and cross-AI review workflow do not require the repo's `[type:support:basis]` claim markers, explicit evidence/inference splits, or source-basis exposure in their own load-bearing outputs. The result is a system where the upstream research artifact is epistemically labeled, but the downstream planning/review/verification artifacts can still sound more settled than their visible basis. Sources: `AGENTS.md:70-83`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:70-80`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:28-33,354-363`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:709-758,851-854`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:623-726`; `.codex/agents/gsd-code-reviewer.md:261-317`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:117-135,188-243`.
- [e:c+r:i] Pushback duty is still narrow instead of generalized. The planner will push back against decision simplification by recommending phase splits, but I did not find a comparable standing instruction for planner/checker/verifier/reviewer roles to challenge weak framing, canon drift, long-arc ambiguity, or quality-lowering shortcuts the way repo doctrine explicitly requires. On the current surfaces, pushback is strongest around scope fidelity and weaker around doctrine fidelity. Sources: `AGENTS.md:57-69`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:46-58,100-112`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:90-127`; `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:454-527`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:117-135`.
- [e:c+r:i] Review pressure is explicit but still too forgiving by default. The cross-AI review prompt asks for generic strengths/concerns/risk, then the synthesis shape elevates only agreed strengths/concerns and treats divergent views as secondary, while `planner-reviews.md` makes HIGH-severity consensus concerns the only must-address bucket. A lone incisive criticism can therefore be procedurally downgraded even when it is the most important thing in the file. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:117-135,188-243`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/planner-reviews.md:13-24,28-39`.
- [e:c:i] The code-review surface is structurally softer than the repo's stated desire for strong review boundaries. `execute-phase` marks code review as required but advisory-only, explicitly says it never blocks execution, and proceeds even when the review fails or the review invocation errors. That keeps velocity high, but it is weaker reread pressure than the repo's `strong review boundaries` posture would suggest. Sources: `AI-GUARDRAILS.md:100-110`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/execute-phase.md:744-775`.
- [e:c:i] The discuss-stage advisor seam still violates the stack's own anti-generic-agent doctrine. `universal-anti-patterns.md` says never use non-GSD agent types, yet `discuss-phase` spawns `general-purpose` and manually points it at `gsd-advisor-researcher.md`. Even if this works in practice, it is under-specified relative to a repo that is explicitly auditing role-contract integrity. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/references/universal-anti-patterns.md:22-25`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/discuss-phase.md:624-646`.
- [e:c+r:i] The planner prompt still contains an unbalanced throughput bias. The same contract that forbids shallow scope reduction also says `Ship Fast`, emphasizes keeping plans under half the context window, and frames 70%+ context as `rushed, minimal`; absent an equally explicit repo-doctrine instruction inside the planner role to prefer doctrine uplift or stronger future-aware reasoning when warranted, this can still bias toward smaller green plans over opportunity-seeking judgment. Sources: `AGENTS.md:47-69`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:98-112`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:129-167`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:694-703,718-758`.

## Pushback / Review / Claim-Discipline Assessment

- [e:c+r:i] `Pushback`: present, but too local. The current stack has real resistance to decision simplification and scope creep, especially in discuss/planner/checker, but it does not yet propagate the repo's broader duty to challenge weaker methods, thin closure, or future-distorting framing across the full worker chain. Sources: `AGENTS.md:57-69`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:51-58,100-112`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/discuss-phase.md:89-115`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:90-127`.
- [e:c+r:i] `Review`: explicit, but still optimized for coverage more than adversarial sharpness. Cross-AI review exists and is valuable, yet its prompt is generic, its synthesis privileges agreement, and code review is non-blocking. That is better than no reread surface, but it still leaves too much to reviewer goodwill rather than structurally demanding the strongest criticism. Sources: `AI-GUARDRAILS.md:102-110`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:1-9,117-135,188-243`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/planner-reviews.md:13-24`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/execute-phase.md:744-775`.
- [e:c+r:i] `Claim discipline`: strong in research, weak everywhere else. The repo wants claim type/source-basis exposure on load-bearing planning/process artifacts, but the current role contracts only make provenance mandatory for `RESEARCH.md`; downstream plan, review, and verification artifacts are still largely prose contracts with no formal evidence-basis discipline. Sources: `AGENTS.md:70-83`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:70-80`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:28-33,354-363`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:623-726`; `.codex/agents/gsd-code-reviewer.md:261-317`.
- [e:r:i] My bottom-line assessment is that the stack already carries a serious `completion is not enough` doctrine for the phase-critical chain, but it does not yet carry the repo's full `excellence under adversarial reread` doctrine with equal force. The missing pieces are not primarily about adding more commands; they are about making worker prompts and review protocols demand better judgment more explicitly and more consistently. Sources: `AGENTS.md:47-83`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:42-112`; `.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:23-31`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:21-30`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:117-135`.

## Strongest Justified Criticisms

- [e:c+r:i] `The operative contract surface is still split between modern doctrine and legacy launch-facing overlays.` The repo has done meaningful AGENTS-era prompt modernization, but the registered `.toml` contracts still carry legacy `CLAUDE.md` and legacy-skill assumptions. For a lane about operative role contracts, that is the sharpest criticism available because it directly attacks whether the best doctrine is the doctrine workers are most likely to receive. Sources: `.codex/config.toml:20-114`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:39-50`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.toml:29-44`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:44-57`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.toml:37-50`.
- [e:c+r:i] `Review doctrine is still more consensus-and-advisory than adversarial-and-binding.` The review stack asks for cross-AI feedback, but then proceduralizes it into agreed concerns, medium-consensus thresholds, and non-blocking code review. That is not the same thing as encoding a duty to surface and preserve the strongest criticism. Sources: `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/review.md:117-135,231-243`; `.codex/get-shit-done/references/planner-reviews.md:13-24`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/execute-phase.md:744-775`; `AI-GUARDRAILS.md:102-110`.
- [e:c+r:i] `Claim discipline does not survive downstream from research into the rest of the load-bearing planning loop.` The repo's doctrine asks for claim status/source-basis exposure on planning/process artifacts, but the current contracts largely stop at plain prose once planning, review, and verification begin. This leaves too much room for authoritative-sounding closure without visible epistemic basis. Sources: `AGENTS.md:70-83`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:70-80`; `.codex/agents/gsd-phase-researcher.md:28-33`; `.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/plan-phase.md:709-758`; `.codex/agents/gsd-verifier.md:623-726`.
- [e:c+r:i] `The core planner still carries an unresolved speed doctrine.` Its strongest modern protections are real, but they coexist with `Ship Fast` and context-efficiency pressure not obviously rebalanced by equally explicit prompt-level duty to seize higher-quality opportunities, escalate doctrine-sensitive ambiguity, or prefer stronger future-aware reasoning when needed. In this repo, that tension is now substantive, not cosmetic. Sources: `AGENTS.md:47-69`; `.planning/AGENTS.md:98-112`; `.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:129-167`.

## Strategic Opportunities

- [p:r:i] Unify the role-contract surface so the registered launch-facing files and the modern AGENTS-era doctrine stop disagreeing about what project instructions, skill locations, and builder identity even are.
- [p:r:i] Extend claim-discipline expectations beyond `RESEARCH.md` into planner, checker, verifier, and review outputs with a lightweight scheme that preserves readability while exposing evidence/inference/source-basis on load-bearing judgments.
- [p:r:i] Recast review prompts and synthesis rules so they explicitly preserve lone high-signal criticisms, future-awareness continuity checks, and doctrine-sensitive rereads rather than mostly rewarding reviewer overlap.
- [p:r:i] Decide whether code review is meant to be a real quality boundary or merely a logging surface. The current contract says `required` and `non-blocking` at the same time; the repo should eventually choose the intended pressure model more cleanly.
- [p:r:i] Either formalize the advisor-mode exception to the no-generic-agent rule with explicit doctrine and constraints, or stop routing that path through `general-purpose`.
- [p:r:i] Bring the repo's broader pushback duty into worker prompts explicitly so future-aware doctrine, canon-seam integrity, and anti-thinness judgment do not depend mainly on a good orchestrator remembering to compensate.
