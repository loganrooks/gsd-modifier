# Checkpoint 4 Workflow / Harness Excellence Audit Bundle

This bundle defines how Checkpoint 4 should be executed from the accepted Checkpoint 3 scope result.

## Purpose

Checkpoint 4 is not another mapping pass.

Its job is to pressure-test the accepted Codex+GSD audit envelope against the repo's higher standard:

- not whether the current workflow can technically produce an artifact
- but whether it reliably drives toward the best planning, research, execution, review, and verification work this repo can currently support

The bundle must therefore decide, with explicit ownership:

- what is already genuinely strong
- what is merely pass/fail-thin
- what quality opportunity is being left unused
- what belongs to doc-level doctrine
- what belongs to workflow protocol
- what truly belongs to machinery-owned follow-through in Checkpoint 5

## Shared Excellence Touchstones

Across all Checkpoint 4 lanes, treat a surface as merely adequate when it mainly:

- catches obvious failure after the fact
- relies on operator memory or goodwill to stay rigorous
- produces reviewable-looking artifacts without materially improving judgment
- preserves closure while leaving high-value quality opportunities unused

Treat a surface as strong when it reliably:

- preserves load-bearing distinctions without flattening them
- pressures weak reasoning before it hardens into plan or execution
- produces artifacts that can survive later expert reread
- reduces future re-litigation rather than merely unblocking the next step
- exposes meaningful opportunities for better work rather than hiding behind pass/fail success

## Mandatory Seam Set

Every lane should preserve and feed evidence into the seam set, even when the seam has a natural primary home:

- `future-awareness and canonical-ref continuity`
- `AGENTS/governance reach into operative workers`
- `named-agent authority and reasoning-policy truth`
- `continuity under compaction or resume`
- `execution-completion plus verification or UAT closure`
- `branch/worktree boundary materialization`

The later seam synthesis must assign:

- primary owner
- secondary affected lanes
- current strength
- failure mode
- likely ownership of follow-through if the seam is weak

## Launch Order

- Launch lanes `1` through `4` first.
- Do not launch `5` until all four lane outputs exist.
- Do not launch `6` until the seam synthesis exists.

## Split Rule

If any Checkpoint 4 lane concludes that its assigned surface is still too broad for one honest excellence audit:

- stop and say so explicitly
- name the proposed split
- justify why the split is load-bearing rather than merely convenient
- do not bluff completeness

Do not silently widen the lane or bury the split pressure inside vague prose.

## Global Non-Goals

- do not patch the harness in Checkpoint 4
- do not patch governance docs in Checkpoint 4
- do not decide that Checkpoint 5 must open just because machinery is interesting
- do not confuse broad command coverage with real control of the quality bar
- do not accept `good enough to pass` as a closure condition
- do not silently widen the accepted Checkpoint 3 seam set; if a new seam becomes necessary, justify the expansion explicitly against the accepted Checkpoint 3 result

## Closure Standard

Checkpoint 4 is not complete when it merely produces a cleaner description of the harness.

It is complete only when:

- the repo has a defensible answer about whether the current workflow stack is driving excellence or mostly enforcing minima
- doc-level, workflow-protocol, and machinery-owned problems are separated cleanly
- the strongest justified criticisms are explicit
- quality opportunities are explicit rather than silently deferred
- the repo can decide whether Checkpoint 5 should open without guesswork
