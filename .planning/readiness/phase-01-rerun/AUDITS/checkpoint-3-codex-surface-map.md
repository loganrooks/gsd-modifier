## Load-Bearing Codex Layers

- [e:c+r:i+d] The first real load-bearing layer is instruction and config precedence, not the size of `.codex/`. Root `AGENTS.md` and `.planning/AGENTS.md` govern artifact rigor, spawn discipline, and source-basis labeling ([AGENTS.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/AGENTS.md:70), lines 70-80; [AGENTS.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/AGENTS.md:100), lines 100-124; [.planning/AGENTS.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.planning/AGENTS.md:42), lines 42-82). Official Codex docs then add the outer rule: project-scoped `.codex/config.toml` only applies as a trusted-project layer, and `AGENTS.md` is searched from the current directory upward, with direct user instructions still winning.[^official-config][^official-agents]
- [e:c+r:i+d] The second load-bearing layer is the joint repo-local runtime seam between `.codex/config.toml` and `.planning/config.json`. The former sets Codex-facing defaults: `gpt-5.4`, `high` reasoning by default, `xhigh` in plan mode, the readiness compact prompt, and the hook feature flag ([.codex/config.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/config.toml:1), lines 1-18). The latter sets GSD-side automation posture: research, plan checking, verifier, Nyquist validation, auto-advance, model overrides, `mode = "yolo"`, and `granularity = "fine"` ([.planning/config.json](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.planning/config.json:15), lines 15-47). `WORKFLOW.md` explicitly says to treat that GSD config as harness-state, not durable doctrine ([WORKFLOW.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/WORKFLOW.md:138), lines 138-152).
- [e:c+r:i+d] The third load-bearing layer is the narrow hook pilot. Repo-local hooks are real but intentionally small: a `SessionStart` reminder and a Bash-only `PreToolUse` denylist ([.codex/hooks.json](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/hooks.json:2), lines 2-29; [session_start_guardrail.py](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/hooks/session_start_guardrail.py:47), lines 47-76; [pre_tool_use_guardrail.py](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/hooks/pre_tool_use_guardrail.py:8), lines 8-35). Official docs reinforce the narrowness: hooks are under development, disabled by default, and bound to specific event types rather than acting as a general policy engine.[^official-hooks] This makes the repo hook layer load-bearing but not broad.
- [e:c+r:i+d] The fourth load-bearing layer is compaction continuity. The repo explicitly points `experimental_compact_prompt_file` at `tooling/compact-prompts/readiness.md` ([.codex/config.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/config.toml:1), lines 1-4), and that prompt preserves the active readiness package, read order, current checkpoint, blockers, review constraints, and update obligations across summarization ([readiness.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/tooling/compact-prompts/readiness.md:1), lines 1-65). This is one of the smallest but highest-leverage surfaces in the current repo.
- [e:c+r:i+d] The fifth load-bearing layer is the agent registry plus instruction-file drift. `.codex/config.toml` registers named agents by absolute `.toml` `config_file` path ([.codex/config.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/config.toml:20), lines 20-114). The `.toml` files still tell key workers to read `./CLAUDE.md` and look in `.claude/skills/` or `.agents/skills/` ([gsd-planner.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-planner.toml:37), lines 37-50; [gsd-executor.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-executor.toml:24), lines 24-39; [gsd-plan-checker.toml](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.toml:33), lines 33-46). Their `.md` siblings have been updated toward `AGENTS.md` language instead ([gsd-planner.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-planner.md:44), lines 44-57; [gsd-executor.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-executor.md:31), lines 31-46; [gsd-plan-checker.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/.codex/agents/gsd-plan-checker.md:40), lines 40-53). Official current docs prominently describe custom agents as markdown-based subagents, which makes this repo's `.toml` registry look operationally real but weakly aligned with the main official narrative.[^official-subagents]
- [e:c+r:i+d] The sixth load-bearing layer is spawn-time reality checking. Root `AGENTS.md` requires effective launch settings to be verified against `~/.codex/state_5.sqlite` after every spawn ([AGENTS.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/AGENTS.md:123), lines 123-124), and `AI-GUARDRAILS.md` warns not to confuse requested settings with effective ones ([AI-GUARDRAILS.md](/home/rookslog/workspace/projects/prix-guesser/AI-GUARDRAILS.md:87), lines 87-99). ## What Checkpoint 4 must inspect

- [g:c+r:i+d] Inspect effective spawn settings, not just file-declared intent: model, reasoning, sandbox, approval behavior, and inherited overrides. Root `AGENTS.md` already says this, and official subagent docs make it more urgent.[^official-subagents]
- [g:c+r:i+d] Inspect the authoritative agent-instruction path. The `.toml`/`.md` drift is already strong enough that a deeper audit should decide whether runtime authority, human-facing docs, and official Codex expectations are aligned or dangerously split.
- [g:c+r:i+d] Inspect hook coverage gaps. Repo-local hooks cover only session start and Bash pre-tool use, while official hooks are event-limited and under development; later audit should identify what harmful actions remain outside that coverage.[^official-hooks]
- [g:c+r:i+d] Inspect continuity under compaction and resume. The readiness compact prompt is clearly load-bearing, but Checkpoint 4 should test whether it preserves the right facts and whether those facts match the session re-entry doctrine.
- [g:c+r:i+d] Inspect the Codex-to-GSD seam as a seam, not as a stock product assumption. The live behavior likely depends on repo-local patches, `.planning/config.json`, and agent prompt payloads more than on generic GSD or generic Codex narratives.
- [g:c+r:i+d] If later audit needs cloud or remote-execution conclusions, split that lane explicitly. Official cloud-task internet, setup-script, and environment controls are real, but they are not the same surface as the repo's local Codex harness.[^official-cloud]

## What can stay lighter

- [s:c:i] A full per-agent semantic review of all 24 repo-local agents can stay lighter in Checkpoint 3. The important finding here is the registry authority and drift pattern, not a line-by-line audit of every worker prompt.
- [s:c:i] Deep stock-GSD workflow internals can stay lighter until a later checkpoint shows that the repo-local overlay or a specific workflow seam is actually carrying the observed problem.
- [s:c:d] Official release-note or marketing-style Codex surfaces can stay lighter once their concrete capability/limitation claims have been cross-checked against the primary docs.
- [s:c:d] Older or closed unofficial anecdotes can stay light unless they explain a still-live drift question. They are useful as history, not as current guarantees.
