# Post-Falsificationist Review Doctrine

This note defines the epistemic posture the readiness package is trying to operationalize.

It exists because `Checkpoint 5` is no longer only about patching a few harness files. It is also about making the review and audit structure better at navigating uncertainty, under-owned consequences, and premature closure without collapsing into either soft verification or reflexive attack posture. [e:c:i] Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/GATES/checkpoint-5.md:24-29,73-79`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/TASKS.md:14-16`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:140-155`.

## Purpose

- [g:c:i] The readiness package should not treat `passes review` as the governing standard. It should ask whether the current line of work is strong enough to carry forward, whether it survives later scrutiny, and whether it closes real uncertainty responsibly rather than cosmetically. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:140-155`.
- [g:c:i] The package should also not treat a single successful attack as sovereign. Competing audits must be compared by spec quality, source coverage, self-justification quality, and claim survivability rather than by sheer aggression, vendor, or reasoning setting. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:7-10,16-22,38-54,58-82,102-105`.
- [e:c+r:i] This stance fits the actual Checkpoint 4 and Checkpoint 5 problem shape. The strongest converged criticism is not that the harness lacks all structure; it is that strong upstream structure still weakens into closure-biased downstream disposition and under-owned propagation. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDITS/checkpoint-4-cross-lane-seam-synthesis.md:54-57`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/GATES/checkpoint-5.md:53-64,73-81`.

## Working Definitions

### Verificationist Review

- [s:b:i] `Verificationist review` means a review posture that asks mainly whether an artifact can be accepted, whether it nominally satisfies its stated criteria, or whether no obvious blocker was found.
- [e:c:i] In this package, that posture is known to undercall missing scope, premature closure, and under-owned downstream consequences. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:26-34`.

### Falsificationist Review

- [s:b:i] `Falsificationist review` means a review posture that asks mainly how to break or refute the candidate artifact, often by pressure-testing the strongest available criticism.
- [e:c:i] In this package, that posture is useful but incomplete. It can surface neglected risks, but it can also over-promote expansion and treat one aggressive challenge as if it were automatically governing. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:26-34,67-71`.

### Post-Verificationist Review

- [s:b:i] `Post-verificationist review` means refusing to stop at `it passes`.
- [s:b:i] It asks what remains materially weak, under-justified, under-propagated, or misleadingly complete even when the artifact can technically clear a pass/fail gate.

### Post-Falsificationist Review

- [s:b:i] `Post-falsificationist review` means refusing to treat a single successful attack, anomaly, or adversarial framing as automatically sovereign.
- [s:b:i] It compares competing lines of reasoning, competing audits, and competing artifact-system interpretations by how well they explain the evidence, survive scrutiny, and improve the surrounding doctrine or workflow rather than by whether one sharp criticism can be staged.
- [e:c+r:i] In package practice, this means using pressure-oriented audits as challenge artifacts, but qualifying them through comparison against stronger gap-exposure and completeness-challenge audits before promoting their claims into governing scope. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:67-82`.

### Epistemic Gap

- [s:b:i] An `epistemic gap` is a weakness in the warrant structure of an artifact or checkpoint judgment.
- [s:b:i] It is present when a conclusion appears stronger, cleaner, or more settled than the evidence, inference chain, comparison set, anomaly accounting, or downstream operationalization actually justifies.
- [s:b:i] Useful subtypes include:
  - `evidential gap`
  - `inferential gap`
  - `comparative gap`
  - `anomaly-accounting gap`
  - `propagation gap`
  - `operationalization gap`
  - `standards gap`
  - `scope gap`

### Gap Exposure

- [s:b:i] `Gap exposure` is a review mode whose job is to surface and classify material gaps without collapsing into either soft approval or reflexive contrarianism.
- [e:c:i] In this package, it is the preferred governing posture when the real question is whether a candidate stack is still incomplete, under-propagated, or prematurely closed. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:29-34,67-82`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:149-155`.

### Completeness Challenge

- [s:b:i] A `completeness challenge` asks whether an artifact that presents itself as closure-ready has actually owned the producer/consumer chain, anomaly set, standards of judgment, and downstream consequences its own claims make relevant.
- [s:b:i] It is not a demand for impossible totality. It is a demand that declared completeness survive scrutiny proportional to the checkpoint’s ambition and risk.

### Progressive And Degenerating Response

- [s:b:i] A `progressive response` solves a real problem while improving explanatory and operational coherence across the surrounding harness.
- [s:b:i] A `degenerating response` protects the checkpoint cosmetically while multiplying exceptions, ambiguity, hidden debt, or downstream reinterpretation pressure.

## What Counts As A Real Gap

- [g:c:i] A claimed gap should not govern just because it sounds plausible or demanding.
- [g:c:i] A claimed gap should be able to answer:
  - where the gap lives
  - what kind of gap it is
  - which sources support the claim
  - why it matters downstream
  - what the right disposition is
  Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:109-136`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:47-54,58-82`.
- [g:c:i] If a reviewer cannot make those points, the result is suspicion or pressure, not yet a governing gap.
- [g:c+i] Not every quality opportunity is automatically a gap. An opportunity becomes a gap when leaving it untouched would materially weaken the checkpoint’s truthfulness, downstream reliability, or resistance to later audit.

## What This Changes In Practice

### 1. Checkpoint Closure

- [g:c:i] Checkpoint closure should not be granted merely because no fatal contradiction was found.
- [g:c:i] Closure should require explicit anomaly accounting, explicit residual-risk accounting, and clear separation between clean completion and debt-carrying completion where that distinction matters. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:140-161`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDITS/checkpoint-4-cross-lane-seam-synthesis.md:24,48,55,62`.

### 2. Audit Production

- [g:c:i] Audit specs should not ask only `is this correct?`
- [g:c:i] They should also ask:
  - what remains under-owned?
  - what remains under-propagated?
  - what has been treated as settled too early?
  - what future foreclosure or re-litigation pressure is being created?
- [g:c+i] Strong spec-writing also requires read-set discipline:
  - include the governing protocol rather than only indirect doctrine summaries
  - distinguish candidate, consumer, and chain-tail / representation surfaces where propagation matters
  - require at least one question about read-set adequacy so the spec can challenge its own likely blind spots
  - give adjudicators enough direct surfaces to verify contested claims rather than trusting prior lane outputs wholesale
  - give reread lanes enough material to judge evidence-base adequacy, not just conclusion quality
- [g:c+i] When sibling lanes use parallel output structures for comparison, the adjudication layer should explicitly guard against false convergence by structure.
- [e:c:i] This is already the direction of the package’s stronger audit posture. The protocol and comparison policy both reject naive pass/fail and naive model-prestige resolution. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:131-155`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:9-10,67-82,102-105`.

### 3. Audit Comparison

- [g:c:i] When multiple audits exist, do not flatten them into consensus.
- [g:c:i] Compare them by:
  - governing spec quality
  - epistemic posture
  - source coverage
  - independence
  - self-justification quality
  - claim survivability
  Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md:12-63`.
- [g:c+i] A pressure artifact can still be valuable, but it should usually serve as a challenge input rather than immediate governing doctrine unless its claims survive comparison against stronger-framed audits.

### 4. Propagation Analysis

- [g:c:i] A post-falsificationist stance does not stop at the edited file.
- [g:c:i] It asks what producer/consumer chain the change activates and whether the surrounding templates, prompts, workflow handoffs, wrappers, and status language still misstate the new doctrine.
- [e:c+r:i] This is directly relevant to Checkpoint 5 because the active question is not only whether Track B/C surfaces were improved, but whether the promoted review posture actually propagates into the real consumers of review output and launch truth. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/TASKS.md:14-16`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/GATES/checkpoint-5.md:24-29,56-63,74-79`.

### 5. Deviation, Deferral, And Opportunity Handling

- [g:c:i] Post-falsificationist practice requires explicit disposition, not silent omission.
- [g:c:i] Real anomalies and real opportunities should be routed visibly into:
  - `TASKS.md`
  - `DEFERRED.md`
  - `OPPORTUNITIES.md`
  - `DEVIATIONS.md`
  depending on whether they block, later-bind, or widen the line of work.
- [e:c:i] The package already has these routing surfaces and requires explicit classification before moving on. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PROTOCOL.md:109-136`.

### 6. Anti-Regret Scope Judgment

- [g:c+i] When deciding whether to keep a problem local or promote a wider lane, do not ask only whether promotion is justified.
- [g:c+i] Also ask whether non-promotion is itself justified.
- [g:c+i] Non-promotion is not a neutral default. It is a scope claim that can leave quality gains, anomaly-accounting work, or under-owned downstream consequences on the table if adopted too cheaply.
- [g:c+i] In practice, this means every locality judgment should be read as a two-sided claim:
  - `promotion is not required`
  - `non-promotion is defensible without avoidable regret`
- [e:c:i] This is directly relevant to the active Checkpoint 5 propagation-audit bundle, because the package now treats the Track B/C adjudication as incomplete until the locality/non-promotion judgment has itself been reread under the same post-falsificationist standard. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/TASKS.md:15-18`; `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/GATES/checkpoint-5.md:24-30,73-80,92-100`.

## What It Does Not Mean

- [g:c+i] It does not mean reviewers should be rude.
- [g:c+i] It does not mean every artifact must be attacked until something breaks.
- [g:c+i] It does not mean every wider concern automatically becomes active checkpoint scope.
- [g:c+i] It does not mean one `xhigh` artifact, one Claude artifact, or one elegant criticism gets sovereign status by itself.
- [g:c+i] It does mean that closure, scope, and strength claims should earn their authority under explicit comparison and explicit anomaly accounting.

## Practical Questions For Future Audits

- [g:c+i] What is the strongest justified criticism that survives direct comparison with the artifact’s own evidence?
- [g:c+i] What is merely adequate but not actually strong?
- [g:c+i] What does the artifact ask downstream consumers to assume without owning those assumptions explicitly?
- [g:c+i] Which anomalies are being explained, and which are merely being stepped around?
- [g:c+i] Is the current response progressive or degenerating?
- [g:c+i] If this checkpoint closed today, what later audit pain or re-litigation pressure would we be knowingly exporting?
