## What The Proposal Still Compresses Or Risks

- [d:r:i] **Annotation posture is under-specified as a shape**. `114:32-34` describes annotation as "Add an explicit held `.claude` runtime row plus a bounded note that the repo currently has a second observed runtime with different version / carrier posture." The 11-r1 reread at `11-r1:83` named the same candidate as "keep `observed_basis_only` and add a named held-`.claude`-version entry." These are not the same shape. A "held runtime row" implies a structurally parallel entry with posture metadata; a "named held-`.claude`-version entry" implies a single scalar in the held_later array or a compact field. `114` folds these into one line. Inside [.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json:78-101] the difference is material: a held row would structurally mirror `observed_runtime_version_set` and require a parallel source path, alignment state, and check-protocol entry; a held scalar would sit beside the existing three `held_later` strings at [.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json:96-100]. For a decision surface whose whole purpose is picking a shape, this is the detail that carries most and the proposal thins it most.
- [d:r:i] **The dual-basis risk is framed without criteria**. `114:36-39` says dual-basis "risks suggesting a stronger equivalence than the current routes and consumers have earned." The claim is plausible, but the 11-r1 reread at `11-r1:82` named dual-basis as `observed_basis_.codex_plus_explicitly_held_.claude` — a label that carries `observed` posture for `.codex` and `held` posture for `.claude`. That is not stronger equivalence than annotation; it is the same equivalence surface wearing a different label. The difference between annotation and dual-basis is whether the top-level posture label changes (`observed_basis_only` → `observed_basis_.codex_plus_explicitly_held_.claude`), not whether `.claude` becomes a first-class observed runtime. `114` compresses a label-discipline question into an equivalence-risk framing, and the parent thread loses the more articulable distinction.
- [d:r:i] **The typed-carrier risk is framed against the current field rather than against the held family-6 mapping lane**. `114:42-44` says typed-carrier "introduces a new carrier family earlier than the current field seems to require." But the wider route-asymmetry field (family 6 at [../outputs/12-uplift-cross-runtime-concern-family-split-opus47-max-r1.md:107-121]) is explicitly held as a mapping lane; the proposal cannot yet know whether the full field warrants a separate typed carrier. Saying the field "does not seem to require" a new carrier now borrows a judgment the mapping lane has not yet made. The stronger articulation is that typed-carrier is the shape most load-bearing on information the held lane would produce, so it should be the shape held until family-6 mapping lands, not the shape rejected on appetite.
- [d:r:i] **The recommendation flattens three co-equal candidates into one preferred direction in the space of a few lines**. 12-r1 at `12-r1:184-216` carried a long argument for family-10 moving first, with named precedence rules and explicit rebuttals for family-6 and family-3. `114:50-56` carries five bullets totaling roughly 90 words to pick a shape among three candidates. The 113 split posture at `113:58-66` was: "the next bounded lane should act on the concern-family split itself rather than choosing among these three by ambient preference." `114`'s short recommendation risks the very ambient-preference mode `113` warned against. A sharper package would articulate, per candidate shape, (a) what it lets the anchor carry that the current shape does not, (b) what it does not authorize about the posture label, (c) what the implementation footprint looks like in [tooling/codex/project_uplift.py](../../../../../tooling/codex/project_uplift.py) and [.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json](../../../../../.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json), and (d) the empirical signal that would revise the recommendation.
- [d:r:i] **The version-gap evidence is cited indirectly**. 12-r1 at `12-r1:82-83, 12-r1:88-89` named the `.codex` `1.38.3` versus `.claude` `1.34.2` four-minor-version gap as the motivating concrete that annotation would surface inside the anchor. Direct inspection confirms [.codex/get-shit-done/VERSION] carries `1.38.3` and [.claude/get-shit-done/VERSION] carries `1.34.2`. `114:23-26` instead points at `43` for the anchor posture and leaves the version gap outside the proposal body. The proposal's argument becomes harder to evaluate without its motivating evidence inlined; a reader who has only `114` open sees "widen the anchor, if at all" without the concrete asymmetry that makes widening matter.
- [d:r:i] **The verification gates are containment gates, not selection gates**. `114:66-71` carries four gates. All four apply equally to annotation, dual-basis, and typed-carrier; none of them would fail one shape and pass another. The result is that the gates secure the proposal's boundary but do not discipline the shape choice. A selection gate the proposal could carry: "the chosen shape must preserve the top-level `compatibility_posture` string as `observed_basis_only` until a later lane earns a stronger posture label." That gate passes annotation, passes typed-carrier (which sits outside uplift memory), and fails dual-basis (which relabels the posture). Without such a gate, the three candidates travel under the same discipline.
- [d:r:i] **The "opens first" argument silently forecloses the parallel option**. 12-r1 at `12-r1:199` and `12-r1:210-216` stated family-6 mapping "can open in parallel with family 10 since surfaces do not collide." `114:14-20` argues family-10 opens first because it is a decision surface and because it gates family-3. Both arguments are true; neither argument says family-6 must wait. `114` does not name the parallel option at all, which reads as a sequential commitment the 12-r1 analysis did not earn. The field thins: later operators reading `114` alone will see family-6 as blocked when 12-r1 explicitly kept it parallel-able.
- [d:r:i] **Annotation's effect on `build_compatibility_basis()` is unspecified**. [tooling/codex/project_uplift.py:270-302] carries the helper that writes the anchor. At `project_uplift.py:22, 67-75` it hard-codes `.codex/get-shit-done/VERSION` via `RUNTIME_VERSION_REL_PATH` while separately enumerating `.codex, .claude, .gemini, .config/opencode, .opencode, .config/kilo, .kilo` in `RUNTIME_DIRS`. An annotation that carries `.claude`'s version into the anchor has two plausible helper shapes: a new `CLAUDE_RUNTIME_VERSION_REL_PATH` constant alongside the existing one (narrow addition, Codex/Claude asymmetry preserved at the constant layer), or a generalization over a subset of `RUNTIME_DIRS` (broader addition, helper becomes multi-runtime-aware). `114` does not name either shape. The later implementation slice inherits a helper-contract ambiguity larger than the JSON-shape ambiguity above, and the implementation slice is where that ambiguity is hardest to carry cleanly.
- [d:r:i] **Family-3's home is treated as monotone in the shape choice**. `114:54` says annotation "gives the next family, consumer-chain asymmetry, a clearer immediate home without forcing a separate typed carrier first." 12-r1 at `12-r1:191` kept family-3's home plural across the three shapes: annotation-row home under annotation, anchor-sub-row home under dual-basis, separate-carrier home under typed-carrier. `114` collapses the three-home surface into one — annotation's home — and then uses that as an argument for annotation. The reasoning is circular inside the proposal's own premises: the three shapes were equally capable of housing family-3; annotation is preferred because annotation's home for family-3 is clearer. Any shape's chosen home will be the clearest one under that shape.
- [d:r:i] **The proposal holds no guidance for what the implementation slice should do if `project_uplift.py progress-note` renders the annotation asymmetrically across runtimes**. Direct inspection: [.codex/get-shit-done/workflows/progress.md:158-189] loads the uplift note via `project_uplift.py progress-note --json`; [.claude/get-shit-done/workflows/progress.md] contains zero occurrences of `compatibility`, `uplift`, or `project_uplift`. The asymmetry is load-bearing because it means annotation's operator-facing effect is Codex-only until family-3's consumer-chain proposal lands. `114` does not name this asymmetry as part of why annotation-first is a sensible sequence with family-3, and the silence compresses a real operator-facing deliverable.

## Which Widening Shape Should Move First

- [d:r:i] Annotation-first is directionally the right first move. The ordering in `114:46-48, 114:74-76` — (1) annotation; (2) implementation slice on existing anchor; (3) family-3 consumer-chain proposal — is sound. What should not carry forward is the packaging: the proposal picks the shape before narrowing what annotation means, and the narrowing work belongs inside this lane rather than inside the later implementation slice.
- [d:r:i] The stronger first move is **annotation posture revised to split into two sub-shapes** before the implementation slice opens:
  - **Annotation-as-held-scalar**: one additional string in `compatibility_basis.held_later` at [.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json:96-100] naming `.claude`'s runtime version as explicitly held, with no new top-level field and no helper change beyond reading `.claude/get-shit-done/VERSION` via the existing `RUNTIME_DIRS` enumeration. Minimal surface, minimal operator-facing change, preserves `observed_basis_only` label without widening.
  - **Annotation-as-structural-row**: a new `held_runtime_version_entries` array (or similar) at the same nesting level as `observed_runtime_version_set`, with `.claude` as its first row and posture metadata (`status: held`, `source_path`, `alignment_state: not_observed`). Larger helper footprint (new constant or generalized loader), larger progress-note surface, stronger symmetry with the future family-3 annotation.
  Both sub-shapes satisfy annotation posture. They differ in whether the annotation is a scalar inside the existing held_later array or a structurally parallel row. The 11-r1 reread read annotation the first way at `11-r1:83`; `114` reads it the second way at `114:32-34`. That ambiguity is what the revised proposal should resolve.
- [d:r:i] The sequencing argument for annotation-first survives this split but also gains one qualifier: the parallel opening of family-6 mapping should be preserved per 12-r1 at `12-r1:199`. Annotation-first gates family-3's home; it does not gate family-6's discovery. If the parent thread later decides typed-carrier is actually warranted after family-6 mapping lands, annotation-as-held-scalar is the shape that moves most cleanly under that revision (one string reversion), annotation-as-structural-row moves less cleanly (a new array plus its dependents), dual-basis moves least cleanly (posture label was changed). That sub-shape discipline broadens the future optionality annotation preserves.
- [d:r:i] The stronger **ordering** is therefore:
  1. Narrow `114` in place (or in a successor proposal) to pick between annotation-as-held-scalar and annotation-as-structural-row, with the helper contract named.
  2. Keep the family-6 mapping lane as a parallel-openable lane rather than a sequenced-after lane.
  3. Open the implementation slice on the narrowed annotation shape.
  4. Open the family-3 consumer-chain proposal once the implementation slice lands.
  5. Open family-8 route-translation triage only after family-6 mapping lands.
- [d:r:i] Not annotation alternatives: dual-basis and typed-carrier are not rejected; they are held. Dual-basis would open correctly if a later lane earns a stronger posture label. Typed-carrier would open correctly if family-6 mapping discloses a field that one compatibility carrier cannot carry cleanly. Neither judgment is available now; the shape-selection gate should therefore preserve their future optionality rather than close it.
