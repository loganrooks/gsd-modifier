## Governing Standards

- [g:c+i] Do not treat `passes review`, `no blockers found`, or `good enough to proceed` as satisfying endpoints when the better next step is stronger mapping, stronger doctrine, stronger propagation, or stronger rerun design. Sources: .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:9, .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:87, .planning/AGENTS.md:100, .planning/AGENTS.md:105.
- [g:c+i] Non-intervention is not a neutral default. Any recommendation to leave a surface untouched must show why non-promotion or non-modification is defensible without avoidable regret. Sources: .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:132, .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:140.
- [g:r:i] Intervention is not a neutral default either. Any recommendation to intervene must say what futures it forecloses, what reversal cost it creates, and what evidence or later signal would materially count against the intervention if it turns out to be wrong.
- [g:c+i] Audit comparison should not flatten outputs into consensus or let one aggressive artifact become sovereign by style alone. Compare by spec quality, read-set adequacy, source coverage, independence, self-justification, and claim survivability. Sources: .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:104, .planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md:113.
- [g:r:i] Any single cross-review output, including prestige-model output, is challenge input rather than governing conclusion unless the workspace explicitly accepts the residual risk in writing or compares it against another independent lane.
- [g:c+i] Keep future-flexibility statusing explicit. Distinguish `direct doctrine`, `bounded-open branches`, `preserve-only seams`, `reversal-sensitive boundaries`, and `inquiry debt` rather than flattening them into generic `open` language. Sources: .planning/AGENTS.md:84, .planning/AGENTS.md:94.
- [g:r:i] Practice `critical inheritance`, not obedience and not shallow rupture. The past does not grant all authority by default, but neither is it disposable just because it underreached. Each major artifact family should be judged for what must be preserved, what must be reinterpreted, what must be superseded, and what must be dropped.
- [g:r:i] Do not require strict falsifiability as the blanket criterion for framing claims. This workspace inherits a post-falsificationist posture: framing claims should be contestable, revisable, pressure-tested against rivals and anomalies, and explicit about what would materially weaken them, but not every load-bearing orientation claim must be reducible to a Popper-style falsification test.
- [g:r:i] Do not let ordinary human-timeline assumptions quietly narrow the audit's feasible intervention set. If a recommendation argues against a stronger intervention because it feels too large, it must say what execution model, review capacity, and agentic capability assumptions made it seem too large.
- [d:r:i] Working execution-capacity model for this workspace: one operator orchestrating parallel Codex and Claude lanes over a multi-week budget, capable of multi-module repo-local refactors and bounded follow-through on repo-local harness surfaces, but not assuming broad upstream `gsd` ownership by default. Stronger interventions can therefore be ruled out only against that model, not against an unspecified ordinary-human baseline.
- [g:r:i] Judge proposals partly by whether they keep stronger product, harness, and planning futures open under changing conditions, rather than by whether they minimize immediate disruption.

## Non-Goals

- [g:r:i] This workspace is not trying to prove that the first readiness package was worthless, nor to canonize the current docs-refresh work as final truth.
- [g:c+i] This workspace should not silently widen current Checkpoint 5 ownership. If a later rerun-design proposal wants new subphases or insertion points, it must argue for them explicitly. ## Required Outputs

- mission reconstruction for the readiness package
- outcome / underreach audit
- mapping-adequacy and comparative-mapping judgments
- inheritance-disposition matrix for major artifact families
- suppressed-opportunity / non-intervention ledger
- interventions-considered-and-rejected ledger with named regret analysis
- rerun-design options with explicit tradeoffs
- prerequisites for any second major attempt
- recommended next program, plus explicit branch/switch triggers

## Success Conditions

- [g:r:i] A later strong reviewer should be able to see:
  - what the first readiness attempt was trying to do
  - where it actually succeeded
  - where it underreached or translated doctrine into tame closure
  - how much of that came from weak mapping versus weak judgment
  - what a more ambitious and more future-preserving second attempt should do differently
- [g:r:i] A failed outcome for this workspace would be another elegant but closure-biased summary that says little more than `reasonable effort, tighten a few things, proceed`.
