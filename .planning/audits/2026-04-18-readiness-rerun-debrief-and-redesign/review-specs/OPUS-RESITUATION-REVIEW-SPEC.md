## Purpose

- [g:r:i] This is not a rewrite commission.
- [g:r:i] The job is to review, inherit, resituate, and pressure-test what this workspace has now become after an extensive but incomplete philosophical inquiry.
- [g:r:i] The center of gravity is this audit workspace, but the review target exceeds the audit alone. It must also judge implications for:
  - the readiness rerun
  - the harness and its governing surfaces
  - the repo / project doctrine
  - the broader GSD ecosystem horizon
  - the framing and situatedness of the current operator/orchestrator

## Review Frame

- [g:r:i] Treat the commentary corpus as a situated exploratory artifact family, not as sovereign doctrine.
- [g:r:i] Treat the derivative translation plus active chunk corpus as the primary inheritance layer for this review.
- [g:r:i] Do not clog the review by reading the full source note in parallel unless a bounded drift-check actually requires it.
- [g:r:i] Treat the question as one of `critical inheritance`: what in this work should be preserved, reinterpreted, challenged, deferred, or translated into a next local program.
- [g:r:i] Do not assume that anything in the inquiry is simply `given`. Read, interpret, and judge how it should be inherited.
- [g:r:i] Do not reduce the review to `is the audit setup ready now?`
- [g:r:i] Do not reduce the review to `what files should be rewritten next?`
- [g:r:i] The stronger question is: given the inquiry, commentary, and translation work now on disk, how should this workspace resituate itself before any stronger mutation, rewrite, or rerun-design move is attempted?

## Core Questions

1. What does the inquiry and commentary corpus actually earn as a stronger reading of the situation, and what in it remains interpretive pressure rather than settled inheritance?
2. How should the commentary corpus be inherited at different scales:
   - this audit workspace
   - the readiness rerun
   - the harness / workflow surfaces
   - the repo's project-level doctrine
   - the broader GSD ecosystem horizon
3. Which parts of the corpus should be treated as:
   - preserve
   - reinterpret
   - challenge
   - defer
   - do not operationalize yet
4. What does the corpus reveal about the current operator/orchestrator's situatedness, framing pressures, and possible distortions?
5. Which current framing habits remain most risky here:
   - tame minimal-change bias
   - bridge-audit inheritance bias
   - philosophical sprawl
   - prestige-model dependence
   - doctrine-vocabulary lock-in
   - self-dramatizing overreach
   - treating interpretive vocabulary as if it already settled intervention design
6. How should concrete example surfaces such as `.planning/CLAIM-TYPES.md` be inherited?
   - treat it as a load-bearing inheritance surface that may need re-situation, limitation, supplementation, or no change
   - judge what it is still good for
   - judge where its limits should be made explicit
   - judge whether it needs a sibling layer rather than replacement
7. What actionable implications does the corpus create for:
   - the audit program
   - the harness and workflow surfaces
   - project-level steering
   - ecosystem-facing doctrine or template questions
8. Which implications are strong enough to justify a concrete next-step proposal, and which are still too weak or too incomplete for that?
9. What evidence or comparison lanes are still missing before any serious rewrite or rerun-design mutation would be justified?
10. What should explicitly *not* be rewritten yet?
11. What is the best next local program from here if the aim is not merely to admire the inquiry, but to inherit it responsibly?

## Required Posture

- [g:r:i] Do not rewrite canon, readiness artifacts, harness files, workflow files, or templates.
- [g:r:i] Do not treat the commentary corpus as if it directly authorizes rewrites.
- [g:r:i] Do not treat your own review as sovereign doctrine.
- [g:r:i] Do not use raw Codex logs. If a narrow wording dispute seems material, say what would need verification instead of defaulting to logs.
- [g:r:i] Treat `../planning-notes/SESSION-FRAMING-BRIEF.md` as a situated briefing artifact, not a neutral transcript replacement.
- [g:r:i] Maintain a post-falsificationist posture:
  - identify what materially weakens or re-situates a framing
  - face rival explanations where they matter
  - preserve anomaly pressure
  - do not demand strict Popper-style falsifiability for every framing claim
- [g:r:i] Also resist the opposite failure: do not let rich vocabulary or philosophical density masquerade as earned operational closure.
- [g:r:i] Distinguish clearly between:
  - what the corpus describes
  - what it interprets
  - what it recommends
  - what you recommend should happen next
- [g:r:i] If you think some later doc or harness modification is likely warranted, describe it as a proposal target, not as an instruction to rewrite now.

## Special Emphasis

### Situatedness

- Treat situatedness as multi-level, not merely personal:
  - operator/orchestrator position
  - audit-workspace framing
  - readiness-rerun posture
  - repo governance posture
  - ecosystem ambition and comparison horizon
- Ground any situatedness claim in the read set.
- Separate grounded interpretation from looser speculation.

### Inheritance Rather Than Flat Adoption

- Do not ask only `is this right?`
- Ask:
  - what in this corpus becomes a live inheritance obligation
  - what needs reinterpretation before inheritance
  - what remains too speculative to carry forward
  - what should remain deliberately outside current operationalization

### Proposal Without Rewrite

- If a more concrete next-step proposal is warranted, write it as a proposal for local follow-up.
- Do not draft the rewritten target artifacts themselves.
- The proposal should identify:
  - what kind of artifact or lane should be created next
  - what question it should answer
  - what read set it needs
  - what it must not silently assume

## Failure Modes To Avoid

- flattering the workspace for being organized
- collapsing the whole inquiry into `interesting but non-actionable`
- treating the commentary corpus as settled operational doctrine
- treating the commentary corpus as mere decorative philosophy
- recommending rewrites just because some surfaces are implicated
- recommending no change merely because the next move is hard to name
- using philosophical density to hide that the next action is still under-specified
- using anti-tame rhetoric to force premature large-scale intervention
