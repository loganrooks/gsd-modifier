## Core Framing Additions From The Current Session

- [d:r:i] The purpose of the new audit is not just to improve upstream GSD docs. It is to better position a second readiness attempt aimed at structurally improving the harness and its agential dynamics.
- [d:r:i] The aim is not merely `future awareness` in the vague sense. It is to help the harness preserve multiple excellent future options, remain adaptable under changing conditions, and resist premature foreclosure into local minima.
- [d:r:i] The first readiness package likely underreached in part because its map of the harness was too weak, too local, or too incomplete to justify stronger structural recommendations.
- [d:r:i] The upstream docs work matters because it may help produce a stronger terrain map, but the docs themselves are still not the whole answer.
- [d:r:i] The audit should remain suspicious of tame recommendations that ask for little or no modification. Those recommendations carry a burden of proof.
- [d:r:i] The audit should also remain suspicious of ordinary human-capacity or ordinary human-timeline assumptions. Agentic execution may make much larger interventions tractable than conservative planning language usually admits.
- [d:r:i] Working execution-capacity assumption for this workspace: one operator can orchestrate parallel Codex and Claude lanes over a multi-week budget, can carry bounded multi-module repo-local refactors, and can use selective cross-vendor audits, but should not assume broad upstream `gsd` ownership by default.
- [d:r:i] The relationship to the past should be one of `critical inheritance`: the past is neither sovereign nor disposable. The audit should decide what to preserve, reinterpret, supersede, or drop.
- [d:r:i] The better mapping we want is partly a moving target. The currently realizable `next iteration` of mapping and docs is not identical to the ideal intervention-ready map, but it can move us closer to it.
