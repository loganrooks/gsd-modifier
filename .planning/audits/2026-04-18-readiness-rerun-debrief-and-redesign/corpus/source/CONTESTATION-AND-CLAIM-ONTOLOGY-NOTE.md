# Contestation And Claim Ontology Note

Status: open inquiry / preserve for continuation  
Date: 2026-04-18

## Why This Note Exists

- [d:r:i] This note preserves an unresolved but load-bearing philosophical thread that emerged while designing the readiness debrief audit.
- [d:r:i] It should not be treated as settled doctrine yet.
- [g:r:i] Keep this thread open across compaction and later sessions rather than forcing a premature clean answer.

## Current Problem Cluster

The live questions are no longer only:

- what should be contestable?
- under what burden of proof?

They now also include:

- what is contestation?
- what are its preconditions?
- what are its normative limits?
- what kinds of things are contestable in different ways?
- whether the `empirical hypothesis` / `non-empirical principle` split is even the right governing division
- whether `claim`, `statement`, or `proposition` is the right primitive unit for audit ontology at all

## Current Provisional Positions

### 1. Not Everything Should Be Equally Contestable

- [d:r:i] Contestation should not mean universal immediate reopenability.
- [d:r:i] Different things are contestable in different registers and at different levels.
- [d:r:i] Some surfaces may be metalevel-contestable rather than locally contestable.

### 2. `Empirical / Non-Empirical` Is Too Crude As Master Division

- [d:r:i] The better question may be: what role is this item playing in inquiry?
- [d:r:i] Useful candidate roles currently include:
  - empirical / mechanistic
  - interpretive / explanatory
  - strategic / normative
  - regulative
  - constitutive / grounding
  - stipulative
- [o:r:i] It remains open whether this role-based breakdown is itself sufficient or still too proposition-centered.

### 3. `A = A` And Grounding Principles Complicate The Frame

- [d:r:i] `A = A` is usually not treated as an empirical hypothesis.
- [d:r:i] It often functions more like a constitutive, logical, grammatical, transcendental, or grounding principle depending on framework.
- [o:r:i] Open question: what does `grounds A = A` even mean?
  - proving it?
  - making it true?
  - making it necessary?
  - making identity-judgment possible?

### 4. The Primitive Unit Is In Question

- [o:r:i] The current audit ontology leans heavily on `claims`, `statements`, and `propositions`.
- [o:r:i] It is not yet clear whether that is acceptable abstraction or reductionist flattening.
- [o:r:i] Open concerns:
  - whether discourse is more than additive sets of claims
  - whether orientations, vocabularies, rhetorical structures, silences, exclusions, and framing moves are being reduced too quickly to proposition-like units
  - whether establishing `claim` as primitive already prejudges the audit's metaphysics of language and reasoning

## Continuation: `A = A`, Empiricity, And Modes Of Answerability

### 5. Weakening Identity Does Not Automatically `Empiricalize` It

- [d:r:i] Saying that some frameworks weaken, relativize, or structurally redescribe identity does **not** by itself mean that `A = A` becomes an empirical hypothesis there.
- [d:r:i] More often, what changes is:
  - the role identity plays
  - the scope over which it holds
  - the level at which it operates
  - the conditions under which identity-talk is legitimate
- [d:r:i] The question is often less `is identity empirical now?` than:
  - is identity primitive, derived, local, global, structural, grammatical, or operational in this framework?

### 6. `Empirical` May Be Better Understood As A Mode Of Answerability

- [o:r:i] Treating `empirical` as the name of a fixed kind of statement may be too crude.
- [d:r:i] A more promising line is to treat empiricity as a **mode of answerability / constraint**:
  - what kinds of worldly uptake bear on the item?
  - does observation, measurement, intervention, or recalcitrant phenomena constrain it?
  - or is it governed primarily by inferential, grammatical, constitutive, or transcendental criteria?
- [o:r:i] This suggests replacing a static `what is empirical?` posture with a more dynamic question:
  - how is this item answerable?
  - to what sort of pressure?
  - in what practice?

### 7. Meaning Here Should Not Be Reduced To A `Ti Esti` Question

- [d:r:i] Asking only `what does this mean?` risks treating meaning as a static object that can be pointed to and possessed.
- [d:r:i] A better line for this thread may be:
  - how does meaning operate here?
  - what does the principle do in the practice of judgment?
  - what inferential permissions, exclusions, stabilizations, or distinctions does it enact?
- [o:r:i] This may require a shift from a noun-like ontology of propositions toward a more verbal / operational account of how a principle functions within discourse, reasoning, and world-disclosure.

## Continuation: The Erring In The Form Of The Question

### 8. The Object Of Inquiry Has Shifted

- [d:r:i] The discussion no longer concerns only which kinds of things should be contestable.
- [d:r:i] It now also concerns whether the audit ontology itself is already distorting the field by treating discourse as if it were primarily composed of claims that can be typed and governed.

### 9. The Possible Erring

- [o:r:i] The erring may lie in the form of the question itself rather than only in the answer given to it.
- [d:r:i] Questions such as:
  - `is A = A empirical or not?`
  - `what grounds A = A?`
  - `what type of claim is this?`
  may already presuppose:
  - that the relevant unit is a proposition-like item
  - that understanding means classification
  - that grounding must itself be propositionally expressible in the same register
  - that meaning is an object rather than an operation, movement, or institution of possible sense

### 10. Consequence For Audit Ontology

- [d:r:i] If the audit relies too heavily on a proposition-centered ontology, it may capture explicit assertions while missing:
  - framing moves
  - inherited vocabulary
  - sequencing effects
  - rhetorical force
  - omissions and exclusions
  - what is rendered relevant or irrelevant before explicit claims are even made
- [g:r:i] Future audit design should remain open to units richer than `claim`, including:
  - framing
  - criterion
  - operative distinction
  - exclusion
  - inheritance relation
  - orientation
  - closure move

## Working Sketch: Two-Layer Audit Ontology

### 11. Why A Two-Layer Approach

- [d:r:i] `Claim` remains useful for traceability, citation, and review of explicit assertions.
- [d:r:i] But `claim` is insufficient for tracking how a discourse-space is pre-shaped before explicit assertions are made.
- [d:r:i] A two-layer ontology is therefore a pragmatic compromise:
  - keep a lower operational layer for explicit traceable items
  - add an upper discourse-forming layer for framing, exclusion, orientation, and closure dynamics

### 12. Layer One: Operational Units

- [d:r:i] Candidate units:
  - claim
  - inference
  - assumption
  - decision
  - stipulation
  - open question
- [d:r:i] Main virtues:
  - citation-ready
  - easy to compare across artifacts
  - good for burden-of-proof analysis
  - good for identifying what was asserted, inferred, decided, or left open
- [o:r:i] Main limit:
  - risks flattening discourse into additive proposition-like units

### 13. Layer Two: Discourse-Forming Units

- [d:r:i] Candidate units:
  - framing move
  - operative distinction
  - vocabulary regime
  - exclusion
  - inheritance relation
  - sequencing move
  - closure move
  - orientation-setting gesture
- [d:r:i] These are not merely extra `claims`; they help determine:
  - what counts as salient
  - what counts as evidence
  - what can appear as an option
  - what gets treated as premature, realistic, excessive, irrelevant, or decisive
- [d:r:i] This layer is where underreach, foreclosure, false closure, or inherited narrowing may become visible even when the explicit claims look individually defensible

### 14. Relation Between The Layers

- [d:r:i] Layer two often conditions layer one.
- [d:r:i] A framing move can make certain claims appear natural, modest, excessive, or unnecessary before they are explicitly argued.
- [d:r:i] A closure move can reduce what later appears as a reasonable inference space.
- [d:r:i] An exclusion can make a missing intervention invisible rather than explicitly rejected.
- [g:r:i] The audit should therefore not read only for what artifacts say, but also for how they shape the field in which saying occurs.

### 15. Practical Use In The Readiness Debrief

- [d:r:i] The two-layer model would let the audit ask both:
  - what explicit recommendations, assumptions, and decisions were made?
  - how was the field of relevant intervention options pre-shaped before those recommendations appeared?
- [d:r:i] This is especially relevant where the readiness package may have narrowed intervention space through:
  - localist framing
  - minimal-disruption bias
  - burden asymmetry against ambitious change
  - inherited terminology that made some surfaces appear secondary or off-scope
- [g:r:i] Treat this model as a working tool, not as a final metaphysics of discourse.

## Continuation: Are `Discourse-Forming Units` Still Too Objectifying?

### 16. The New Risk

- [o:r:i] Even the richer layer may still be reductive if it treats framing, exclusion, orientation, and closure as if they were simply more manageable objects to be catalogued.
- [d:r:i] The risk is that what was introduced to escape a proposition-centered ontology simply reproduces objectification at a higher level.

### 17. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] `Unit` should be understood operationally here, not metaphysically.
- [d:r:i] The audit may still need provisional handles for tracing movement, but those handles should not be mistaken for the thing itself.
- [d:r:i] A framing move or closure move may be less like a self-contained object and more like:
  - a relation
  - a modulation of the field
  - a constraint on what can appear
  - a directional force in discourse
- [g:r:i] Future audit language may need to alternate between:
  - tractable tags or handles for analysis
  - relational / field-sensitive prose that does not pretend the phenomenon is exhausted by the tag

## Continuation: Evidence For Field Effects Without Pure Projection

### 18. The Problem

- [o:r:i] If the audit shifts from explicit claims to operations, relations, and field effects, it risks arbitrary projection unless it can say what warrants such readings.

### 19. Provisional Warrant Conditions

- [d:r:i] Field-sensitive readings should not rely on a single sentence taken in isolation.
- [d:r:i] Stronger warrant comes from convergent traces such as:
  - recurrence across artifacts
  - asymmetry in what is named versus what remains unarticulated
  - stable vocabulary that repeatedly organizes salience
  - sequencing effects where early framing constrains later options
  - downstream consequence patterns
  - contrast with plausible omitted alternatives
  - rival readings explicitly considered and comparatively weakened
- [d:r:i] The goal is not strict proof but disciplined, contestable interpretation with visible grounds.

### 20. Practical Consequence

- [g:r:i] Future audit lanes should distinguish:
  - direct explicit claims
  - trace patterns that suggest field effects
  - stronger and weaker interpretations of those patterns
- [g:r:i] The audit should avoid presenting a field reading as self-evident when it depends on comparative interpretation.

## Continuation: What Counts As A Trace?

### 21. The New Risk

- [o:r:i] If `trace` is left too broad, everything can start to count as evidence for everything else and the audit loses discipline.

### 22. Provisional Narrowing

- [d:r:i] A trace should not mean any feature whatsoever.
- [d:r:i] A candidate trace becomes stronger when it has at least some of the following:
  - recurrence rather than singularity
  - relational force rather than isolated presence
  - differential salience (what is marked versus unmarked)
  - consequence-bearing effects on later reasoning or option-space
  - contrastive value against plausible alternatives
  - legibility across more than one artifact, step, or moment
- [d:r:i] In this sense, a trace is less a raw datum than a constrained sign of how a discourse is being organized.

### 23. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid saying `everything is a trace`.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what repeated or structurally consequential marks does the corpus leave?
  - what do they organize?
  - what rival readings remain live?

## Continuation: What Is `Organization` Here?

### 24. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If traces are taken as residues of organization, the audit must still ask what kind of organization is meant.

### 25. Provisional Distinctions

- [d:r:i] `Organization` should not be reduced immediately to conscious authorial intention.
- [d:r:i] Candidate senses include:
  - intentional ordering by an explicit agent
  - emergent ordering across multiple artifacts and iterations
  - institutional or workflow grammar that shapes what appears reasonable
  - inherited vocabulary that carries prior structuring force
  - field-shaping constraints that are not cleanly attributable to one author or moment
- [d:r:i] The same discourse effect may be overdetermined across several of these levels at once.

### 26. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit readings should avoid prematurely collapsing:
  - `the text is organized this way`
  into
  - `the author intended this`
- [g:r:i] When possible, distinguish:
  - explicit intention
  - local rhetorical choice
  - repeated pattern
  - institutional inheritance
  - broader field effect

## Continuation: Text, Field, And The Distribution Of Plausibility

### 27. The New Pressure Point

- [o:r:i] If organization distributes permission and plausibility, the audit must still ask where the text ends and the wider field begins.

### 28. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] The text should not be treated as self-sufficient.
- [d:r:i] But neither should the wider field be invoked so loosely that any reading can be justified by appeal to an indefinite context.
- [d:r:i] A better posture is to treat the text as one local condensation of a wider field, while asking what aspects of that field become legible through:
  - recurrence
  - inherited vocabulary
  - procedural form
  - stable burden asymmetries
  - repeated exclusion or narrowing patterns
- [g:r:i] Future audit work should make the crossing explicit:
  - what is directly textual?
  - what is traceable institutional carry-over?
  - what is broader inferred field effect?

## Continuation: Reflection, Reproduction, And Responsibility

### 29. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If the field is only partially legible through textual condensations, the audit must still ask when a text merely reflects a field and when it actively reproduces one.

### 30. Provisional Distinction

- [d:r:i] `Reflection` suggests symptomatic registration: the text bears marks of a wider field without necessarily stabilizing or extending it.
- [d:r:i] `Reproduction` suggests active carry-forward: the text helps reinstall, normalize, transmit, or operationalize the field's ordering.
- [d:r:i] A text is more plausibly reproductive when it does at least some of the following:
  - codifies a burden structure
  - repeats and stabilizes inherited vocabulary
  - converts a contingent framing into procedure or doctrine
  - transmits a narrowing or permission structure into later artifacts
  - shapes downstream judgment, sequencing, or option-space

### 31. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid treating every symptom as reproduction.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - does the artifact merely register a prior ordering?
  - or does it help carry that ordering forward into later reasoning, canon, workflow, or governance?

## Continuation: Reinforcement, Reproduction, Institution

### 32. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If reproduction comes in degrees, the audit needs a clearer distinction between merely reinforcing an order and helping institute one.

### 33. Provisional Gradient

- [d:r:i] Candidate gradient:
  - reflective: bears marks of an order
  - reinforcing: strengthens or normalizes an order already in circulation
  - reproductive: carries the order forward into later artifacts, reasoning, or procedures
  - instituting: helps found, refound, codify, or authorize the order as a governing practical arrangement
- [d:r:i] `Institution` is stronger than simple repetition.
- [d:r:i] An artifact is more plausibly instituting when it does at least some of the following:
  - codifies the ordering into canon, workflow, or governance
  - becomes an authoritative reference point for later work
  - sets burdens, thresholds, or sequencing rules that later artifacts inherit
  - converts a prior tendency into an explicit normative or procedural standard

### 34. Practical Consequence

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid collapsing all carry-forward effects into a single category.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what degree of carry-forward is happening here?
  - is this artifact symptomatic, reinforcing, reproductive, or instituting?

## Continuation: Authority, Uptake, And The Force Of Institution

### 35. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If an artifact is said to institute an order, the audit must still ask where that instituting force comes from.

### 36. Provisional Sources Of Authority

- [d:r:i] Authority should not be reduced to a single source.
- [d:r:i] Candidate sources include:
  - canonical status
  - workflow position
  - procedural centrality
  - repeated uptake
  - social deference
  - explicit ratification
  - infrastructural embedding
- [d:r:i] The same artifact may derive force from several of these at once.

### 37. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid assuming that canonical text alone explains authority.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what gives this artifact binding or orienting force?
  - how is that force distributed?
  - who or what reproduces it downstream?

## Continuation: Declared Authority And Effective Authority

### 38. The New Question

- [o:r:i] The audit must distinguish what is officially named as authoritative from what later work actually has to answer to in practice.

### 39. Provisional Distinction

- [d:r:i] `Declared authority` names the authority an artifact is explicitly given:
  - canon
  - charter
  - governing rule
  - approved doctrine
- [d:r:i] `Effective authority` names the authority an artifact actually exercises through:
  - workflow bottlenecks
  - templates and defaults
  - review burdens
  - repeated citation or silent inheritance
  - infrastructural embedding
- [d:r:i] These can align, but they can also diverge sharply.

### 40. Practical Consequence

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should ask both:
  - what is officially declared authoritative?
  - what effectively governs downstream reasoning, sequencing, and option-space?
- [g:r:i] A key risk for the readiness debrief is that declared canon may look future-aware while effective workflow authority remains locally narrowing or burden-asymmetric.

## Continuation: Critiquing The Divergence

### 41. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If declared authority and effective authority diverge, the audit must still ask how to criticize that divergence without collapsing into either formalism or crude efficacy.

### 42. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] Neither side should automatically dominate.
- [d:r:i] A declared authority can be normatively stronger yet practically weak.
- [d:r:i] An effective authority can be practically decisive yet normatively under-justified.
- [d:r:i] The divergence itself is therefore often the primary object of critique.

### 43. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid:
  - treating official canon as decisive merely because it is formally ratified
  - treating procedural efficacy as self-legitimating merely because it governs practice
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what normatively ought to govern?
  - what actually governs?
  - how is the divergence reproduced?
  - what interventions would better align declared and effective authority without foreclosing stronger futures?

## Continuation: What Kind Of Intervention Closes The Gap?

### 44. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If the real object of critique is the divergence between declared and effective authority, the audit must still ask what kinds of intervention can actually close that divergence.

### 45. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] No single intervention form should be presumed sufficient.
- [d:r:i] Different divergence-types likely call for different intervention-types, including:
  - canon uplift, when normative doctrine is missing or weak
  - transmission repair, when doctrine exists but does not reach later workflow layers
  - burden redistribution, when procedural asymmetry blocks stronger options
  - workflow redesign, when the process grammar itself narrows the field
  - infrastructural change, when templates, defaults, or tooling silently override doctrine
  - experimental counter-institution, when a rival practical ordering must be tried rather than merely described
- [d:r:i] The first diagnostic question may therefore be:
  - what kind of gap is this?
  rather than:
  - what is the one correct intervention?

### 46. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid assuming that canon uplift alone closes a workflow problem.
- [g:r:i] Future audit work should also avoid redesigning workflow without clarifying the normative order it is meant to carry.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - where does the divergence reside?
  - what reproduces it?
  - what layer would have to change for declared and effective authority to come into a better relation?

## Continuation: Against A Hidden Linear Model Of Intervention

### 47. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If the gap between declared and effective authority is layered, the audit must still ask how to think intervention without presuming that changing one privileged layer will simply cascade through the rest.

### 48. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] A layered divergence should not be presumed to have a single master cause or a single master remedy.
- [d:r:i] Canon, workflow, burden structure, infrastructure, and uptake may interact recursively rather than linearly.
- [d:r:i] This means intervention may need to be thought in terms of:
  - coupled adjustments
  - leverage points
  - feedback loops
  - sequencing effects
  - temporary asymmetries where one layer must move first without being mistaken for the sole decisive layer
- [d:r:i] The relevant question is often not:
  - which one layer causes the rest?
  but:
  - which relations stabilize the current order?
  - where can an intervention interrupt or recompose those relations?

### 49. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid assuming that upstream doctrinal repair will automatically propagate into downstream practice.
- [g:r:i] Future audit work should also avoid assuming that infrastructural tweaks alone will produce the desired normative order.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what must move together?
  - what can move first?
  - what would count as evidence that a local intervention is actually changing the wider order rather than merely relocating the problem?

## Continuation: Intervention Legibility And Order-Change

### 50. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If an order can absorb local reform, the audit must still ask how one recognizes a genuine change in order rather than a merely tolerated variation.

### 51. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] A real order-change should not be identified only by the presence of new language, isolated fixes, or local compliance.
- [d:r:i] Stronger signs of order-change would include:
  - burdens redistributed rather than cosmetically renamed
  - previously marginal options becoming sustainably live
  - later artifacts reasoning differently without needing exceptional justification
  - workflow and infrastructure carrying the revised norm instead of merely permitting it
  - old closure patterns losing their automatic plausibility
  - downstream uptake that is not dependent on one heroic interpreter
- [d:r:i] In contrast, absorbed variation often looks like:
  - new doctrine with unchanged thresholds
  - new templates with old review burdens
  - local exceptions that do not generalize
  - rhetorical future-awareness with unchanged practical narrowing

### 52. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid treating declared reform or one-off successful deviation as proof of order-change.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what downstream behavior now becomes ordinary that previously required strain?
  - what burdens have actually shifted?
  - what old closure moves have lost force?

## Continuation: Why Orders Absorb Reform

### 53. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If an order can tolerate local novelty without changing, the audit must still ask what gives that order absorptive resilience and what kinds of intervention can escape it.

### 54. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] An order may absorb reform through several interacting mechanisms:
  - scale mismatch, where a local fix is too small for the wider ordering it confronts
  - decoupling, where doctrine changes but workflow or infrastructure do not
  - temporal dissipation, where change is not repeated long enough to stabilize
  - burden recapture, where the old proof asymmetry is silently reimposed
  - personnel dependence, where the new order exists only through exceptional interpreters
  - rhetorical domestication, where ambitious change is redescribed in old vocabulary
- [d:r:i] Interventions more likely to escape absorption may involve:
  - coupled multi-layer change
  - infrastructural carry-through
  - redistributed burdens
  - repeated downstream uptake
  - changed defaults for what counts as ordinary or reasonable

### 55. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid equating novelty with transformation.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - by what mechanism would the old order absorb this reform?
  - what would have to change so that the reform becomes self-carrying rather than interpreter-dependent?

## Continuation: What Makes A Change Self-Carrying?

### 56. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If the key distinction is between interpreter-dependent change and self-carrying change, the audit must still ask what gives a change the capacity to reproduce itself.

### 57. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] A change is less interpreter-dependent when it is carried by the reproduction mechanisms of the order rather than by exceptional personal force.
- [d:r:i] Candidate carriers include:
  - repetition across artifacts and decisions
  - codification into canon or procedure
  - infrastructural embedding in templates, defaults, and tooling
  - burden redistribution that makes the new path ordinary rather than exceptional
  - pedagogy or onboarding that transmits the revised orientation
  - exemplars and precedent that give later actors usable models
  - uptake by multiple actors rather than one privileged interpreter
- [d:r:i] Self-carrying change therefore seems less like a single event and more like a reorganization of how the order reproduces itself.

### 58. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid treating codification alone as enough to make a reform self-carrying.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - through what mechanisms would this change be learned, repeated, and defended downstream?
  - what would still collapse if the strongest current interpreter disappeared?

## Continuation: Succession And The Carriers Of Continuity

### 59. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If self-carrying change must survive succession, the audit must still ask what kind of succession is at issue.

### 60. Provisional Distinctions

- [d:r:i] `Succession` is not one thing.
- [d:r:i] Candidate forms include:
  - succession of people
  - succession of sessions or conversational contexts
  - succession of models or agent configurations
  - succession of artifacts and canon revisions
  - succession of workflow instances
  - succession of environments, timelines, or strategic conditions
- [d:r:i] Different succession-types stress different carriers of continuity.

### 61. Candidate Carriers By Succession Type

- [d:r:i] People succession stresses:
  - pedagogy
  - exemplars
  - onboarding
  - distributed uptake
- [d:r:i] Session or context succession stresses:
  - summaries
  - retained notes
  - stable framing artifacts
  - canon clarity
- [d:r:i] Model or agent succession stresses:
  - explicit doctrine
  - burden structures
  - workflow scaffolds
  - legible examples rather than tacit memory
- [d:r:i] Artifact succession stresses:
  - supersession discipline
  - inheritance markers
  - canonical retrieval
- [d:r:i] Workflow succession stresses:
  - templates
  - defaults
  - review criteria
  - infrastructural embedding
- [d:r:i] Environment or condition succession stresses:
  - adaptive doctrine
  - reopenability rules
  - preserved optionality rather than rigid fixation

### 62. Practical Consequence

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid asking whether a change survives `succession` in the abstract.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - succession of what?
  - which carriers would have to preserve the change across that kind of handoff?
  - which carriers preserve continuity, and which preserve adaptability?

## Continuation: Preserving Capacity Rather Than Only Content

### 63. The New Question

- [o:r:i] If the more important continuity is continuity of capacity rather than mere continuity of inherited conclusions, the audit must still ask what kind of thing that capacity is.

### 64. Provisional Candidates

- [d:r:i] The relevant capacity may not be reducible to a single rule or doctrine.
- [d:r:i] Candidate descriptions include:
  - competence
  - discipline
  - disposition
  - practical reason-form
  - ethos
  - cultivated responsiveness
- [d:r:i] Each description highlights something and obscures something:
  - `competence` stresses skill
  - `discipline` stresses trained practice
  - `disposition` stresses tendency or orientation
  - `practical reason-form` stresses patterned judgment
  - `ethos` stresses a lived normative style
  - `cultivated responsiveness` stresses adaptive attunement rather than static rule-following

### 65. Provisional Pressure

### 474. Provisional Response

## Continuation: What Disposition Vocabulary Keeps Interrupts Real?

### 479. The New Question

### 480. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] The key is probably a small set of mutually distinct outcomes.

- [d:r:i] If it is too large, the layer becomes hard to use consistently.

- [d:r:i] That set matters because each disposition changes the state of the interrupt.
- [d:r:i] None of them means:
  - seen, noted, and left semantically unchanged

- [d:r:i] So the purpose of disposition vocabulary is not just categorization.

### 481. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid disposition vocabularies that allow interrupts to be “acknowledged” without entering a new state.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:

  - is the interrupt changing current action, opening inquiry, being deferred under a fresh trigger, or being retired?

## Continuation: How Does Routing Logic Avoid Becoming Stale Doctrine?

### 491. The New Question

### 492. Provisional Response

- [d:r:i] A routing map should probably be treated as a revisable hypothesis about how interruptibility and ownership currently flow.
- [d:r:i] That means it needs periodic rereading against actual behavior, not just preservation.

- [d:r:i] This matters because routing maps are especially vulnerable to fossilization.
- [d:r:i] Once written, they can keep telling the system:
  - who owns what
  - where interrupts belong
  - what depends on what

- [d:r:i] So the question is not only:

- [d:r:i] It is:
  - is it still being verified against lived routing?

  - treat every significant mismatch between mapped and actual routing as either
    - map error
    - execution drift
    - or a signal that the ownership architecture itself has changed

### 493. Practical Guardrail

- [g:r:i] Future audit work should avoid treating dependency maps as once-written settlement of routing truth.
- [g:r:i] Prefer asking:
  - what recent behavior has actually pressure-tested this routing assumption?
