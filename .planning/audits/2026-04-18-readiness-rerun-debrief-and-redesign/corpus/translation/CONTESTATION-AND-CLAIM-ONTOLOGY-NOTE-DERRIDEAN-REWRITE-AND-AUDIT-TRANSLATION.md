## 1. Derridean Rewrite

The question can no longer be posed as though contestation were a procedure later applied to already constituted objects. The issue is not only which claims may be contested, nor only what burden of proof attaches to them. The more difficult issue is how the field of possible contestation is itself instituted: what comes to count as a claim, what enters as evidence, what appears too local or too ambitious, what arrives already burdened as realistic or excessive, what is admitted into review, and what is rendered marginal before any explicit argument begins.

On this reading, contestation is never simply the reopening of self-identical units. It is distributed by prior framings, operative distinctions, inherited vocabularies, and procedural forms. A discourse does not first present neutral items and then submit them to challenge. It organizes in advance the very space in which challenge, defense, reopening, closure, and relevance can appear. The question of contestation therefore exceeds the question of the contestable object. It reaches the conditions of legibility under which something becomes contestable in one register, uncontestable in another, or invisible altogether.

This is why the empirical / non-empirical distinction becomes unstable as a master division. The opposition may remain useful, but it cannot peacefully govern the field. For what matters is less the assignment of an item to a fixed class than the mode of answerability by which it is constrained. One must ask not only what a statement is, but to what pressure it answers, in what practice it functions, by what criteria it is constrained, and through what institution of sense it becomes intelligible at all. Once framed this way, the inquiry shifts from a taxonomy of statements toward a differential analysis of force, constraint, and role.

The same displacement appears in the question of `A = A`. The temptation is to ask whether identity is empirical, non-empirical, primitive, derived, grounded, or ungrounded, as though these were simply available boxes. But such a question may already repeat the metaphysics it seeks to examine. It may presume that identity presents itself as a stable object for classification, that grounding must be formulable in the same register as the grounded item, and that the meaning of identity can be possessed as a determinate content. A more patient reading asks instead how identity functions, what it permits, what it excludes, what sort of judgment it makes possible, and under what conditions the mark of sameness can be reiterated at all.

From there the pressure falls on the primitive unit of the audit. If `claim`, `statement`, or `proposition` is treated as basic, then the discourse is already being cut up according to one metaphysics of readability. Such units may be operationally necessary, but they are not innocent. They privilege what is explicit, assertoric, and citation-ready. They risk missing what works before or beside assertion: framing, silence, inherited vocabulary, rhetorical force, sequencing, exclusion, and the institution of what will later count as evidence or relevance. In that sense, the problem is not merely that some important things are omitted from a claim-centered audit. The stronger problem is that claim-centrism may itself be one of the organizing reductions under examination.

The proposed move to a two-layer ontology is therefore best understood not as a resolution but as a supplement. `Claim` remains indispensable for traceability, review, and burden-of-proof analysis. But the supplement reveals the insufficiency of the supplemented term. A second layer of framing moves, operative distinctions, exclusions, inheritances, and closure dynamics is introduced because the first layer cannot account for the pre-shaping of the field in which explicit claims arise. Yet this supplement does not simply complete the first layer. It also exposes that the first layer was never self-sufficient.

Still, the second layer does not escape danger. If framing, exclusion, orientation, and closure are treated as new stable objects, the audit simply reproduces objectification at a higher level. What was added to loosen the proposition-centered frame becomes another catalog of manageable entities. The better posture is to treat such handles operationally, as provisional tags for relations, modulations, inheritances, and directional pressures that are not exhausted by the names given to them. The tag is a convenience for tracing movement; it must not be mistaken for mastery over the movement.

This is where `trace` becomes decisive. A trace is not just any feature whatsoever. Nor is it a free license for projection. It marks a differential remainder through which a field becomes partially legible: recurrence, asymmetry, consequence-bearing sequencing, stable vocabulary, repeated exclusions, inherited burden structures, and other marks whose force appears across more than one sentence or moment. The trace is not pure presence. It is legible precisely through relation, repetition, displacement, and what remains marked by what is not said. But because this legibility is comparative and interpretive, it demands discipline. A field reading must expose its grounds, its rival readings, and the difference between stronger and weaker interpretations.

The same caution applies to organization. If a discourse is organized, this must not be collapsed into authorial intention. Organization may be intentional, emergent, procedural, inherited, institutional, or overdetermined across several levels at once. A text may condense a wider field without reducing to one conscious plan. It may register a prior ordering, or it may help reproduce and operationalize that ordering. The important distinction is not between text and context as though they were externally separable domains, but between more direct textual marks, more traceable inheritances, and broader inferred field effects. Crossing among these levels must be made explicit rather than smuggled in.

What follows is a consequence for audit reasoning. The audit cannot be satisfied with extracting explicit claims and classifying them by type. It must also read for the distribution of plausibility: what appears natural, local, realistic, premature, excessive, irrelevant, or already settled; what burden asymmetries have been installed; what exclusions make alternatives invisible rather than explicitly rejected; what inherited vocabulary silently carries prior structuring force into later artifacts. Contestation then becomes not merely the right to challenge a sentence, but the disciplined reopening of the field that made that sentence appear in the way it did.

The deepest erring, then, may lie in the form of the question. If the audit asks only what kind of claim something is, it may miss how the field was organized so that some items arrive already typed, already burdened, already scaled down, already deprived of force, or already granted the authority of realism. The task is therefore double: retain enough operational structure to make interpretation contestable and reviewable, while refusing to mistake that structure for a final metaphysics of discourse.

## 2. Practical Audit-Design Translation

### Core Design Shift

- Treat `claim` as an operational handle, not as the final ontology of auditable material.
- Keep explicit-assertion analysis, but add a parallel lane for discourse-forming effects.
- Write audit language so that tags remain provisional handles for relations and field effects, not declarations that the phenomenon has been fully captured.

### Minimal Ontology For The Audit

- Keep a lower layer for explicit operational units:
  - claim
  - inference
  - assumption
  - decision
  - stipulation
  - open question
- Add an upper layer for discourse-forming handles:
  - framing move
  - operative distinction
  - vocabulary regime
  - exclusion
  - inheritance relation
  - sequencing move
  - closure move
  - orientation-setting gesture

### Reading Protocol

- Run two passes on each artifact set:
  - Pass 1: what was explicitly asserted, inferred, decided, stipulated, or left open?
  - Pass 2: how was the field of plausible intervention options pre-shaped before those explicit items appeared?
- In pass 2, read specifically for:
  - what is made salient
  - what is treated as background
  - what appears realistic versus excessive
  - what appears in scope versus off-scope
  - what burden asymmetries are installed
  - what options are made visible, invisible, or premature

### Warrant Discipline For Field Readings

- Do not present a field-effect reading as self-evident.
- Require visible grounds for each upper-layer reading, preferably from convergent traces such as:
  - recurrence across artifacts
  - stable vocabulary
  - asymmetry in what is named versus unarticulated
  - sequencing effects
  - downstream consequence patterns
  - contrast with plausible omitted alternatives
  - rival readings explicitly considered
- Distinguish in the write-up:
  - direct textual evidence
  - traceable institutional or artifact inheritance
  - broader inferred field effect

### Anti-Reduction Guardrails

- Do not collapse:
  - discourse effect into authorial intention
  - text into wider field
  - symptom into reproduction
  - handle into metaphysical unit
- When describing a field effect, say which level is being claimed:
  - explicit intention
  - local rhetorical choice
  - repeated pattern
  - institutional inheritance
  - broader field effect

### Contestation Model

- Define contestation by register rather than treating everything as equally reopenable.
- At minimum, distinguish:
  - local contestation of an explicit claim
  - contestation of an inference chain
  - contestation of a framing move
  - contestation of a burden structure
  - metalevel contestation of the audit ontology itself
- For each register, specify:
  - what counts as a challenge
  - what warrants are admissible
  - what kind of revision follows if the challenge lands

### Reflection Versus Reproduction

- Add an explicit check for whether an artifact:
  - mainly registers a prior ordering
  - or helps carry that ordering forward into later canon, workflow, governance, or review practice
- Mark reproduction only when the artifact does at least some of the following:
  - codifies a burden structure
  - stabilizes inherited vocabulary
  - converts contingent framing into procedure or doctrine
  - transmits narrowing into later artifacts
  - shapes downstream judgment or option-space

### Suggested Output Shape

- For each audit conclusion, separate:
  - explicit findings
  - discourse-forming findings
  - warrant basis
  - rival reading still live
  - confidence level
  - downstream consequence if accepted
- Keep prose for relational and field-sensitive phenomena.
- Use tags for navigation and comparison, but do not rely on tags alone where the phenomenon is irreducibly relational.

### Practical Questions For The Readiness Debrief

- What explicit recommendations, assumptions, and decisions were made?
- What framing moves made those recommendations appear modest, necessary, or sufficient?
- What vocabulary or burden structures were inherited rather than freshly argued?
- Which alternatives were explicitly rejected, and which were never allowed to become salient options?
- Where did sequencing narrow the intervention space before major conclusions were reached?
- Which effects are directly textual, which are traceable carry-over, and which remain broader but plausible field inferences?
- Where does the package merely reflect a prior field, and where does it reproduce that field into later audit or planning practice?

### Immediate Implementation Consequence

- If later audit specs continue to use `claim` language heavily, annotate that usage as provisional and operational.
- Do not lock the readiness debrief architecture to a purely proposition-centered model without first deciding how upper-layer findings will be represented, warranted, contested, and revised.
