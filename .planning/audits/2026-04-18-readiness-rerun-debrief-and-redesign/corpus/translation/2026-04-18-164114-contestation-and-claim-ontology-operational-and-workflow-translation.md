# Operational Translation

1. `Textual pressure:` The note keeps doubting whether `claim`, `statement`, or `proposition` is the right primitive, then proposes a second layer for framing, exclusion, sequencing, vocabulary, and closure. `Interpretive translation:` If that pressure is taken seriously, an audit that tracks only explicit assertions will miss part of what the text treats as load-bearing. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Use a provisional multi-unit audit ontology: one layer for explicit assertions and another for discourse-shaping moves such as framing, exclusion, inheritance, and closure. Keep the second layer explicitly marked as an analytic handle rather than a settled metaphysics. `Scope:` Audits that are evaluating reasoning, not only factual correctness. `Confidence / uncertainty:` Medium-high confidence in the need for more than one unit of analysis; lower confidence in any final list of units.

2. `Textual pressure:` The note argues that not everything is contestable in the same way and repeatedly shifts from `what kind of thing is this?` toward `how is this answerable?` `Interpretive translation:` A workable audit design should distinguish registers of contestation instead of forcing every disagreement into one burden-of-proof model. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Require reviewers to identify what register is being challenged before judging adequacy: empirical, interpretive, strategic, normative, regulative, constitutive, or stipulative. Where the register is mixed or uncertain, record that rather than flattening it. `Scope:` Cases where evidence, framing, and norm-setting are entangled. `Confidence / uncertainty:` Medium confidence in the need for typed contestation; lower confidence in this exact register set, since the note keeps the taxonomy open.

3. `Textual pressure:` The note wants to read traces, field effects, and framing patterns without letting `everything count as evidence for everything else.` It asks for convergent traces, rival readings, and visible grounds. `Interpretive translation:` Pattern-level judgments need a warrant standard different from the one used for direct quotation or explicit claims. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Separate evidence handling into at least three lanes: direct textual support, inferred pattern with cited recurrence or asymmetry, and broader field-effect interpretation. For the latter two, require the analyst to name what repeated marks support the reading and what rival reading remains live. `Scope:` Diagnoses of framing, exclusion, burden asymmetry, and closure patterns. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for layered warrant; medium confidence in where exact threshold lines should be drawn.

4. `Textual pressure:` The note insists that discourse is shaped before explicit claims appear and that what is rendered central, peripheral, realistic, or premature can matter as much as what is asserted. `Interpretive translation:` An audit should not stop at what a document says; it should also inspect how it pre-shapes the option space. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Add explicit checks for framing and exclusion: what is treated as central, what is backgrounded, what alternatives are unnamed, what burdens are asymmetrically assigned, and what vocabulary quietly narrows the field. Treat these checks as interpretive rather than self-evident. `Scope:` Strategic reviews, design critiques, postmortems, and any artifact that narrows future options. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for this surface; medium confidence in how much of it can be standardized without turning it into rote pattern-matching.

5. `Textual pressure:` The note distinguishes enabling closure from premature foreclosure and keeps tying closure to map quality, proportionality, preserved seams, and reopening triggers. `Interpretive translation:` Audit conclusions should not close more than the mapped terrain can bear. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Bind every substantive closure to an explicit scope, a stated remainder, and a reopening condition. If the evidence is thin or the map partial, prefer narrower commitments and explicit preservation of live seams over broad finalizing language. `Scope:` Recommendations, findings, canon-affecting summaries, and decision artifacts with downstream force. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for closure discipline; medium confidence in any one formal schema for expressing it.

6. `Textual pressure:` The note keeps returning to declared authority versus effective authority, and to artifacts whose formal function understates the force they actually carry. `Interpretive translation:` Audit design cannot rely on genre labels alone when deciding how much scrutiny an artifact needs. `Concrete audit-design implication:` Assess audit surfaces partly by effective downstream force: what sets burdens, what gets silently inherited, what workflow bottlenecks govern practice even if they do not call themselves governance. Increase answerability requirements as effective force rises. `Scope:` Templates, checklists, prompts, summaries, and other operational artifacts. `Confidence / uncertainty:` Medium-high confidence in the distinction; medium confidence in how to measure effective force consistently.

# Workflow Translation

1. `Textual pressure:` The note separates explicit claims from framing, then warns that even richer units can become objectifying if treated as settled objects. `Interpretive translation:` A workflow should separate passes of review without pretending those passes are exhaustive or final. `Concrete workflow implication:` Use staged audit procedures: first extract explicit assertions and decisions, then inspect framing, exclusions, and burden patterns, then decide what can responsibly be closed. Do not merge those moves into one undifferentiated review pass. `Scope:` Audits, postmortems, architecture reviews, and requirements reviews. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the value of staged review; medium confidence in the exact pass structure.

2. `Textual pressure:` The note treats mixed disputes as a source of distortion when they are forced into one register. `Interpretive translation:` Review protocols should ask not only whether something is wrong, but what kind of wrongness is being claimed. `Concrete workflow implication:` In review templates and issue discussion, require objections to name their register and burden: factual challenge, interpretive challenge, strategic objection, normative objection, framing concern, or authority/force mismatch. That does not settle the dispute, but it prevents different disputes from talking past each other. `Scope:` Pull-request reviews, design reviews, requirements review, and issue triage. `Confidence / uncertainty:` Medium-high confidence in the protocol need; medium confidence in how granular the objection types should be.

3. `Textual pressure:` The note values reconstructible judgment-paths and rejects both polished smoothing and raw transcript dumping. `Interpretive translation:` Handoff artifacts should preserve how a decision was judged, not only what was decided. `Concrete workflow implication:` Decision logs, postmortems, and handoff notes should retain a compact record of live alternatives, tradeoffs, uncertainties that changed the path, and conditions that would reopen the matter. Avoid artifacts that leave only the winner and erase the path. `Scope:` Decision records, prompt handoffs, architecture notes, and summary documents that must survive succession. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for this kind of remainder; low-medium confidence in the minimum viable amount for every workflow.

4. `Textual pressure:` The note treats hidden governance as a practical problem: local prompts, defaults, and templates can carry real authority while presenting themselves as convenience. `Interpretive translation:` Workflow surfaces that quietly govern should become more answerable without necessarily becoming full charters. `Concrete workflow implication:` When a prompt, checklist, template, or default starts setting real burdens downstream, add lightweight provenance, rationale, and override or redraw conditions. Review those surfaces periodically for function-force divergence rather than letting them sediment silently. `Scope:` Prompt design, operational checklists, review templates, and canon-adjacent workflow scaffolds. `Confidence / uncertainty:` Medium-high confidence in the need for answerable indirect governance; medium confidence in how much metadata each surface can carry before becoming unusable.

5. `Textual pressure:` The note asks how anomalies can stay open long enough to become intelligible without forcing every disturbance to persist forever. `Interpretive translation:` Workflow needs a middle state between immediate closure and indefinite preservation. `Concrete workflow implication:` Add a lightweight anomaly lane for review and triage: preserve the disturbance with a minimal carrier, record why it is not yet settled, state what comparative checks or future signs would mature it into a live rival, and state what would let it fade responsibly. `Scope:` Issue triage, requirements disputes, architecture questions, postmortem follow-ups, and audit spillover items. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for a protected middle state; medium confidence in how formal that lane should be.

6. `Textual pressure:` The note repeatedly distinguishes closure that enables work from foreclosure that exceeds what the map can bear. `Interpretive translation:` Governance and canon-update workflows should not reward forceful summary when the terrain is still partial. `Concrete workflow implication:` For canon updates, policy changes, or summary artifacts with downstream authority, require an explicit statement of present commitment, preserved seam, and redraw trigger. If those cannot be named, the workflow should default to narrower local action rather than pretending to have achieved settled doctrine. `Scope:` Governance updates, standards documents, decision summaries, and policy-like prompts. `Confidence / uncertainty:` High confidence in the need for this closure rule; medium confidence in the exact threshold for when an artifact has enough force to require it.
