# Commentary

**Commentary Bounds**
Primary span: `## Continuation: What Counts As Better Discrimination?` through `### 245. Practical Guardrail`.
Backward / lateral touchpoints: this slice reopens chunk 16's concern with `discriminatory yield` and asks what that yield consists in, how it can remain disciplined without a sovereign metric, and what kind of formation could cultivate the judgment able to use it.
Forward reliance: none; this reading stops before `From Salience To Weight-Bearing Judgment`.

**Revisits / Transformations**
Revisited motif: chunk 16's `adequacy by discriminatory yield`.
Current relation: criterion-setting and formative deepening.
What is preserved: supports still have to be judged by what discrimination they enable rather than by their decorative presence.
What is transformed: the problem is widened from support adequacy to the standards of better discrimination, the anti-relativist discipline of configurational judgment, and the formation of answerable prudence in agents and harnesses.
Grounding basis: both the current chunk's wording and reopened earlier chunk text.

This chunk takes the measure introduced in the last slice and refuses to leave it empty. If supports are to be tested by discriminatory yield, then the note has to ask what counts as `better`. The answer is immediate and important: there is no sovereign metric. Better discrimination may involve fewer category mistakes, richer option-preservation, more proportionate closure, stronger burden placement, higher practical traction, better anomaly sensitivity. These dimensions do not collapse into one another, and the note does not pretend they do. One support may preserve futures better while thickening process too much. Another may sharpen decision speed while flattening a distinction that ought to have stayed live. The word `better` therefore has to stay tied to task, horizon, foreclosure risk, and cost. This is not indecision. It is a refusal to replace judgment with one optimizing number.

That refusal then courts a familiar danger: if `better` is always configurational, does the whole enterprise dissolve into preference? The note's answer is one of the clearest in the file. Configurational judgment is not arbitrary if the dimensions being weighed are not optional. Burden placement, map reach, reversibility, horizon, foreclosure cost, practical traction, transmissibility: these still constrain the scene even when their weight changes from case to case. What varies is not whether they matter at all, but which misdiscrimination is most dangerous here and what form of closure or preservation fits that local conjunction. Relativism is blocked by legibility, tradeoff ownership, rival comparison, anomaly pressure, future cost, reconstructible discrimination-path. This is a strong formulation because it refuses both rigid universalism and the managerial escape hatch in which one merely says `context matters` and hides the weights.

Once answerable prudence is named as the alternative to rule and relativism, the problem becomes formative. The note is explicit that prudence will not arise from rule exposure alone. It needs cases, counter-cases, burden disciplines, rival rereadings, maps with under-mapped surfaces visible, prompts that surface distinctions at the right moment, retrospective replay of closure and foreclosure. Formation here is not doctrinal download. It is habituation under criticizable scaffolds. That point matters because the note is moving toward harness design without collapsing into a simplistic prompt-optimism. Prompts matter, exemplars matter, review forms matter, burden structures matter, repeated replay matters. But they matter as an ensemble that redistributes what becomes ordinary in judgment.

The last movement complicates that formation by separating attention from prudence. Attention is foundational because it governs salience. It decides what even appears as a seam, burden, anomaly, or future cost. Yet the note insists that attention is not enough. A system can teach agents to notice and still fail to teach them how to weigh, when to commit, when to resist closure, when to accept closure with residual openness intact, when to bear the cost of acting against convenience. Prudence therefore includes trained attention, proportional judgment, timing sensitivity, commitment under uncertainty, and some willingness to incur burden when the better path is costlier. Courage enters here in a stripped practical sense. Not heroism as spectacle. The capacity to carry a harder judgment before the order makes it easy.

This matters because it shows where formation can underperform while looking sophisticated. A harness can teach the right vocabulary of care, futures, burdens, and seams while leaving agents unable to rank pressures or hold a costly line. The note also refuses the opposite fantasy. Courage without trained attention is noise. So the relation is articulated: attention lets a scene appear, prudence weighs what the scene now demands, courage may be required when the prudent path still cuts against inherited ease. The chunk ends here because the next problem follows directly: by what process does something merely seen become weight-bearing enough to enter judgment at all?

This slice therefore gives the whole note a sharper evaluative and educational center. Better discrimination is plural and task-bound. Configurational judgment is disciplined by recurring dimensions and by answerability. Prudence has to be formed, not assumed. Attention is necessary, but the scene does not judge itself merely because it has become visible. If I extend the point, I would say that the chunk is trying to build a pedagogy of non-arbitrary judgment: one that trains salience, forces weighting into the open, and prepares agents to carry costs without mistaking cost-bearing alone for wisdom. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely and leaves the next question open: how visibility becomes weight.

# Operational Translation

## Plural Criteria Of Better Discrimination

Textual pressure: `better discrimination` cannot be reduced to one metric.
Interpretive translation: evaluation has to move across several dimensions that remain in tension with one another.
Audit-design implication: assess supports and judgments by category-mistake reduction, option-preservation, closure proportion, burden placement, practical traction, and anomaly sensitivity rather than by a single proxy such as decisiveness.
Scope: support evaluation and judgment assessment.
Confidence: high.

## Configurational Weighting Under Constraint

Textual pressure: the value of a discrimination depends on task, horizon, foreclosure risk, and cost.
Interpretive translation: weighting varies by case, but recurring dimensions still constrain the range of plausible judgment.
Audit-design implication: require judgments to state which dimensions are being weighed, why those dimensions matter here, and what rival weighting remains plausible.
Scope: warrant standards and evaluation grammar.
Confidence: high.

## Anti-Relativist Answerability

Textual pressure: configurational language can become a cover for preference.
Interpretive translation: relativism is blocked when the discrimination path remains legible, criticizable, comparative, and revisable.
Audit-design implication: require explicit tradeoff ownership, rival comparison, reconstructible reasoning paths, and named weakening conditions so that `context` cannot function as a black box.
Scope: judgment review and anti-arbitrariness safeguards.
Confidence: high.

## Formation Through Ensembles

Textual pressure: prudence does not arise from rule exposure alone.
Interpretive translation: good judgment is formed by structured cases, counter-cases, burden disciplines, prompts, maps, rereadings, and replay.
Audit-design implication: treat prudence formation as an ensemble problem rather than a doctrine-delivery problem.
Scope: harness design, onboarding, review pedagogy.
Confidence: high.

## Attention Is Necessary, Not Sufficient

Textual pressure: attention governs salience, but salience alone does not decide commitment or closure.
Interpretive translation: prudence exceeds trained noticing.
Audit-design implication: separate systems that teach agents what to notice from systems that teach how to weigh, time, preserve, and commit on what has been noticed.
Scope: formation analysis and support design.
Confidence: high.

## Cost-Bearing Prudence

Textual pressure: the better path may still be harder to carry than the convenient one.
Interpretive translation: prudent judgment sometimes requires a willingness to incur burden before the order makes the better line easy.
Audit-design implication: evaluate whether a harness gives agents support when the stronger judgment is locally costlier instead of assuming right noticing will automatically become right action.
Scope: burden economics and decision support.
Confidence: medium-high.

# Workflow Translation

## Multi-Dimensional Support Review

Inferential step: because better discrimination is plural, workflow should evaluate supports across several dimensions instead of one score.
Workflow consequence: review forms should ask what specific misdiscrimination a support reduces, what it newly preserves, what burden placement it changes, what traction it yields, and what new costs it introduces.
Scope: support review, audit calibration, rerun evaluation.
Confidence: high.

## Weighting Disclosure

Inferential step: because configurational judgment can conceal preference, workflow should force weighting into the open.
Workflow consequence: recommendation and review artifacts should state what dimensions were weighted, why that weighting fit this case, what rival weighting remained plausible, and what evidence would weaken the choice.
Scope: decision logs, review protocols, architecture judgment.
Confidence: high.

## Prudence Formation Loops

Inferential step: because prudence is formed through guided encounter rather than slogans, workflow should include structured replay and rival rereading.
Workflow consequence: training and review loops should use cases, counter-examples, burden checks, rereads, and closure retrospectives to teach what agents are repeatedly supposed to notice and preserve.
Scope: onboarding, reviewer calibration, harness improvement.
Confidence: high.

## Attention-To-Judgment Supports

Inferential step: because attention alone underforms prudence, workflow should support the move from noticing to weighing and commitment.
Workflow consequence: prompts and review forms should ask not only what is salient, but what that salience changes in burden, timing, closure, and action.
Scope: prompt design, review scaffolds, decision support.
Confidence: high.
