# Commentary

This second chunk tries to save two things that do not sit comfortably together. It wants the tractability of units, because an audit needs to cite, compare, trace, and contest. It also wants to stop those units from silently becoming the ontology of discourse. The proposed two-layer model is the compromise: keep `claim` and its neighboring operational units because they are good at traceability, then add a second layer for framing, exclusion, inheritance, sequencing, closure, and orientation. What matters is that the note does not present the second layer as a decorative supplement to the first. It presents it as the place where the field of possible claims is already being shaped.

The lower layer is defined by virtues that belong to audit administration in the best sense of the term. Claims, inferences, assumptions, decisions, stipulations, and open questions are citation-ready. They can be compared across artifacts. They support burden-of-proof analysis. They let the audit say what was asserted or left open. The note does not withdraw any of that. It treats those virtues seriously. But it also names their price. The lower layer risks flattening discourse into additive proposition-like units. That phrase matters because the worry is not merely incompleteness. The worry is a kind of reduction by convenience: once the audit gets good at handling traceable items, it may start treating whatever is less traceable as secondary, atmospheric, or outside scope.

The upper layer is introduced to block that reduction. Its candidate units are striking because they are not just alternative content buckets. A framing move, operative distinction, vocabulary regime, exclusion, inheritance relation, sequencing move, closure move, or orientation-setting gesture does not merely add one more item to a list of items. It shapes what will later count as salient, evidential, realistic, premature, excessive, or decisive. The note is especially sharp here. Individually defensible claims can still belong to a narrowed field. A discourse can look reasonable point by point while its reasonableness has already been pre-shaped by what it excluded, inherited, or made to seem obvious.

The relation between the layers therefore matters more than the layers taken separately. Layer two conditions layer one. A framing move can make a later claim look modest rather than ambitious. A closure move can shrink the inference space before anyone argues explicitly for that reduction. An exclusion can render an intervention invisible rather than rejected. The note does not say that every claim is reducible to field effects. It says that explicit assertions arrive in a space whose boundaries may already have been drawn. That distinction matters, because otherwise the second layer would become a total explanation and lose discipline.

Then the note turns its suspicion against its own repair. Even discourse-forming units may still objectify what they are trying to preserve. That is the strongest point in this chunk. The escape from proposition-centered flattening can reproduce the same problem one level higher if framing, exclusion, and orientation are treated as stable objects waiting to be catalogued. The note therefore weakens the term `unit` from within. A unit is to be understood operationally, not metaphysically. A framing move may be less a bounded thing than a relation, a modulation, a directional force, a constraint on what can appear. This is not a rejection of handles. The note still wants handles. But it insists that handles remain answerable to what they fail to capture.

That insistence leads directly to the problem of warrant. Once the audit begins making field-sensitive readings, it risks projection. The note's answer is careful. A field effect should not be inferred from one sentence in isolation. Stronger readings come from convergent traces: recurrence, asymmetry, stable vocabulary, sequencing effects, downstream consequence patterns, contrast with plausible omitted alternatives, and rival readings that have been considered and comparatively weakened. The standard sought here is neither deductive proof nor free theoretical impression. The phrase the note uses is better: disciplined, contestable interpretation with visible grounds. That phrase could easily harden into a slogan, but in this chunk it still has real pressure behind it, because the note spends lines specifying what those grounds would have to look like.

The final consequence is procedural. Audit lanes should distinguish direct explicit claims, trace patterns that suggest field effects, and stronger or weaker interpretations of those patterns. That separation matters because it keeps the inferential step visible. A field reading is not self-evident simply because it feels plausible after immersion in the corpus. It has to show how it moved from text to pattern to interpretation. This commentary is under the same demand. If I say the chunk is trying to preserve relation against objectification, that is an interpretive condensation, not a sentence the note itself gives me. The note's discipline lies in forcing such condensations to remain contestable. It wants richer reading without letting richness excuse vagueness.

# Operational Translation

1. Textual pressure: `claim` remains useful, but it is insufficient for how discourse-space is pre-shaped. Interpretive translation: the audit needs one layer for explicit traceable items and another for discourse-forming conditions. Audit-design implication: adopt a two-layer schema that records operational units separately from framing, exclusion, inheritance, sequencing, and closure effects. Scope: audit ontology and unit of analysis. Confidence: high.

2. Textual pressure: layer two conditions what later appears natural, modest, excessive, or even visible. Interpretive translation: discourse-forming features are not extra annotations; they shape the evidential and option space for explicit claims. Audit-design implication: require relation markers that link a framing or closure reading to the explicit items it conditions. Scope: evidence handling and claim-to-context linkage. Confidence: medium-high.

3. Textual pressure: richer units can still objectify if treated as catalogued things. Interpretive translation: tags are operational handles, not exhaustive descriptions of the phenomenon. Audit-design implication: pair discrete tags with short field-sensitive prose so the audit does not pretend the tag fully captures the relation it names. Scope: schema design and annotation style. Confidence: medium-high.

4. Textual pressure: field-sensitive reading risks arbitrary projection. Interpretive translation: such readings need warrant conditions stronger than isolated quotation. Audit-design implication: require convergent grounds such as recurrence, asymmetry, stable vocabulary, sequencing effects, downstream consequences, omitted alternatives, and rival-reading comparison before elevating a field interpretation. Scope: warrant standards and review thresholds. Confidence: high.

5. Textual pressure: the note distinguishes explicit claims, trace patterns, and stronger or weaker interpretations of those patterns. Interpretive translation: the audit should separate observation from inference from evaluation. Audit-design implication: report field readings in three lanes: direct textual evidence, cross-text trace pattern, and interpretive judgment with stated strength. Scope: reporting format and contestation protocol. Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

1. Inferential step: because the note distinguishes operational and discourse-forming layers, audit workflow should not collapse extraction and interpretation into one pass. Workflow consequence: use a first pass for explicit assertions and a second pass for framing, exclusion, sequencing, inheritance, and closure effects. Scope: audit procedure and review sequencing. Confidence: high.

2. Inferential step: because the second layer can itself objectify, workflow must keep tags subordinate to prose explanation. Workflow consequence: annotation tools or templates should require a short explanation of how the tagged phenomenon operates in context, not just a label. Scope: prompt design, review templates, handoff artifacts. Confidence: medium-high.

3. Inferential step: because field readings need convergent grounds, reviewers need a standard way to show warrant. Workflow consequence: require reviewers to list the traces supporting a field interpretation and at least one rival reading they considered. Scope: review protocol, postmortems, architecture critique. Confidence: high.

4. Inferential step: because the note refuses to present field readings as self-evident, workflow should preserve inferential gradation. Workflow consequence: distinguish direct citation, pattern claim, and interpretive conclusion in audit outputs so later readers can contest the jump rather than only accept or reject the whole bundle. Scope: debriefs, issue triage, canon-update proposals. Confidence: high.
