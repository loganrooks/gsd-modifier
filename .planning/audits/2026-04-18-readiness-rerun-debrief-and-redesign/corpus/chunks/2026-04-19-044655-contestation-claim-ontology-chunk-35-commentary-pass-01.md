# Commentary

**Commentary Bounds**
Primary span: `## Continuation: What Infrastructure Carries Future Interruptibility?` through `### 478. Practical Guardrail`.
Backward / lateral touchpoints: this slice extends chunk 34's actionable inquiry debt by asking what infrastructure actually carries future interruptibility, how that infrastructure stays selective enough to be trusted, and what counts as evidence that it has become ceremonial noise.
Forward reliance: none; this reading stops before `What Disposition Vocabulary Keeps Interrupts Real?`

**Revisits / Transformations**
Revisited motif: chunk 34's `actionable inquiry debt`.
Current relation: routing infrastructure and trust discipline.
What is preserved: inquiry debt matters only if later decision surfaces actually notice it.
What is transformed: the problem becomes infrastructural and attentional. Storage alone is insufficient; the system must route interrupts to the right surfaces without training later planners to ignore them.
Grounding basis: current chunk's wording alone.

This chunk takes the idea of actionable inquiry debt and turns it into infrastructure. A note by itself is not enough. Interruptibility needs a small ensemble: a debt register, links to affected decision surfaces, milestone or phase review points, and planning or rerun checklists that explicitly ask whether relevant triggers have fired. The note's most important line here is that the real problem is not storage but routing. A debt entry only matters when it reappears at the surface where it can still alter action.

That immediately creates a second risk. Interruptibility can become noisy. The note is careful to say that the problem is not just over-recording but over-surfacing. Many entries can sit safely in a register if only the right ones reappear at the right time. Selectivity therefore depends on tight triggers, mapping from entries to decision surfaces, thresholding by stakes and reversibility, and bounded surfacing windows. A good interrupt system does not ask every future planner to reread the whole register. It asks only which entries are relevant here and whether any trigger has actually fired.

The final section then asks how one would know that the interrupt system has already gone ceremonial. The answer is strong because it looks for practical non-consequence rather than mere presence. The same interrupt appears again and again without changing anything. Reviewers acknowledge it formulaically and move on. High and low stakes entries get surfaced alike. Triggers are marked `seen` without any state change. Entries reach surfaces that cannot act on them. Later work shows no memory that the interrupt appeared at all. The system can then claim that it surfaced the concern while in practice staging interruption rather than interrupting.

This chunk therefore gives the note an attention economy. Future interruptibility is valuable, but it must be budgeted. It needs central memory and distributed surfacing. It must be selective enough to preserve trust. And it must be judged by changed action, inquiry, or explicit deferral, not by the mere appearance of reminders. If I extend the point, I would say that the slice is treating interrupt infrastructure as a fragile channel of future seriousness: one that fails not only when it forgets, but also when it remembers too indiscriminately to remain believable. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely.

# Operational Translation

## Central Memory, Distributed Surfacing

Textual pressure: debt entries do nothing if they remain inert in storage.
Interpretive translation: interruptibility requires centralized recording and targeted resurfacing at the right future decision surfaces.
Audit-design implication: build inquiry debt around a central register linked to phase gates, planning surfaces, rerun review points, and other locations where the debt could distort real action if missed.
Scope: future-aware infrastructure and decision routing.
Confidence: high.

## Interrupt Budgeting

Textual pressure: over-surfacing destroys trust in the interrupt layer.
Interpretive translation: interruptibility must be selective rather than ambient.
Audit-design implication: tighten triggers, map entries to specific decision surfaces, and bound surfacing windows by stakes, dependence, and reversibility.
Scope: alert design and future-surface governance.
Confidence: high.

## Ceremonial-Drift Diagnostics

Textual pressure: surfacing events can look active while changing nothing.
Interpretive translation: interrupt systems decay when reminders persist without practical consequence.
Audit-design implication: treat repeated acknowledgment without action, indiscriminate surfacing, and lack of downstream memory as evidence that the interrupt channel is becoming ceremonial.
Scope: infrastructure validation and attention-discipline review.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Interrupt Routing Architecture

Inferential step: because storage is not enough, workflow should route debt entries to the surfaces that can act on them.
Workflow consequence: debt registers should connect to planning gates, rerun checklists, review prompts, and architecture reconsideration surfaces rather than sitting as standalone notes.
Scope: planning infrastructure and future-aware process design.
Confidence: high.

## Selective Surfacing Policy

Inferential step: because interruptibility becomes noise when every debt resurfaces everywhere, workflow should surface only entries with fired triggers and relevant decision impact.
Workflow consequence: review gates should ask which entries are relevant here, which triggers have actually fired, and which unresolved questions could distort this specific surface now.
Scope: planning, review, and rerun workflow.
Confidence: high.

## Interrupt Trust Check

Inferential step: because ceremonial reminders train readers to ignore the whole channel, workflow should periodically test whether interrupts still alter behavior.
Workflow consequence: infrastructure audits should check whether surfaced entries changed decisions, opened inquiry, produced explicit deferral, or reached action-capable surfaces instead of being merely acknowledged.
Scope: infra review and governance maintenance.
Confidence: high.
