**Revisits / Transformations**

What is preserved: labels of role or site should not quietly become seriousness ladders.
What is transformed: the note now generalizes that concern to the whole ontology and to the movement from topological labels like `architectural` or `local` to scalar claims about force and consequence.
Grounding basis: current chunk's wording alone.

This chunk generalizes a warning that first appeared in the materiality classes. If a small class set can become a hidden ladder, then perhaps the broader ontology itself is at risk of the same drift. The note's answer is that the ontology is mixed. Many distinctions here are topological: locus, function, temporality, carrier role, relation between layers. They tell us where something acts and what kind of difference it makes. But some dimensions are genuinely scalar: downstream force, burden intensity, foreclosure cost, continuity strength. The danger is that scalar seriousness quietly colonizes topological language. `Architectural` begins to mean more important in every sense; `local` begins to mean lesser; `provisional` begins to mean weaker. The note is exactly right to resist that slide.

That is why the next section insists on explicit translation. Topological labels cannot be allowed to carry scalar force by insinuation. If someone says a feature is architectural, central, local, or provisional, the framework should force them to add what scalar consequence follows and on what basis. This is one of the strongest disciplines in the note because it does not ban translation. It simply makes it answerable. Sometimes a local feature really is high-force. Sometimes an architectural feature is low-force in practice. Those possibilities remain visible only if role and consequence are separated before they are rejoined.

The warrant section then calibrates that translation. Not every scalar claim needs the same support. A modest claim about nontrivial downstream force can rest on reasoned pattern and limited evidence. A strong claim about high rerun consequence requires more: direct evidence, patterned consequences across cases, comparative history, explicit reasoning about why the role-site implies force here. The note's graded warrant logic is important because it prevents role labels from doing too much work under the cover of plausibility.

The last section then asks what to do when the desired force claim outruns its support. The answer is not silence by default, but it is certainly not rhetorical overstatement. Weaken the claim, mark it as projected or hypothesized, open a research lane where warranted, preserve it as guarded future possibility, or make action's provisional basis visible if action cannot wait. The key shift is epistemic mode. The question is not only whether we may say something. It is in what claim-form we may say it honestly.

This chunk therefore turns the ontology itself into a site of warrant discipline. Topological and scalar distinctions both matter, but they must not stand in for one another. Translation between them must be explicit, proportionately warranted, and weakened when evidence is weak. If I extend the point, I would say that the slice is trying to stop the audit from smuggling force through role-language: it keeps the ontology mixed and the warrants graded so that `architectural`, `central`, `local`, or `provisional` can no longer masquerade as self-evident consequence claims. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely.

# Operational Translation

## Mixed Ontology Discipline

Textual pressure: many distinctions here are about role and relation, while others are about force and magnitude.
Interpretive translation: the ontology is topological in some dimensions and scalar in others.
Audit-design implication: distinguish role/site labels from force/consequence labels explicitly rather than letting one substitute for the other.
Scope: ontology design and category use.
Confidence: high.

## Forced Topology-To-Force Pairing

Textual pressure: role-language easily smuggles seriousness.
Interpretive translation: every scalar inference from a topological label needs an explicit translation step.
Audit-design implication: require a topology claim, a scalar consequence claim, and a basis for the translation whenever role labels are used to support force claims.
Scope: claim formulation and warrant display.
Confidence: high.

## Graded Warrant For Force Claims

Textual pressure: stronger scalar claims need stronger support.
Interpretive translation: modest force claims can rest on reasoned plausibility, while stronger claims need patterned or direct consequence evidence.
Audit-design implication: scale warrant requirements to claim strength instead of demanding maximal proof for every inference or letting strong claims ride on intuition.
Scope: warrant standards and force assessment.
Confidence: high.

## Honest Claim-Mode Under Weak Warrant

Textual pressure: desired force claims can outrun the evidence available.
Interpretive translation: weak warrant changes the epistemic mode of the claim.
Audit-design implication: downgrade, hypothesis-mark, project, preserve as guarded future possibility, or pair with a research lane rather than keeping strong rhetoric with weak support.
Scope: claim statusing and rhetorical discipline.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Role/Force Separation Rule

Inferential step: because topological labels can become seriousness proxies, workflow should separate role naming from force claims.
Workflow consequence: reviews and templates should ask what role is being named, what force is being inferred, and what evidence licenses that inference here.
Scope: review prompts, audit writing, template design.
Confidence: high.

## Warrant-Calibrated Force Claims

Inferential step: because scalar claims vary in strength, workflow should calibrate evidence demands rather than using one flat burden.
Workflow consequence: minor force inferences may proceed on reasoned pattern, but stronger consequence claims should trigger explicit evidence gathering or comparative support checks.
Scope: architecture review, debrief analysis, claim governance.
Confidence: high.

## Claim-Strength Downgrade Path

Inferential step: because warrant may lag behind desired rhetoric, workflow should provide honest weaker modes of assertion.
Workflow consequence: when evidence is insufficient, the system should convert strong force claims into projections, guarded hypotheses, or future-preserving notes, and open inquiry only where stakes justify it.
Scope: report writing, review discipline, research handoff.
Confidence: high.
