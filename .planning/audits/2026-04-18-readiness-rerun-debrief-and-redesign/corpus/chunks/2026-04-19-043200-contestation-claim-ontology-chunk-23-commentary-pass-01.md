This chunk asks a necessary follow-up to the last one: if a less-favored option can be judgeable before it is finished, what sort of unfinishedness still counts? The note's answer is one of the most useful distinctions in the current batch. An unknown is fertile when its incompleteness is oriented. The option can say what remains open, why it matters, what difference the answer would make, and what next inquiry or experiment would clarify it. Incompleteness then has bounded uncertainty, directional intent, consequence-awareness, and a next move. Empty underdevelopment lacks precisely those features. Its unknowns are not shaped; they do not guide inquiry; they do not tell us what a later clarification would change. This is a strong distinction because it keeps review from collapsing into either premature dismissal or sentimental indulgence.

The next movement applies that distinction to method. If fertile incompleteness should be developed rather than dismissed, then the path of development matters. The note does not want a question-path that simply translates the new option back into the dominant profile's categories. A good question-path clarifies stakes, burdens, closure effects, and future-preserving differences while letting the option grow under its own distinction. It asks what map, comparison, or experiment would most increase discriminability next. It clarifies unknowns by consequence, not by sheer descriptive completion. This is where the slice is strongest: development is not equivalent to assimilation. A partial option can become more reviewable without first becoming legible only in the incumbent grammar.

That is why the last section turns to the `next most discriminating move`. The note refuses the assumption that the biggest inquiry is the best one. A move is more discriminating when it increases judgeability more than descriptive mass. It clarifies a treatment-relevant distinction, changes what can now be weighed, reduces an unknown that blocks burden or closure judgment, helps decide between paths, and preserves distinctiveness while making the option more assessable. The best next move is therefore not maximal research. It is the inquiry that most sharply changes what judgment can responsibly do.

This is a subtle but important shift because it turns research sequencing into a prudential question. One can drown a partial option under data without increasing its judgeability at all. One can also ask the wrong clarifying question and force the option prematurely into the old schema's terms. The slice therefore tries to hold two demands together: incompleteness must be productive rather than vague, and development must sharpen discriminability without recapture. The result is a much more exact account of what it means to keep a weak option alive responsibly.

This chunk therefore gives the note a compact method for handling incomplete alternatives. Not all unfinishedness is equal. Fertile incompleteness has oriented unknowns and consequence-bearing next steps. Good question-paths deepen an option without assimilating it. The next move should be judged by the gain in judgeability it yields, not by the volume of additional description it accumulates. If I extend the point, I would say that the slice is defining a politics of careful development: keep the option unfinished where it must remain unfinished, but make every next step answerable to whether it teaches judgment something new. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely.

# Operational Translation

## Fertile Incompleteness

Textual pressure: incompleteness should not be treated as either automatically acceptable or automatically disqualifying.
Interpretive translation: an incomplete option is fertile when its unknowns are bounded, directional, consequential, and tied to a next move.
Audit-design implication: distinguish oriented incompleteness from vague underdevelopment by whether the option can state what remains open, why it matters, and what inquiry would clarify it.
Scope: option review and provisional path assessment.
Confidence: high.

## Anti-Recapture Question-Paths

Textual pressure: development can easily become assimilation into the dominant schema.
Interpretive translation: a good question-path clarifies stakes and burdens without forcing early translation into incumbent categories.
Audit-design implication: shape development questions around burden shifts, closure effects, future preservation, and discriminating comparisons rather than descriptive completion alone.
Scope: inquiry design and comparative development.
Confidence: high.

## Discriminating Next Moves

Textual pressure: more inquiry is not automatically more useful.
Interpretive translation: the best next move is the one that increases judgeability more than it increases descriptive mass.
Audit-design implication: prioritize inquiries that reduce treatment-blocking unknowns, clarify burden or closure assessment, and help decide between rival paths.
Scope: research sequencing and option development.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Incompleteness Triage

Inferential step: because some unfinished options are fertile and some are empty, workflow should triage incompleteness before either dismissing or protecting it.
Workflow consequence: review notes should ask whether the unknowns are bounded, what difference clarification would make, and what next move is actually available.
Scope: option review, branch maintenance, research triage.
Confidence: high.

## Development Without Assimilation

Inferential step: because question-paths can recapture weaker options, workflow should guide development by consequence rather than incumbent completion standards.
Workflow consequence: prompts for developing alternatives should ask what burden, closure, or future-preservation difference the option would make and what comparison or experiment would sharpen that difference next.
Scope: prompt design, review scaffolds, alternative-path development.
Confidence: high.

## Judgeability-Per-Complexity Heuristic

Inferential step: because the best next move is not necessarily the largest one, workflow should rank inquiry by discriminating yield.
Workflow consequence: planning for follow-up work should ask what unknown most blocks responsible judgment now and what inquiry would add the most judgeability for the least unnecessary complexity.
Scope: follow-up design, research planning, rerun preparation.
Confidence: high.
