**Revisits / Transformations**
Revisited motifs: chunk 15's `least-resistance redesign` and chunk 11's `transmissible prudence`.
Current relation: conversion, delimitation, and pressure-testing.
What is preserved: a new order still has to become reproducible without depending indefinitely on one strong interpreter, and judgment still needs transmissible supports.
What is transformed: the problem shifts from victory and durability in general to the finer question of what within heroic judgment can be externalized, what must remain indirect, and how one can test those supports before the next live case arrives.
Grounding basis: both the current chunk's wording and reopened earlier chunk text.

This chunk begins by refusing a cheap verdict on heroism. Heroic effort is neither simply admirable nor simply a failure state. It can be a bridge. It can keep a difference alive while the order is still too weak to carry it, protect anomalies before relays form, or force visibility where inertia would otherwise win. But the note is just as clear that prolonged dependence on heroism is diagnostic. If the same anomaly keeps needing reassertion, the same burden misalignment keeps returning, no carrier forms, and succession collapses the moment the strongest interpreter leaves, then heroism has stopped bridging and started substituting for recomposition. That distinction is one of the strongest in the sequence. It blocks both the romance of exceptional intervention and the fantasy that one can abolish it by decree.

The next section asks what conversion would actually mean. The answer is not to flatten the whole heroic act into procedure. The answer is to separate the repeatable portion from the irreducibly situational remainder. The note defines the repeatable portion very carefully. It is not whatever is easiest to write down. It is what can be externalized without destroying the judgment's practical truth. Recurring burden patterns, reusable distinctions, prompts that surface the right pressure, exemplar structures a successor can replay: these can often travel. The remainder has a different status. Timing-sensitive proportionality, singular conjunctions of history and horizon, configuration-reading that cannot be parameterized in advance: these are not mystical leftovers. They are the parts that would become false if translated too directly into rule.

That makes the practical task two-sided. One can fail by externalizing too little and leaving everything dependent on exceptional force. One can also fail by externalizing the wrong thing: turning a situational discernment into a rigid rule, preserving a conclusion while losing the conditions that made it apt, mistaking the indexical for the universally portable. The note's proposal is therefore neither maximal codification nor cultivated mystery. Externalize the repeatable portion. Carry the remainder indirectly, through exemplars, contrastive cases, warning signs, reopenability conditions, and other supports that approach judgment without pretending to exhaust it.

The adequacy of those indirect supports is then judged pragmatically. An exemplar, warning, or reopenability note is not good because it exists. It is good when it changes what a successor can actually discriminate. The note becomes very concrete here. Adequate support helps a successor notice pressure at the right moment, avoid predictable misreadings, compare the present case with a structured precedent or contrast case, see what counts as overreach or underreach, and know when escalation or reopening is required. Exemplars are stronger when they preserve live options, burdens, tradeoffs, and map-limits rather than only the final answer. Warnings are stronger when they connect signs to consequences, thresholds, and next moves. Reopenability is stronger when it comes with triggers, not abstract permission.

That pragmatic standard leads to the last movement: testing discriminatory yield in advance. Waiting for future history is too passive. The note therefore permits rehearsal. Prior cases can be rerun. Counterfactual rereadings can ask what a support would have changed. Rival interpreters can test whether the support actually guides better reading. Contrast cases can expose whether a support travels too broadly or too narrowly. Failure-mode probes can show what misreadings still survive. This is an important move because it refuses to treat support design as successful merely because it sounds wise. The measure is whether controlled rereading already produces better discrimination.

This chunk therefore turns transmissibility into an engineering problem without reducing judgment to engineering. Heroism may be necessary for a time, but it has to convert where it can. Conversion requires distinguishing what can travel directly from what must travel indirectly. Indirect supports are judged by discriminatory yield rather than decorative presence. And that yield can be tested before the next crisis if the order is willing to rehearse itself against its own past. If I extend the point, I would say that the note is trying to build a doctrine of prudent externalization: convert enough to survive succession, preserve enough indirection to keep judgment true, and test the result against controlled rereading rather than trust aspiration. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely and leaves open the next question of what `better discrimination` finally means.

# Operational Translation

## Heroism-Conversion Thresholds

Textual pressure: heroic effort can be necessary as a bridge, yet prolonged reliance on it indicates weak order.
Interpretive translation: heroism becomes problematic when it substitutes for recomposition rather than carrying the order until new carriers form.
Audit-design implication: track warning signs such as repeated heroic reassertion, recurring burden misalignment, absent new carriers, and failed succession tests, and use them to decide when conversion into transmissible supports should begin.
Scope: order-strength diagnosis, succession review, remediation timing.
Confidence: high.

## Repeatable Portion Extraction

Textual pressure: not every element of good judgment should be codified, but some of it can travel.
Interpretive translation: the repeatable portion is what can be externalized without falsifying the judgment's practical truth.
Audit-design implication: identify recurring burden patterns, reusable distinctions, prompts, triggers, and exemplar structures that can be carried forward across cases.
Scope: support design, transmission architecture, judgment capture.
Confidence: high.

## Situational Remainder Preservation

Textual pressure: some judgment depends on timing, proportionality, and configuration-reading that cannot be directly parameterized.
Interpretive translation: the situational remainder is not noise or mystique; it is the part that rule-conversion would misstate.
Audit-design implication: preserve the remainder indirectly through exemplars, contrastive cases, warning signs, reopenability conditions, and escalation paths rather than forcing it into rigid rule form.
Scope: non-formalizable judgment support and escalation design.
Confidence: high.

## Adequacy By Discriminatory Yield

Textual pressure: an indirect support is not adequate merely because it exists.
Interpretive translation: adequacy is measured by whether the support changes what a successor can notice, avoid, compare, and escalate.
Audit-design implication: judge exemplars, warnings, and reopenability notes by the discrimination they enable, the misreadings they prevent, and the next moves they prepare.
Scope: support evaluation, warning design, exemplar quality.
Confidence: high.

## Stronger Indirect Support Forms

Textual pressure: indirect supports vary in strength depending on what they preserve and how concretely they direct action.
Interpretive translation: exemplars, warnings, and reopenability conditions get stronger when they retain live options, burdens, tradeoffs, map-limits, thresholds, consequences, and concrete triggers.
Audit-design implication: require supports to preserve the live structure of judgment rather than only conclusions, and tie warnings or reopenability to concrete thresholds and actions.
Scope: exemplar construction, warning systems, review criteria.
Confidence: high.

## Advance Yield Testing

Textual pressure: future history alone is too slow a test for whether a support will help.
Interpretive translation: discriminatory yield can be pressure-tested through controlled rereading before the next live case arrives.
Audit-design implication: rehearse supports against prior cases, counterfactual reruns, rival rereadings, contrast cases, and explicit failure-mode probes to see whether they already improve discrimination.
Scope: validation method, support verification, rehearsal design.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Heroism Compensation Review

Inferential step: because heroism can bridge or substitute, workflow should inspect what exceptional effort is compensating for before celebrating or condemning it.
Workflow consequence: retrospectives and redesign reviews should ask what recurring gap the heroic act is covering, what carriers are still missing, and whether the same burden keeps returning because conversion has been deferred.
Scope: postmortems, governance review, succession planning.
Confidence: high.

## Conversion Pass

Inferential step: because some portion of judgment can travel without distortion, workflow should deliberately externalize the repeatable portion rather than waiting for informal osmosis.
Workflow consequence: after a successful intervention, review should extract reusable distinctions, burden assignments, prompts, triggers, exemplar structures, and review questions that can survive beyond the original interpreter.
Scope: handoff artifacts, support generation, canon-adjacent tooling.
Confidence: high.

## Indirect-Support Design Review

Inferential step: because the situational remainder still needs support, workflow should review whether exemplars, warnings, and reopenability notes are strong enough to guide successors.
Workflow consequence: support review should ask what pressure the support helps notice, what misreading it blocks, what precedent or contrast case it offers, and what escalation or reopening move it prepares.
Scope: documentation review, prompt design, training artifacts.
Confidence: high.

## Rereading Harness

Inferential step: because adequacy should be tested before the next crisis, workflow should build controlled rereading into support validation.
Workflow consequence: candidate supports should be tested against prior cases, counterfactual reruns, contrast cases, and failure-mode probes to see whether they actually change rival readers' discrimination.
Scope: validation workflows, reruns, review exercises.
Confidence: high.

## Rival-Reader Pressure Test

Inferential step: because a support can sound plausible without yielding better judgment, workflow should expose it to other interpreters before treating it as adequate.
Workflow consequence: review protocols should include rival rereadings that check whether another reader can use the support to reach a more proportionate distinction, better burden placement, or earlier escalation than before.
Scope: peer review, audit calibration, support verification.
Confidence: high.
