# Commentary

This chunk is more constructive than the first one, but it keeps interrupting its own construction. The heading already says `Working Sketch`, and the final instruction says to treat the model as a working tool rather than a final metaphysics of discourse. That restraint matters. The note is not announcing that it has found the correct ontology. It is trying to assemble something the audit can use without forgetting why the earlier ontology became suspect.

The immediate proposal is clear enough: retain a lower layer for explicit, traceable items and add an upper layer for discourse-forming conditions. But the reason for the split matters more than the split itself. `Claim` survives because it is still useful for citation, comparison, review, and burden-of-proof analysis. The note does not repudiate operational tractability. It treats tractability as necessary. The difficulty is that the same tractability can become a quiet reduction. Once discourse is handled through claims, inferences, assumptions, decisions, stipulations, and open questions, whatever pre-shaped their appearance can start to look secondary or non-auditable. The two-layer sketch is an attempt to keep the audit usable without letting usability decide the whole ontology.

The second layer is therefore not just a richer filing cabinet. The candidate units listed there do not behave like extra claims. A framing move, operative distinction, vocabulary regime, exclusion, inheritance relation, sequencing move, closure move, or orientation-setting gesture helps determine what will later count as salient, evidential, realistic, excessive, irrelevant, or decisive. That sequence is the crucial point. The note is saying that reasonableness is not only something explicit claims possess once stated. It is also distributed in advance by the field in which they arrive. That is why it can say that explicit claims may look individually defensible while underreach, foreclosure, false closure, or inherited narrowing remain active.

The relation between the layers carries the argument. Layer two often conditions layer one. A framing move can make a later claim appear natural or modest before anyone argues for that appearance. A closure move can narrow what later seems like a reasonable inference space. An exclusion can make an absent intervention disappear from view rather than appear and be rejected. The note's phrase is exact: the audit should read not only for what artifacts say, but for how they shape the field in which saying occurs. That formulation is stronger than an ordinary call for context. It makes the conditioning relation part of the object of audit.

Then the note subjects even this repair to suspicion. The richer layer may still objectify. That is the sharpest point in the chunk. A model introduced to escape proposition-centered flattening can reproduce the same flattening at a higher level if framing, exclusion, orientation, and closure are treated as tidy objects waiting to be catalogued. The note responds by weakening the word `unit`. A unit is to be taken operationally, not metaphysically. The examples that follow matter because they shift the grammar again: not object, but relation; not item, but modulation of a field; not thing, but directional force or constraint on appearance. The note does not throw away handles. It says the audit still needs them. It also says the handles must not pretend to exhaust what they help track.

That is why the later instruction about language is so important. Future audit writing may need to alternate between tractable tags and relational, field-sensitive prose. The note is conceding that neither mode is sufficient by itself. Tags without prose turn living relations into inventory. Prose without handles drifts toward impressionism. This is one of the places where the text comes closest to a method, but even here it stops short of doctrine. It offers an alternation, not a formula.

The warrant section keeps that alternation disciplined. If the audit moves from explicit claims to field effects, it risks arbitrary projection. The answer is not to retreat to explicit claims only, and not to license atmospheric reading. The note lists the conditions that make a field-sensitive reading stronger: recurrence across artifacts, asymmetry between what is named and what remains unarticulated, stable vocabulary, sequencing effects, downstream consequence patterns, contrast with plausible omitted alternatives, and rival readings that have been comparatively weakened. These are not ornamental examples. They tell the audit what kind of evidentiary convergence it should look for before speaking as if a field effect is there.

The practical consequence at the end is modest and exact. Separate direct explicit claims, trace patterns that suggest field effects, and stronger or weaker interpretations of those patterns. That separation keeps the inferential step exposed. It also keeps contestation possible, because a reader can accept the trace pattern while rejecting the stronger interpretation, or accept an interpretation as possible while denying that the grounds are yet strong enough. My own summary risks simplifying the point if I say that the note wants a layered hermeneutics. The text is narrower and more demanding than that. It wants an audit that can register conditioning relations without pretending either that they are self-evident objects or that they are beyond disciplined argument. The sketch remains working, which is another way of saying the problem has been given a more usable form without being closed.

# Operational Translation

1. Textual pressure: the two-layer model is introduced as a pragmatic compromise and explicitly marked as a working tool rather than final metaphysics. Interpretive translation: the schema should support audit work without claiming exhaustive ontological authority. Audit-design implication: define the ontology as provisional and revisable, with explicit room for later adjustment if the layers start obscuring what they were meant to reveal. Scope: ontology charter and revision rules. Confidence: high.

2. Textual pressure: operational units remain valuable for traceability, citation, comparison, and burden-of-proof analysis. Interpretive translation: explicit-assertion tracking is still indispensable. Audit-design implication: keep a stable operational layer for claims, inferences, assumptions, decisions, stipulations, and open questions, rather than dissolving everything into contextual prose. Scope: item extraction and review structure. Confidence: high.

3. Textual pressure: discourse-forming units determine what counts as salient, evidential, realistic, excessive, irrelevant, or decisive. Interpretive translation: framing and exclusion are not peripheral annotations but conditions on what can later appear as reasonable judgment. Audit-design implication: record discourse-forming readings separately and link them to the option-space, evidence standards, or burden distributions they shape. Scope: framing analysis, warrant analysis, contestation registers. Confidence: high.

4. Textual pressure: the richer layer can still objectify if its units are treated as tidy catalogued things. Interpretive translation: tags should function as provisional handles, not exhaustive captures. Audit-design implication: require each discourse-forming tag to be accompanied by short relational prose explaining how it operates in context and what it conditions. Scope: annotation format and reviewer guidance. Confidence: medium-high.

5. Textual pressure: field-sensitive readings require convergent traces and should not be presented as self-evident. Interpretive translation: the audit must distinguish observation, pattern, and interpretation, and grade the strength of the last step. Audit-design implication: report in three lanes: direct explicit claim, trace pattern, interpretive conclusion with stated strength and visible grounds. Scope: evidence handling, reporting, contestation protocol. Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

1. Inferential step: because the note keeps operational units while adding discourse-forming ones, workflow should separate extraction from field-reading without collapsing either. Workflow consequence: use a first pass for explicit traceable items and a second pass for framing, exclusion, inheritance, sequencing, orientation, and closure. Scope: audit procedure, review sequence, postmortem analysis. Confidence: high.

2. Inferential step: because the richer layer can itself re-objectify what it studies, workflow needs a check against overconfident tagging. Workflow consequence: require reviewers to state whether a discourse-forming designation is functioning as a minimal handle, a stronger relational claim, or a comparative interpretation. Scope: review templates, prompt design, handoff artifacts. Confidence: medium-high.

3. Inferential step: because warrant depends on convergent traces rather than isolated quotation, workflow must make grounds auditable. Workflow consequence: field readings should cite the recurrence, asymmetry, stable vocabulary, sequencing effect, omitted alternative, or rival-reading comparison that supports them, rather than gesturing vaguely at corpus feel. Scope: audit outputs, architecture review, decision-log critique. Confidence: high.

4. Inferential step: because the final consequence separates direct claims, trace patterns, and interpretations, workflow should preserve partial agreement and partial dispute. Workflow consequence: structure debriefs and reviews so participants can contest one inferential layer at a time instead of accepting or rejecting the whole reading as a block. Scope: debrief protocol, issue triage, canon-update review. Confidence: high.
