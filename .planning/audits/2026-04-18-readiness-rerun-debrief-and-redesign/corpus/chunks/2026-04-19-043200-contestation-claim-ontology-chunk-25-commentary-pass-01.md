This chunk continues the move from general diagnosis to concrete comparative method. Once recommendation-to-terrain comparison is selected as the first slice, a further decision appears: should one begin with a single decisive case or a broader pattern? The note gives a careful answer. Depth and breadth do different work. A single case can expose the mechanics of underreach in concentrated form; a pattern can show whether the mechanism recurs. The strongest sequence is therefore sequential rather than exclusive: begin with one especially revealing case, then test whether its mechanism generalizes. This is a strong methodological move because it avoids both shallow pattern talk and overgeneralization from one dramatic example.

That makes case selection decisive. A revealing case is not just a loud one. The slice names a more exact profile: rich terrain had already been surfaced, recommendation remained notably thin relative to that terrain, burden asymmetry was visible, later corrective pressure appeared, and the recommendation carried real downstream force. In other words, the best case is one where the package seemed to have enough to do better, still narrowed, and where the narrowing mattered. This is a much better criterion than picking the most rhetorically striking episode.

The note then disciplines the internal order of comparison inside such a case. Start with the mapped seams and terrain richness already visible. Then move to recommendation language and what it closed or narrowed. Then inspect burden placement. Then look at omitted side workflows or adjacent surfaces. Only after that should later corrective intervention enter. This ordering matters because it protects the analysis from hindsight inflation. The earlier pass must first be judged by what it had already made visible before later repair is used as evidence against it.

That protection becomes explicit in the next pair of sections. Later intervention is not automatically corrective evidence. It is stronger evidence when it reopens a seam already visible earlier, repairs an omission rather than importing a wholly external agenda, compensates for an earlier burden or closure effect, and reveals practical costs caused by the earlier narrowing. It is weaker evidence when it depends mainly on later-developed distinctions or carriers unavailable at the time. This is where the note reconnects to its earlier anti-teleological discipline: a later better path does not by itself prove earlier underreach.

The prior-availability section then names a gradient that is especially useful. Explicit earlier visibility is the strongest basis for underreach claims. Weak traceability can still support critique where the pressure-bearing relation was present but underdeveloped. Mere immanent possibility is the weakest basis. It may support philosophical reconstruction of what could have been live, but it is weaker support for saying the earlier pass underreached by its own lights. This gradient matters because it calibrates the strength of the indictment to the strength of continuity. It protects the debrief from both excessive accusation and retrospective innocence.

This chunk therefore gives the note a rigorous comparison protocol for underreach diagnosis. Begin with a revealing case, but do not stop there. Compare in an order that starts from earlier visibility and only then recruits later correction. Grade the continuity between earlier scene and later repair rather than treating all later improvement as equal evidence. If I extend the point, I would say that the slice is building a disciplined hindsight: one that can use later correction to diagnose earlier narrowing without letting later better sense rewrite the evidentiary strength of what had really been available before. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely.

# Operational Translation

## Case-Then-Pattern Sequence

Textual pressure: one case gives diagnostic depth while several cases reveal recurrence.
Interpretive translation: the best method is sequential rather than either/or.
Audit-design implication: begin with one especially revealing case to expose mechanism, then test whether that mechanism generalizes across additional recommendation turns.
Scope: comparison design and debrief sequencing.
Confidence: high.

## Revealing Case Criteria

Textual pressure: not every dramatic case is diagnostically rich.
Interpretive translation: a revealing case combines rich prior terrain, thin recommendation, visible burden asymmetry, later corrective pressure, and downstream force.
Audit-design implication: select anchor cases where the earlier package had enough visible terrain to do better and where the narrowing had consequences.
Scope: case selection and comparative prioritization.
Confidence: high.

## Internal Comparison Order

Textual pressure: evidence inside a case can blur if comparison order is loose.
Interpretive translation: earlier visibility must be established before later correction is used.
Audit-design implication: compare in the order of terrain richness, recommendation language, burden placement, omitted adjacent surfaces, and only then later corrective intervention.
Scope: case-reading method and evidence ordering.
Confidence: high.

## Corrective-Evidence Test

Textual pressure: later intervention is not automatically evidence that earlier work underreached.
Interpretive translation: later intervention counts more strongly when it reopens earlier-visible seams, repairs earlier omissions, compensates for prior closure effects, or reveals costs caused by earlier narrowing.
Audit-design implication: distinguish later correction tied to earlier availability from later reinterpretation that depends mainly on new horizons or new carriers.
Scope: evidentiary warrant and hindsight discipline.
Confidence: high.

## Prior-Availability Gradient

Textual pressure: underreach claims vary in strength depending on earlier availability.
Interpretive translation: explicit visibility, weak traceability, and immanent possibility support different levels of critique.
Audit-design implication: calibrate claims of earlier underreach to the strongest level of prior availability actually present rather than using one accusatory register.
Scope: critique strength and continuity assessment.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Anchor-Case Selection

Inferential step: because one revealing case can expose mechanism before broader patterning, workflow should deliberately select an anchor case first.
Workflow consequence: comparative debriefs should choose the case with the richest prior terrain, clearest narrowing, real downstream force, and strongest later corrective signal.
Scope: debrief setup, comparative audits, rerun analysis.
Confidence: high.

## Ordered Case Review

Inferential step: because hindsight can inflate critique, workflow should fix the order of comparison inside the anchor case.
Workflow consequence: reviewers should establish what the earlier pass had already surfaced before bringing in later interventions, so correction is used as evidence only after prior visibility is clear.
Scope: review protocol, case analysis, evidence discipline.
Confidence: high.

## Availability-Calibrated Critique

Inferential step: because later correction can support stronger or weaker claims, workflow should grade criticism by earlier availability.
Workflow consequence: debrief notes should state whether the case shows explicit miss-level continuity, weaker traceable continuity, or only retrospective possibility before setting the tone of critique.
Scope: debrief writing, audit tone, remedy calibration.
Confidence: high.

## Mechanism-Generalization Check

Inferential step: because one case can mislead if isolated, workflow should test whether its mechanism recurs.
Workflow consequence: after the anchor case, compare several additional turns to see whether the same terrain-to-recommendation narrowing, burden asymmetry, or omission pattern appears elsewhere.
Scope: comparative follow-up, failure diagnosis, package-level analysis.
Confidence: high.
