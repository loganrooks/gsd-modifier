# Commentary

**Commentary Bounds**
Primary span: `## Continuation: Minimum Sufficient Legibility` through `### 193. Practical Guardrail`.
Backward / lateral touchpoints: this slice reopens chunk 13's account of answerable indirect carriers and also returns to chunk 11's concern with rival maps and chunk 9's concern with live possibility, now under the names of profile revision, anomaly, and rival relay.
Forward reliance: none; this reading stops before `Comparing Anomalies Without Premature Recapture`.

**Revisits / Transformations**
Revisited motifs: chunk 13's `answerable indirect carriers`, chunk 11's `genuine rivalry`, and chunk 9's `immanent possibility`.
Current relation: specification, incubation, and partial transformation.
What is preserved: local carriers still need answerable legibility; rivalry still depends on a real alternative line rather than decorative variation; a live possibility still needs more than retrospective desire.
What is transformed: the problem shifts to thresholds, heuristics, and shelters. The rival no longer first appears as a fully formed map. It appears as anomaly, counter-mapping, or a fragile relay that needs enough protection to remain legible before it can count as a rival ordering.
Grounding basis: both the current chunk's wording and reopened earlier chunk text.

This chunk begins by narrowing the previous question. If indirect governance should remain local while becoming answerable, then the issue is no longer whether legibility matters. The issue is how much legibility a given carrier has to bear. The note refuses any single measure. Genre and artifact size are discarded early. The threshold is configurational: force, risk, horizon, burden effects, reversibility, inheritance likelihood. That list matters because it treats a local surface as something that may order far more than its appearance suggests. A small prompt can deserve richer legibility than a large essay if it carries heavier downstream force, longer horizon effects, or stronger inheritance pressure. The question of `minimum sufficient legibility` is therefore already a question of distributed power.

The next step is equally careful. Since the same dimensions recur, the note permits profiles. This is a practical concession to recognizability. Some carriers do resemble one another: high-inheritance defaults, narrow-function bottlenecks with broad force, doctrinal carriers with weak uptake. Without such profiles, every case risks becoming too singular to compare. But the note limits the status of profiles very tightly. They are heuristic, recurrent, revisable, operational. They are not kinds that explain the case in advance. The warning against flattening is important because the very thing that makes profiles useful also makes them dangerous. A good profile speeds recognition. A stale one turns situated relays into a familiar class and lets the order stop looking.

That is why the chunk next asks what revises a profile. The answer is not mere accumulation. A profile changes under pressure: cases that no longer fit without strain, rival maps that redraw what counts as force or burden, failed interventions, ecological shifts, new risks, hidden carriers that alter the practical picture. A profile needs revision when it survives only by stretching to explain away anomaly, when its cuts blur differences it once clarified, when it no longer orients action responsibly. This is one of the strongest claims in the slice because it ties revision to practical failure, not just conceptual tidiness.

Then the note makes the harder move and asks why revision so often does not happen. The answer is political and reproductive. Habit, sunk authority, institutional convenience, prestige, cognitive economy, infrastructural embedding, defensive intelligibility: these are not simply mistakes in judgment. They are supports that let a profile keep reproducing itself. A stale profile survives because it remains the easiest way for the order to recognize itself. That formulation matters. It turns non-revision into a problem of reenactment. Templates, prompts, categories, and prestige do not merely reflect an order; they keep its old discriminations in circulation.

From there the question of revision has to be reframed. Anomaly is rarely enough. Crisis is not enough either if everything new is translated back into inherited terms. Revision becomes possible only when anomaly resists domestication and acquires support: rival carriers, counter-maps, protected reinterpretive space, practical failures that can no longer be absorbed, some shift in what the order can bear to recognize about itself. The note's phrase about `contested recomposition of intelligibility` is apt. Revision is not automatic learning. It is the building of another way for the order to read itself.

That is why the chunk turns from revision conditions to shelter. The space that keeps anomaly open is not merely a meeting room or a role. It can be institutional, temporal, methodological, textual, practical. Delay can be shelter. A tracked anomaly register can be shelter. Counter-artifacts and rival canons can be shelter. Protected experimentation can be shelter. Distributed discussion can be shelter. What all these forms do is keep novelty from being forced to justify itself entirely in the old order's terms before it has carriers of its own.

The final movement gives this shelter a stricter test. Minimum shelter is not indulgence. The note is not defending permanent preservation of every irritation. A live anomaly needs enough support to remain named, persist across succession, gather comparison, test consequences, and begin to articulate another line of questioning. Noise fails differently. It never acquires recurrence, relational consequence, comparative traction, or transmissible articulation. This is a disciplined distinction. The anomaly does not have to present a full doctrine before it deserves protection, yet it must show some capacity to become more than local disturbance. Shelter is therefore diagnostic as well as protective. It gives the anomaly time to become legible, and in doing so it reveals whether there is really a rival relay there to carry.

This chunk therefore gives the previous carrier analysis a temporal and reproductive depth it did not yet have. The problem is no longer only how to make a local surface answerable. It is how to keep profiles from hardening into self-reproducing recognition, how to let anomalies stay anomalous long enough to gather carriers, and how to distinguish a living counter-line from noise without demanding premature proof. If I extend the point, I would say that the chunk is trying to design the prehistory of revision: the legibility thresholds, heuristics, shelters, and tests through which another order can begin to become readable before the current one translates it away. That phrasing is mine, but it follows the sequence closely and leaves open the next problem of how anomalies should be compared without immediate recapture.

# Operational Translation

## Configurational Legibility Thresholds

Textual pressure: no single factor decides how much legibility a local carrier should bear.
Interpretive translation: minimum sufficient legibility depends on the carrier's force, risk, horizon, burden effects, reversibility, and inheritance likelihood.
Audit-design implication: size provenance, rationale, burden, override, and traceability requirements by the carrier's combined profile rather than by genre label, length, or declared localness.
Scope: prompt/default/scaffold governance and criticizability thresholds.
Confidence: high.

## Revisable Carrier Profiles

Textual pressure: recurring carrier configurations are useful, but rigid typologies would flatten situated differences.
Interpretive translation: profiles should function as heuristic recognition aids, not as exhaustive classes.
Audit-design implication: maintain recurrent carrier profiles as revisable operational handles that help compare cases and size legibility requirements while still forcing case-specific divergence notes.
Scope: audit ontology, carrier comparison, artifact classification.
Confidence: high.

## Profile Revision Triggers

Textual pressure: profiles do not revise themselves through example accumulation alone.
Interpretive translation: revision is warranted when anomaly, rival maps, failed interventions, ecological shifts, new risks, or hidden carriers strain the old profile beyond responsible use.
Audit-design implication: define explicit triggers for extending, splitting, or abandoning a profile when it explains away anomaly, obscures differences, or loses orienting force under changed conditions.
Scope: profile maintenance and methodological revision.
Confidence: high.

## Reproductive Supports Of Stale Profiles

Textual pressure: non-revision is sustained by reenactment as much as by belief.
Interpretive translation: habit, authority, convenience, prestige, cognitive economy, infrastructural embedding, and defensive intelligibility reproduce stale profiles.
Audit-design implication: treat profile inertia as a governance problem by tracing the templates, prompts, categories, prestige lines, and workflow conveniences that keep the old profile alive.
Scope: inertia diagnosis, governance analysis, process review.
Confidence: high.

## Conditions For Revisability

Textual pressure: anomaly and even crisis can be reabsorbed into inherited terms.
Interpretive translation: revision needs supportive conditions such as counter-maps, rival carriers, protected reinterpretive space, practical failure pressure, and a shift in what the order can recognize.
Audit-design implication: when revision is needed, ask what supporting carriers and protections are missing rather than assuming more evidence alone will move the profile.
Scope: revision planning, counter-mapping strategy, governance response.
Confidence: high.

## Shelter For Rival Relays

Textual pressure: anomaly has to remain anomalous long enough to form another intelligibility.
Interpretive translation: shelter is a temporal, procedural, textual, institutional, or practical condition that slows recapture and gives anomaly carriers of its own.
Audit-design implication: provide anomaly shelters through registers, delay mechanisms, counter-artifacts, distributed discussion, protected experimentation, or other forms that prevent immediate redescriptive capture.
Scope: review design, research lanes, canon-adjacent experimentation.
Confidence: high.

## Diagnostic Protection Against Noise

Textual pressure: preserving anomaly should not collapse into preserving every disturbance indefinitely.
Interpretive translation: a live anomaly is one that can become a rival relay under limited protection; noise does not acquire recurrence, relational consequence, comparative traction, or transmissible articulation.
Audit-design implication: evaluate protected anomalies by whether they remain named, survive succession, gather comparison, show consequences, and begin to articulate another question-path, while allowing isolated disturbance to fade responsibly.
Scope: anomaly review, triage, preservation rules.
Confidence: high.

# Workflow Translation

## Legibility Sizing Review

Inferential step: because local carriers can have very different force, risk, and inheritance profiles, workflow should review how much legibility each one actually needs.
Workflow consequence: prompt, template, and scaffold review should ask how much order the carrier exercises, what burden or foreclosure its opacity could produce, how reversible its effects are, and what minimum apparatus would keep it challengeable.
Scope: prompt design, template governance, workflow-surface review.
Confidence: high.

## Carrier Profile Registry

Inferential step: because recurring profiles help recognition without replacing situated judgment, workflow should keep a revisable registry of profile handles rather than ad hoc labels or rigid taxonomies.
Workflow consequence: artifact review and governance workflows should name the closest carrier profile, record where the case exceeds or breaks it, and keep the profile itself open to extension, splitting, or retirement.
Scope: audit taxonomy, review templates, governance documentation.
Confidence: high.

## Profile Inertia Audit

Inferential step: because stale profiles persist through reproductive supports, workflow should inspect the supports rather than blaming isolated oversight.
Workflow consequence: when a profile keeps surviving despite anomaly, review should trace what template, prestige line, workflow convenience, or infrastructural embedding is reenacting it and what loss or redistribution revision would require.
Scope: retrospectives, governance audits, process redesign.
Confidence: high.

## Protected Counter-Mapping Lanes

Inferential step: because anomaly needs carriers and space before it can support revision, workflow should provide lanes where counter-mapping can persist without immediate recapture.
Workflow consequence: audits and redesign efforts should maintain anomaly registers, counter-artifacts, experimental branches, delay mechanisms, or distributed discussion spaces that let rival articulation develop before it must justify itself fully in inherited terms.
Scope: research workflows, postmortems, architecture reconsideration, canon-prep work.
Confidence: high.

## Shelter Diagnostics

Inferential step: because shelter is both protective and diagnostic, workflow should review whether a preserved anomaly is becoming a live rival or fading as noise.
Workflow consequence: anomaly follow-up should check for recurrence, comparative traction, relational consequence, transmissible articulation, and emerging alternative discrimination, then either deepen support or let the disturbance fade responsibly.
Scope: anomaly triage, issue review, revision pipelines.
Confidence: high.
