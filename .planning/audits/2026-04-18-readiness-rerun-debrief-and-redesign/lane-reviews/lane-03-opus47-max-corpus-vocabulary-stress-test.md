## Case A Without Corpus

### Operative move

The case has two artifacts, and its operative move is the contrast between them. The first artifact (`05-sensitivity-converged-synthesis-output.md`) issues a `proceed to a narrow canon-patch response pass` gate verdict with `0` blocking findings and `3` patch-first findings, framed as `safe enough to proceed / narrow enough to patch lightly / still open enough that the patch must not pretend to close rankings`. The second artifact (`05-post-sensitivity-response-plan.md`) is a self-correction. It accepts the gate as a gate but rejects it as a headline outcome, and replaces the narrow `passes / patch lightly` framing with a `canon uplift + Milestone 2 carry-forward + long-arc steering` framing, recommending a two-step `proposal then execution` shape and a `REQUIREMENTS.md → PROJECT.md → LONG-ARC.md → ROADMAP.md` execution order.

The operative move, in one line, is: **a post-verificationist refusal to let a passing gate stand as the headline conclusion of a substantively richer round.**

### What the baseline vocabulary can already say

The existing doctrine and claim-marker system carry this case almost in full.

- The `POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md` definition of `post-verificationist review` is exactly the move the self-correction makes. The doctrine reads `post-verificationist review` as `refusing to stop at "it passes"` and as asking `what remains materially weak, under-justified, under-propagated, or misleadingly complete even when the artifact can technically clear a pass/fail gate`. The response plan's diagnosis — "the converged output translated a large amount of substantive deliberation into a narrow `passes / patch lightly` posture" — is a textbook instance of that posture being invoked against an upstream artifact.
- The doctrine's `Anti-Regret Scope Judgment` clause is even more directly load-bearing. It explicitly requires a two-sided locality judgment: not only `promotion is not required`, but also `non-promotion is defensible without avoidable regret`. The original synthesis's narrow patch was effectively a one-sided non-promotion. The correction explicitly invokes the second side: a narrow patch would underuse earned doctrine and would export Milestone 2 friction. That is anti-regret reasoning in the doctrine's own language.
- The doctrine's `progressive` vs `degenerating` distinction also discriminates the two artifacts. A response that protects the gate cosmetically while creating downstream re-litigation pressure satisfies the definition of `degenerating response`. A response that solves a real problem while improving operational coherence across the surrounding canon satisfies `progressive response`.
- The doctrine's `Deviation, Deferral, And Opportunity Handling` clause explicitly demands that real opportunities be routed visibly into `OPPORTUNITIES.md` or `TASKS.md` rather than silently absorbed. The correction's "make preserve-only and reversal-sensitive branches more explicit where silence is doing too much work" is the same pattern.
- The `CLAIM-TYPES.md` markers also discriminate the two artifacts cleanly. The original synthesis's headline verdict reads as `[s:b:i]` ("safe enough to proceed", a stipulated local rule with weak support) plus `[g:c:i]` patch directives. The correction reframes the verdict as a gate, not an endpoint, and adds new `[g:r:i]` doctrine ("the canon and roadmap now reflect the best mature-product doctrine we have actually earned"). The marker shift from `s:b:i` to `g:r:i` already names the change in epistemic status.

### Recommendation that follows from the baseline

- Adopt the corrected next step. Pursue the broader `canon uplift + Milestone 2 carry-forward + long-arc steering` response in the two-step `proposal then execution` shape recommended by the response plan, and in the recommended `REQUIREMENTS.md → PROJECT.md → LONG-ARC.md → ROADMAP.md → optional 01-CONTEXT.md → consistency review` order. Treat `STATE.md` as relay-only.
- Hold the gate verdict and the broader response as two different things. The gate verdict still licenses a response pass; it does not license the cosmetic patch the gate verdict's own headline initially implied.
- Keep the still-open rankings open. Do not let the broader uplift become a winner-selecting pass on wrappers, money family, hosting identity, contribution doctrine, or event-memory shape.

### What remains open, deferred, or contested

- Exactly which earned doctrine merits durable promotion into `LONG-ARC.md` versus continued residence in audit memory.
- Which preserve-only seams need explicit status language and which can survive on negative space alone for one more round.
- Whether the broader response itself risks creating a different overreach failure mode by carrying too much earned doctrine into canon at once.
- Sequencing pressure relative to the readiness rerun and to `01-CONTEXT.md`.

## Case C Classification And Defense

**Primary classification:** `rephrasing_only`.

**Defense.** The R5.17d3 adjudication is operationally a function/force adjudication, and the corpus's vocabulary maps cleanly onto its central moves. ## Cross-Case Inheritance Judgment

The three cases force a sober answer. The cases are themselves strong examples of the existing doctrine working at high discipline. The corpus's distinctions map onto the cases as more compact labels for moves the doctrine already authorizes and the artifacts already execute. No distinction earned operational inheritance on this evidence; several earned reflexive-only inheritance as reviewer-register vocabulary; one or two earned nothing meaningful here.

Specifically: `function / force` for declared-vs-operational divergence; `declared / effective authority` for the same gap framed by authority rather than force; `graded underreach` (explicit miss / traceable undercarry / retrospective possibility) for hindsight-calibrated critique; `reflective / reinforcing / reproductive / instituting` for carry-forward strength gradation; `fertile / empty incompleteness` for the maturity of preserve-only seams.

## What The Corpus Did Not Earn

- **Operational inheritance into governing doctrine.** No case showed the corpus tipping a decision the doctrine would not have tipped, or surfacing a residual-open question the doctrine could not have surfaced. Promoting any corpus distinction into load-bearing doctrine on this evidence would be unjustified.
- **A sibling analytic layer.** The derivative translation's two-layer ontology proposal (lower layer for explicit operational units; upper layer for discourse-forming handles) is not justified by these three cases. The existing claim-marker system handled all three cases adequately. A second layer would be paying for capacity the cases did not exercise.

- **Repo-wide vocabulary uplift on the strength of three cases.** Three cases is not a sufficient base for repo-wide propagation of corpus distinctions, even if the distinctions are genuinely sharper as labels. The spec's anti-misread rules forbid inferring from one or even three successful labelings that a distinction deserves doctrine promotion.

The cases test only whether named distinctions like `function / force` change anything operationally. The broader theoretical apparatus could be right or wrong; this lane does not adjudicate it.

- **Inheritance of `decay diagnostics for preserved tensions` even reflexively.** Surfaces a question the doctrine already covers with weaker temporal framing. Did not earn its own slot on this evidence.
- **Demonstration that the existing claim-marker system is insufficient.** The cases used the existing markers tightly and to good effect. The corpus does not show a marker the existing system cannot produce; it shows alternative labels for things the existing system already classifies.

## Honest Failure-Mode Registry

The lane could fail in any of the following ways, and a skeptical reviewer should test each:

- **Over-credit failure**: reading the corpus too sympathetically and finding warrant change where there is only descriptive sharpening. Mitigation: every claimed corpus contribution was tested against the spec's anti-misread rules and the question "does the doctrine already license the same move?" In each case the answer was yes.
- **Under-credit failure**: reading the doctrine too generously and missing where the corpus actually surfaced a question the doctrine does not carry. The candidate underclaims are: the `decay diagnostics for preserved tensions` pressure on Case B; the `answerable indirect carriers` specification missing from Case C's adjudication. Both are genuine corpus contributions; both are absorbable into existing doctrine without changing the case dispositions. The judgment that they are absorbable is itself contestable.
- **Over-generalization failure**: extrapolating from three cases to corpus-wide claims in either direction. Mitigation: the inheritance judgment is explicitly bounded to "on this evidence." Promotion or rejection of corpus distinctions on a different evidence base is not adjudicated by this lane.
- **Elegant-labeling failure**: treating "more elegant naming" as automatic earning. This is the spec's primary anti-misread rule. The lane's classification of all three cases as `rephrasing_only` is the strongest mitigation: elegant labels are credited as reflexive-only inheritance, not as operational gain.
- **Selection-bias failure**: ignoring corpus distinctions that don't fit the three cases but might fit other cases. The corpus's `intervention menus for indirect rule`, `hidden governance regimes` catalog, and broader theoretical framing apparatus may be load-bearing in other cases not tested here. The lane's negative inheritance judgment for these is conditional on this evidence base.
- **Frame-inheritance failure**: accepting the spec's candidate-distinction list as the correct stress-test set. The spec selected these distinctions as "the most likely stress-test candidates"; it is possible the corpus's actually-strongest distinctions are not on this list. The mitigation is to also note distinctions encountered in Stage 2 that the spec did not flag (e.g., `regimes of hidden governance`, `decay diagnostics`, `answerable indirect carriers`), and to test those as well. They were tested above and reached the same conclusion.
- **Skeptical-prior failure**: the reviewer's prior knowledge that the workspace debates corpus-as-sprawl biases toward conservative reading. The procedural mitigation (`What The Corpus Added` written before `What The Corpus Did Not Earn`) is partial. A sympathetic-prior reviewer running the same comparison might find the same `rephrasing_only` classifications but read the inheritance judgment as harsher than necessary.
- **Spec-frame failure**: the spec itself specifies the four classification options and the three inheritance categories. If those options are too coarse (e.g., if `rephrasing_only` collapses meaningful sub-distinctions like "rephrasing that aids spec-writing economy" vs "rephrasing that adds nothing"), the lane's classifications could be too blunt. The mitigation is to record this concern in the failure-mode registry rather than smuggle the sub-distinction into the classification language.
- **Case-scope failure**: the three cases were chosen because they were likely to either reward or expose corpus distinctions. A sample biased toward "doctrine-friendly" cases (cases where the doctrine was already operating at high discipline) would predictably show rephrasing-only outcomes. All three cases here are doctrine-friendly in exactly that sense. A different sample (cases where doctrine struggled) might show different results.

## Disposition Recommendation

The spec asks three questions. They are answered in order.

### Should a proposal lane for bounded doctrine supplementation be opened?

**No, not on this evidence.** Three cases of `rephrasing_only` are not a sufficient base for promoting any corpus distinction into governing doctrine. A bounded doctrine-supplementation lane could be justified by a different evidence base — specifically, by cases where the existing doctrine genuinely under-determines disposition and where the corpus's distinctions do operational work the doctrine cannot do. None of the three cases tested here meet that bar.

A narrower variant of the proposal lane is plausible: a *vocabulary-curation* lane (not a doctrine-supplementation lane) that names a small set of corpus distinctions — `function / force`, `declared / effective authority`, `graded underreach`, `fertile / empty incompleteness`, `answerable indirect carriers` — as standardized reviewer-register lexicon. That would let future audit specs use the labels compactly without giving them governing force. Whether even that vocabulary lane is worth the workspace's attention is a separate judgment that depends on whether the existing prose-with-doctrine-citations idiom is felt to be too verbose for routine spec-writing. This lane does not adjudicate that question.

### Should the corpus remain reviewer-register only?

**Yes, on this evidence.** The corpus distinctions earned reflexive-only inheritance at most. They are useful as compact labels that an experienced reviewer can deploy in spec-writing or in audit prose, but they did not change any of the three cases tested. The corpus should remain a reviewer-register resource — *named*, *citable*, *reusable* — without being promoted into load-bearing canon, governance doctrine, or workflow scaffolding.

### Or should the workspace narrow back toward the lane-01 revisions without corpus uplift?

**Yes, on this evidence.** The lane-01 revisions can proceed on the strength of the doctrine that already carried the bridge-audit, the sensitivity correction, and the R5.17d3 adjudication. They do not require corpus uplift to land cleanly. Adding corpus distinctions to the revisions on the strength of three rephrasing-only cases would be the kind of premature inheritance the spec's anti-misread rules forbid.

### Synthesis

The honest disposition is the conservative one: keep the corpus as reviewer-register vocabulary; let lane-01 revisions proceed without corpus uplift; if the workspace later runs a different stress test against doctrine-stressed cases (cases where the doctrine genuinely under-determines disposition), reopen the question of operational inheritance at that point. A bounded vocabulary-curation lane is plausible but not required.

This recommendation is intended to be readable in two registers. A skeptical reviewer who thinks the corpus is philosophical sprawl should be able to read this artifact and see that the corpus did not earn operational inheritance on the three cases tested. A corpus-sympathetic reviewer should also be able to see that the corpus's distinctions are credited as accurate reviewer-register labels, that the strongest forward-design contribution (`answerable indirect carriers`) is named explicitly, and that the negative inheritance judgment is conditional on this evidence base rather than absolute. Both readings should find the inheritance judgment harder to fake than a less-disciplined comparison would have made it.
