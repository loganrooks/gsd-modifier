## 1. Overall Resituation Judgment

The workspace has, in the interval between lane-01 and this review, moved one step further than the lane-01 disposition already authorized. It did not just accept lane-01's structural critique. It opened a large derivative philosophical inquiry whose depth and internal discipline exceed what any single rewrite of the audit setup would have produced, while also substantially raising the risk that the workspace is now producing vocabulary faster than it is producing testable interventions.

Three things are true together and must be held together:

1. The inquiry corpus has earned real interpretive gains that the audit workspace did not have before it. Graded underreach vocabulary, the function/force split, absorption-mechanism diagnostics, productive tension versus accidental contradiction, prelicensed-outcome diagnostics, and the discipline around conservative openness are not decorative. They give later audit lanes specific shapes of failure and success that the existing `POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md`, `CLAIM-TYPES.md`, and `../governance/AUDIT-CHARTER.md` do not currently carry.
2. The inquiry has not yet tested itself against a single concrete readiness-era artifact. Chunk 24 even names this gap: the note pivots from abstract machinery to "what most blocks the readiness-rerun debrief now?" but never actually executes the test it argues for. The operational translations in every chunk are high-confidence, but their value as operational guidance has not been demonstrated on any case; they remain plausible rather than verified.
3. The corpus is now itself a load-bearing artifact inside the audit workspace, and it has acquired the kind of interpretive gravity that makes silent inheritance easy. Its own doctrine about curation asymmetry, function/force divergence, and conservative openness applies reflexively to it. Under its own standards, the corpus has not yet passed the test it sets for inherited material.

Those three together imply the right next move is neither to operationalize the corpus into CLAIM-TYPES, discuss-phase, or readiness doctrine, nor to bury it as interesting but non-actionable, but to run a bounded test that forces the corpus to earn its inheritance on at least one readiness-era case. If it earns partial inheritance, then proposal-level uplift of specific surfaces becomes justified. If it does not, the workspace has honestly learned that the philosophical register has outrun the intervention it is meant to support, and the recovery move is to narrow back toward the runtime/harness evidence lane lane-01 already pressed for.

The main audit wave should not launch yet. Lane-01's high-leverage revisions (1–6) are still the more urgent unblocking work and are largely independent of the corpus. The corpus has changed what a serious rerun-design audit can ask, but it has not changed whether the workspace is yet ready to run one.

## 2. What The Inquiry Corpus Actually Earns

I am strict with this list. Only distinctions that either (a) name something the existing repo vocabulary could not carry without distortion, or (b) meaningfully sharpen an existing distinction, belong here. I exclude distinctions that are reconstructible from existing doctrine without loss.

### 2.1 Genuinely new operational distinctions

- **Graded underreach vocabulary: `explicit miss` / `traceable undercarry` / `retrospective possibility`** (chunks 25–26). The current repo has `gap-exposure`, `under-propagated`, `premature closure`, and anti-regret language, but no graded continuity vocabulary. The difference matters: lane-03 reseed-judgment's "the readiness-era mapping was not directionally wrong so much as under-grounded" is a traceable-undercarry claim in all but name, and the corpus now gives that judgment a register it did not have before. The vocabulary also disciplines the tone-of-critique problem the lane-01 review raised implicitly by asking whether the bridge-audit verdict itself was tame; the right answer in corpus terms is that the bridge-audit was under-undertaking the same failure mode by using one accusatory register for multiple continuity strengths.
- **Function/force split for artifacts** (chunk 13). This names something `ARTIFACT-GOVERNANCE.md`'s active/historical/superseded/generated-reference/archived taxonomy does not carry: the divergence between what an artifact claims to do and what it actually orders downstream. `../planning-notes/SESSION-FRAMING-BRIEF.md`'s own self-flag is an early operator intuition of this point; the corpus makes it a first-class review object.
- **Conservative openness diagnostic** (chunk 19). The reframing of "openness must redistribute, not merely defer" blocks a specific failure mode that existing `preserve-only seams` language does not: declaring something open without burdening any default. This is the sharpest corrective the corpus offers to the repo's own future-flexibility statusing.
- **Absorption-mechanism typology** (chunk 5). Scale mismatch, decoupling, temporal dissipation, burden recapture, personnel dependence, rhetorical domestication. The existing readiness doctrine recognizes that reforms can be cosmetically absorbed but does not type the mechanism. When a later debrief asks "why did the `05-gap-closure` canon uplift succeed at doctrine but not at governance follow-through," the typology discriminates among hypotheses the current language collapses into generic "governance weakness."
- **Function-independent force analysis of indirect carriers** (chunks 13–14). `CLAIM-TYPES.md`, `AGENTS.md` pushback language, and `discuss-phase.md` gray-area framing are all indirect carriers in the corpus's sense. The corpus's demand for minimum sufficient legibility on indirect carriers applies directly and names a real gap.
- **Fertile versus empty incompleteness** (chunk 23). The current repo's `bounded-open branches` and `inquiry debt` language is compatible with both. The corpus's criteria let a reviewer say, of a preserved open question, whether it is oriented or merely parked.
- **Productive tension versus accidental contradiction** (chunk 18). Directly applicable to the repo's four-way tension between root `AGENTS.md` compactness, `.planning/AGENTS.md` rigor, `WORKFLOW.md` breadth, and `AI-GUARDRAILS.md` guardrail density.
- **Heroism-conversion thresholds** (chunk 16). The operator's repeatedly flagged pattern of reasserting the same anti-closure stance lane by lane — visible across `POST-FALSIFICATIONIST-REVIEW-DOCTRINE.md`, readiness CHECKPOINT-REVIEW-MATRIX, and the SESSION-FRAMING-BRIEF — is exactly the "heroism still substituting for carrier" condition this chunk defines.

### 2.2 Genuine sharpenings of existing distinctions

- **Declared versus effective authority** (chunk 4). This sharpens the existing `direct doctrine` / `bounded-open branches` / `preserve-only seams` / `reversal-sensitive boundaries` / `inquiry debt` categorization by cross-cutting it: any of those categories can be declared one way and effective another. The `.planning/AGENTS.md:84-94` listing is categorizing declared status; the corpus adds the orthogonal question of effective force.
- **Prelicensed-outcome diagnostic** (chunk 22). Sharpens the existing `anti-regret scope judgment` and `non-promotion is not a neutral default` doctrine. The existing doctrine asks whether non-promotion is defensible. The corpus adds the prior test of whether the review setup has already pre-licensed non-promotion through burden asymmetry, inherited ease, or framing.
- **Revealing-case criteria** (chunk 25). Sharpens the existing audit practice of selecting specific `05-gap-closure` rounds or Checkpoint 5 cases for scrutiny. The criteria (rich prior terrain, thin recommendation, visible burden asymmetry, later corrective pressure, downstream force) give the selection a defensible shape.
- **Configurational-not-scalar judgment** (chunks 10–11, 17). Sharpens the existing refusal of single master criteria throughout the repo doctrine. The corpus is explicit that this is not relativism, which is an addition to the existing `AUDIT-COMPARISON-POLICY.md`-style comparative standards.

### 2.3 Distinctions whose operational value is still projected rather than earned

These are real moves but have not yet been tested on a single repo artifact:

- The two-layer ontology sketch (chunks 1–2). Operationally attractive, but the corpus warns against it hardening into another objectification layer and does not demonstrate the sibling layer doing work on a repo case.
- Succession-typed durability with ensemble carriers (chunks 6–7). Conceptually strong, but the only carriers specifically named in this repo are already-existing artifacts (`CONTEXT.md`, `CLAIM-TYPES.md`, `AGENTS.md`). The ensemble-carrier frame has not yet diagnosed a specific missing carrier in the live harness.
- The materiality-review / block-function / versioning architecture (chunks 27–32). These chunks are building a governance apparatus for the inquiry's own schema. Their application to the live repo is not yet demonstrated, and their accumulation raises the inquiry's own drift risk.
- Interrupt infrastructure and dependency-map revisability (chunks 35–37). Provisionally valuable, but the corpus closes without naming a single interrupt actually missing in the live readiness/harness workflow.

I list these not to dismiss them but to be honest that their confidence markers in the corpus are high while their demonstrated operational leverage is still zero.

## 3. What It Pressures But Does Not Yet Settle

### 3.1 Ontology of the audit itself

The corpus's opening move — treating `claim` as an operational handle rather than the final unit, and introducing a second layer for framing / distinction / vocabulary / exclusion / inheritance / sequencing / closure / orientation — is pressure on `CLAIM-TYPES.md` and on `.planning/AGENTS.md`'s claim-marker rules. But the corpus itself pulls back three times (chunks 2, 19, 31) to warn that this supplement could reproduce the same objectification at a higher level, and it never resolves whether the sibling layer should be (a) a parallel schema, (b) a prose-only analytic register with no tags, (c) a set of required register-shifts inside claim notes, or (d) a deliberately un-operationalized frame that only governs reviewer reading. This is a settled pressure, not a settled prescription.

### 3.2 The readiness-package underreach diagnosis

Chunk 24 explicitly names "primary failure-mode diagnosis" as the highest-leverage blocker and proposes recommendation-against-terrain as the first comparative slice. Chunk 25 says begin with one revealing case. The corpus then generates more vocabulary instead of running that test. The diagnosis it was about to enable is not in the corpus. This is the single sharpest hole.

### 3.3 Whether "another readiness attempt" is the right unit

Lane-01 explicitly posed this (Proposals E execute-and-learn and F harness-code-first). The corpus does not directly engage the challenge. Its language about "orders," "carriers," "relays," and "self-carrying change" is compatible with both the audit-first paradigm and the execute-first or harness-first alternatives, but it does not adjudicate. The corpus's chunk 7 discussion of inauguration versus mere opposition would arguably support harness-code-first more than audit-first, but the corpus does not draw that inference.

### 3.4 The Codex runtime/overlay as first-class evidence

The function/force split and the indirect-carrier legibility rule both imply that Codex skills, overlay sources, workflow templates, and hook scripts must be evidence rather than downstream consequence. But no chunk engages a specific Codex runtime artifact. The pressure is there; the move is not.

### 3.5 The vocabulary's own closure-bias risk

Lane-01 asked whether the post-falsificationist vocabulary is itself a closure-bias amplifier. The corpus extends that pressure to its own new vocabulary (chunks 32, 33's anti-ladder safeguards) but never tests it. Specifically: graded underreach vocabulary has an obvious failure mode the corpus acknowledges (hidden seriousness ladder) but does not test against a single case.

### 3.6 The operator-bandwidth alternative

Lane-01 raised the possibility that underreach is primarily an operator-bandwidth or operator-taste pattern rather than a mapping or doctrine pattern. The corpus's heroism-conversion language (chunk 16) is the closest engagement and is compatible with this reading, but the corpus does not operationalize it as a competing hypothesis. The single-operator-with-Codex execution model remains unnamed as a specific execution assumption, which lane-01 revision 6 already requires.

### 3.7 What counts as a live future for this repo specifically

Chunks 8–9 set strong criteria for live-future recognition (seams, carriers, partial articulation, period-available capacities). The existing readiness `preserve-only` and `reversal-sensitive` doctrine names live futures at the product level (wrapper order, audience-right bundle, money-family ranking, host-identity). The corpus does not test any of those against its own live-future criteria. Doing so might strengthen or weaken the existing preservation set, but the corpus has not performed that work.

## 7. What Should Not Be Rewritten Yet

The spec requires naming at least one thing, why it would be premature, and what evidence or intermediate lane would make it less premature. I name one primary and two secondary.

### 7.1 Primary: `.planning/CLAIM-TYPES.md`

**What would make it less premature.** A concrete lane that picks 2–3 readiness-era recommendations or closures (candidates: the `05-gap-closure` sensitivity-verdict narrowing; the bridge-audit `revise + guarded hybrid reseed` framing; one Checkpoint 5 `R5.17` exclusion judgment) and attempts to write a full review of each using only the existing `CLAIM-TYPES.md` markers plus disciplined prose.
