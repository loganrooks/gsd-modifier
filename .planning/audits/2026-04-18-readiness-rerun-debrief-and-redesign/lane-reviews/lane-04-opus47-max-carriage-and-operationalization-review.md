## Carriage Criteria

This review applies the spec's nine criteria explicitly. A candidate gain is stronger when it improves several at once. The criteria are used as test lenses, not as a scoring rubric; they rule out some moves (e.g., a move that adds only sharper labels without changing diagnosis / routing / reproducibility / implementability is downgraded to `reviewer_register_only`) and raise others (e.g., a move that improves reproducibility, burden visibility, and implementability at once is eligible for `ready_for_local_proposal`).

1. **Diagnostic precision** — does the move sharpen what failure mode is being named?
2. **Burden / authority visibility** — does it expose who owes what justification?
3. **Intervention routing** — does it help pick among remedy families?
4. **Reproducibility by later reviewers** — can a later reader redo the judgment without the current operator's prose?
5. **Reduced dependence on unusually strong operator prose or heroics** — does it survive a less-heroic orchestrator?
6. **Indirect-carrier legibility** — does it make small scaffolds answerable without inflating them into doctrine?
7. **Openness that actually redistributes defaults** — does it de-prelicense rather than only defer?
8. **Technical or procedural implementability** — is it buildable as bounded infrastructure, not only as prose?
9. **Improved attainable-outcome quality rather than merely adequate pass/fail sufficiency** — does it raise the ceiling, not just clear a floor?

2. **Disposition as state change** (chunk 36: `act now` / `open inquiry` / `defer with updated trigger` / `retire`). A deferred idea gets carried forward in prose but has no concrete wake condition, no affected decision surface, and no next-expected-wake-point. Promoting every `<deferred>` entry to the full chunk-34 debt schema would be ceremony; requiring a concrete trigger + decision surface for items that matter enough to cross phases is bounded.

4. **Fertile / empty incompleteness** (chunk 23) applied to `<open_questions>`. The template accepts open questions without requiring that each be fertile (oriented unknown + directional next move) rather than empty (vague underdevelopment). The corpus's distinction is operationally testable: can the open question state what remains open, why it matters, and what next inquiry would clarify it? If yes, the question is fertile and belongs in phase steering. If no, it should be downgraded or retired. This works as a reviewer-register rule rather than as a template field, because template fields risk turning every open question into a mini-spec.

Two distinctions map onto the surface but are overbroad for bounded operationalization:

- **Grammar of consequence as ensemble** (chunk 18). Useful diagnostic vocabulary for describing what the workflow does (it is an ensemble of doctrine + prompts + burden rules + reviews), but not an implementable local move.
- **Profile revision triggers / reproductive supports of stale profiles** (chunk 14). Describes why workflows ossify; would support a meta-audit of the workflow itself but widens beyond the surface set.

### Remaining limits

- Every move here risks adding ceremony to carriers that are already working. The operational test is whether the move changes downstream behavior, not whether it is elegant.
- Disposition-as-state-change is testable on one phase; if it turns out that adding `disposition:` per open-question entry does not change planner or executor behavior, the template edit should be rolled back rather than normalized.
- Answerable indirect carriers is the strongest corpus contribution but the hardest to scope; adding minimum-legibility signals to every workflow file simultaneously is the ceremonial trap. The right test is one carrier at a time.
