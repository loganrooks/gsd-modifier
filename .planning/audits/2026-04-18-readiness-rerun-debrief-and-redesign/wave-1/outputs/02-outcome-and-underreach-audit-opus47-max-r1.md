- Anchor 1 — `R5.16 → R5.17 → R5.18` chain as a mapping-and-judgment case study.
  - What happened: Track B and Track C internal audits found real producer-side improvements and mostly stayed at `revise-current`. Cross-vendor lanes broke out of the specs' consumer lists, read into `agent-contracts.md`, `roadmap.cjs`, `plan-phase.md:827-834`, `transition.md`, `ship.md`, `autonomous.md`, and `checkpoints.md`, and found a governance-level convergence pattern.   - Why this matters: this is the package's highest-powered loop. It correctly identified the strongest available pattern, correctly widened scope, correctly executed a carrier-layer implementation — and then stopped at annotation rather than structural extension, and stopped at propagation-by-content rather than propagation-by-naming. The chain is neither a failure nor a clean win; it is the signature of the package's actual reach.
  - Judgment vs. mapping reading: the mapping weakness (under-read internal specs) was real but was exogenously corrected by cross-vendor lanes. The judgment weakness (one-sided non-promotion default, light remedy selection at landing) was internal and was not corrected. The net shape is mapping-recovered / judgment-undercarried.
