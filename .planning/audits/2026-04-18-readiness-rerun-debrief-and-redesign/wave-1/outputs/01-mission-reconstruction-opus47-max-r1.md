## 1. Overall Mission Judgment

- [d:r:i] The readiness package was not attempting a narrow documentation cleanup. It was attempting a four-layer structural intervention: (a) doctrine preservation, (b) governance-doc normalization, (c) workflow/harness ownership realignment, (d) rerun of Phase 01 under the preserved doctrine and corrected harness. Each layer had a distinct theory of change; collectively they formed a doctrine → governance → harness → rerun causal chain.
- [d:r:i] The **explicit** mission was defensible as stated: finish governance/process cleanup, verify the rerun will honestly consume uplifted canon, then rerun Phase 01. Sources: `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PLAN.md:6`, `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PLAN.md:61-65`, `.planning/readiness/phase-01-rerun/PLAN.md:131-145`.
- [d:r:i] The **implicit** mission was larger and more demanding than the explicit one. The package was implicitly committed to: engineering a review posture (not merely a review checklist), pushing controls downward from docs into harness where the docs were compensating, and defending the 05-gap-closure doctrinal distinctions as the load-bearing payload. That implicit mission was load-bearing in sequencing decisions, closure denials, and scope arguments even where the PLAN did not state it in those terms.
- [d:r:i] The package was **internally coherent at the level of stance** — post-falsificationist review, anti-regret scope judgment, critical inheritance, independence rule, and bounded-checkpoint discipline form a self-consistent epistemic stance — but **internally contradictory at the level of enacted shape**. The anti-closure doctrine kept generating review findings that the sequencing rules were not structurally able to absorb, which produced recursive inward motion (R5.16 → R5.17 → R5.18 a1/a2/b/c/d → R5.19 a1–a5/b1–b5/c1–c5/d1–d4/e) inside a checkpoint that the PLAN had bounded as narrow follow-through.
- [d:r:i] This is the core mission finding: the readiness package's anti-tame doctrine was *correctly* aimed but structurally *unable to discharge outward* within the package's own sequencing rules. Every time the doctrine tried to honor itself, it generated more internal audit scope rather than releasing the package toward its own stated endpoint. This is not "the package failed at its mission"; it is "the package's explicit and implicit missions were in structural tension, and the implicit mission kept winning locally while the explicit mission kept being deferred."
- [d:r:i] The bridge audit's guarded-hybrid verdict was not the final mission statement. It was one lane's tame-leaning translation of a doctrinally anti-tame package into a program-revision-only posture. The charter has already correctly reframed that verdict as `candidate underreach artifact`. Sources: `.planning/audits/2026-04-18-readiness-rerun-debrief-and-redesign/governance/AUDIT-CHARTER.md:8`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-18-readiness-rerun-debrief-and-redesign/CURRENT-STATE.md:13`.
- [d:r:i] The SESSION-FRAMING-BRIEF's operator-added framing (structural intervention, future-preservation, anti-human-timeline bias, critical inheritance) is consistent with the implicit mission reconstruction here, but is itself situated and partial, and should not override direct package evidence where the two disagree. This lane treats the brief as corroboration where the package's own artifacts support it (e.g., anti-regret doctrine, future-seam preservation) and as operator-hypothesis-only support where it does not (e.g., the claim that underreach was specifically from weak *mapping*, which is a diagnostic claim belonging to the mapping-adequacy lane, not to mission reconstruction).

## 4. Theory Of Change

Register: theory-of-change summary.

### Summary sentence

The readiness package's theory of change was: **cleaning doctrine → normalizing governance → realigning harness ownership → then re-running Phase 01 under the preserved doctrine and realigned harness will produce a rerun that is strong enough to reliably carry earned doctrinal distinctions forward**, with a post-falsificationist review posture, commit/independence discipline, and bounded-checkpoint sequencing acting as the structural brakes against closure bias.

### Layered theory of change

1. **Canon layer (largely completed pre-sequence).** The 05-gap-closure audit landed canon uplift in `REQUIREMENTS.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `PROJECT.md`, `ROADMAP.md`. Theory: doctrinal distinctions matter more than phase boundaries and should not be silently flattened by later planning.
2. **Governance layer (Checkpoints 0–2).** Theory: governance docs (`AGENTS.md`, `.planning/AGENTS.md`, `WORKFLOW.md`, `AI-GUARDRAILS.md`, `ARTIFACT-GOVERNANCE.md`, `CLAIM-TYPES.md`) need claim-marker/citation hygiene and normalization so that each rule lives where its scope actually justifies it. Implicit assumption: doc quality is a leading indicator of downstream planning quality.
3. **Mapping layer (Checkpoint 3).** Theory: before judging the harness, honestly map the landscape (Codex orchestration, repo-local GSD flow, review/audit/verification surfaces, governance-that-compensates surfaces). Implicit assumption: mapping before judgment prevents scope drift driven by unmapped assumptions.
4. **Excellence-audit layer (Checkpoint 4).** Theory: test the tandem Codex+GSD stack against a rigor bar, not a pass/fail bar — does it reward excellence, or mostly detect obvious failure? Implicit assumption: a review structure that can only detect obvious failure is already underreaching by design.
5. **Harness-follow-through layer (Checkpoint 5).** Theory: if Checkpoint 4 finds real harness-ownership problems, fix them in narrow harness slices (worker-prompt alignment, review/closure-pressure follow-through, launch-truth capture) rather than papering over with more doc prose. Implicit assumption: harness fixes are durable where doc prose is compensatory.
6. **Rerun-readiness layer (Checkpoint 6).** Theory: verify that doctrine, governance, and harness are stable enough to run Phase 01 honestly rather than re-litigate under a new timestamp.
7. **Rerun layer (Checkpoint 7).** Theory: with layers 1–6 settled, a fresh `discuss → context → plan` for Phase 01 will produce planning that uses the stronger canon as steering advantage rather than regressing to corrected asymmetries.

### Structural brakes (cross-layer)

- **Post-falsificationist review posture**: prevents naive pass/fail closure and single-attack sovereignty.
- **Commit/checkpoint discipline**: prevents time-based snapshots with no reasoning boundary.
- **Independence rule**: prevents a lane from authoring and solely certifying its own closure.
- **Cross-vendor lane at load-bearing review boundaries**: prevents same-model echo.
- **Disposition ladder**: prevents silent omission of anomalies and opportunities.
- **Anti-regret two-sided scope judgment**: prevents non-intervention from being treated as a neutral default.
- **Seam category tracking**: prevents premature foreclosure of reversal-sensitive or preserve-only boundaries.

### Theory-of-change weaknesses now visible

- [d:r:i] The theory assumed each layer would *discharge* into the next. In practice, Checkpoint 5 became an attractor: the anti-closure brakes kept generating findings the layer's bounded scope could not absorb, producing inward recursion (R5.16 → R5.17 → R5.18 split → R5.19 parallel family cluster) rather than discharge to Checkpoint 6.
- [d:r:i] The theory had no explicit *exit condition from a brake*. It had entry triggers for escalation (post-falsificationist stop-and-escalate conditions, gap-handling ladder) but no trigger for "we have now audited the auditor enough; switch to execute-and-learn or harness-code-first instead."
- [d:r:i] The theory assumed that doc/governance/harness cleaning would be smaller than a rerun. In practice, the pre-rerun cleaning has now consumed more session budget than a first rerun attempt likely would have.
