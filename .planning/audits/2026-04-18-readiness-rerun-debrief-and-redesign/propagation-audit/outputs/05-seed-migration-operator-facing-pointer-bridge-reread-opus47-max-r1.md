## Where The Live Bridge Still Compresses Distinct Jobs

Eight places where helper, consumer workflows, specialist packet boundary, durable uplift memory, or typed propagation refresh still flatten distinct work into one surface. Each is named against a specific live-surface line.

1. **`seed_migration_candidate_count` aggregates three distinct populations into one scalar at the consumer surface.** The sum `legacy_unversioned + noncurrent_version_total + current_contract_shape_gap_count` is correct for gating the pointer, but the operator-facing line "Seed migration candidates: {count}" collapses three migration difficulties that have different remediation profiles. Twelve legacy-unversioned seeds and twelve current-shape-gap seeds trigger the same disclosure line despite wildly different `migration_moves` sets (the first requires stamping `seed_contract_version: 2` plus most other keys; the second typically requires one or two missing keys or sections). An operator reading "Seed migration candidates: 12" cannot decide whether to run the specialist packet with or without further preparation. A population-breakdown inside the same disclosure line (e.g., `Seed migration candidates: 12 (legacy 3 / noncurrent 4 / shape-gap 5)`) would preserve disclosure-without-execution without forcing the specialist packet as the only way to read the scalar.

2. **The specialist-packet pointer text `$gsd-seed-migration-inventory --write` folds the harden slice's inspect/write separation back into one default.** The harden slice landed `gsd-seed-migration-inventory/SKILL.md` with explicit text: "Default posture is detect-only. Point operators at `$gsd-seed-migration-inventory` when they want the deeper detect-only packet. Pass `--write` only when the caller explicitly wants durable migration-planning memory." `progress_note_seed_fields` returns the literal `"$gsd-seed-migration-inventory --write"` — the durable-side-effect form. An operator re-entering through `progress` and following the disclosed command writes `.planning/SEED-MIGRATION-REPORT.md` and `.planning/SEED-MIGRATION-MANIFEST.json` on first invocation, which is the precise "deeper packet" ↔ "durable write" merger the harden slice split. The bridge compresses the split at the helper's pointer literal; the SKILL still keeps it separated; the two are now out of alignment.

3. **The pointer string is a frozen literal inside the helper, uncoupled from the SKILL it points at.** `progress_note_seed_fields` returns `"$gsd-seed-migration-inventory --write"` as a hard-coded string. The `gsd-seed-migration-inventory/SKILL.md` name is not imported from anywhere, not asserted against by any contract test, and not re-exported from a shared constant. If the wrapper gets renamed or re-sharded (a move compatible with later family direction — e.g., `gsd-seed-migration-inspect` vs. `gsd-seed-migration-write`) the progress-note string goes stale without a failing test. Compare how `SEED_MIGRATION_CANDIDATE_LABEL` is tested against both workflows — the label has a cross-surface binding; the pointer string does not.

An edit that removes `Only show these lines when UPLIFT_NOTE.show_seed_migration_pointer is true` while keeping the label lines would pass this test while causing the pointer to disclose on quiet corpora. The helper test (`test_write_outputs_and_progress_note_detect_doctrine_change` and its siblings) proves the gating at the helper boundary; the consumer test stops at label presence. The two together still leave one failure mode — dropped conditional in the workflow — uncovered.

5. **`UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json` does not denormalize `seed_migration_candidate_count`.** The current manifest at `.planning/UPLIFT-MANIFEST.json` carries the three populations inside `seed_corpus_posture` (`current_contract_shape_gap_count`, `legacy_unversioned_count`, `noncurrent_version_total`) but no explicit aggregated scalar. The bridge computes the sum at progress-note time. If the helper's aggregation rule later shifts (e.g., a new population type gets added, or shape-gap gets sub-typed into "missing frontmatter key" vs. "missing section heading"), the durable memory will still store the three old populations; later readers of the manifest have no record of what the aggregation rule was when the memory was written. A denormalized `seed_migration_candidate_count` at the manifest level (alongside the three populations) would match how `show_seed_corpus_posture`'s underlying rule (`seed_file_count > 0`) is visible at the manifest level through `seed_file_count`.

6. **The held-later register pointer stayed frozen at the first-landing pointer rather than tracking the bridge.** `UPLIFT-HELD-LATER.md` line 10 still reads `legacy seed corpus migration — partially landed: tooling/portable-gsd/overlay/get-shit-done/workflows/seed-migration-inventory.md`. The bridge (`89/90`) did not upgrade that pointer to reflect the newer progress/resume disclosure surface or the accompanying refresh `37`. Output-04 raised the multi-pointer shape question at §5.6 (route the partially-landed family into both the live surface and the evidence trail). 7. **No frozen durable-output anchor carries the specialist-packet shape inside the evidence index.** The evidence-index entries `seed_migration_pointer_bridge_impl_90` and `seed_migration_pointer_refresh_37` point to implementation/refresh prose. - **It keeps detect-only and rewrite separation intact.** A fixture is synthetic, committed, and read-only. The end-to-end test does not write to `.planning/`. The specialist packet's `--write` behavior is still exercised only in existing tempfile tests; the bridge's conditional-rendering path is exercised through the fixture. No rewrite pressure, no auto-write implication, no generic wrapper sweep.

- **It reuses the bridge's own typing rather than opening a new family.** The fixture would be referenced from the evidence-index as an additional evidence row on the existing carriers (`project_uplift`, `progress_workflow`, `resume_workflow`, `seed_migration_inventory_helper`), not as a new carrier. The end-to-end test would be a new test file or a new test method inside existing test modules. Nothing in the semantic map changes; no new overlay ownership; no new wrapper.

- **It gives later rewrite/normalization, audit-open widening, and entry-wrapper retrofit each a calibration target.** Rewrite needs a frozen shape to plan against. Audit-open widening (if it ever lands) needs to know what the specialist packet currently emits. Entry-wrapper retrofit (in a narrower future form) needs to know what the disclosure contract looks like at the consumer edge. One fixture plus one end-to-end test gives all three a single reference point.

Why not the three candidates in their packet-named form:

- **Later entry-wrapper widening.** The narrow inheritance wins so far — `81/82`, `83/84`, `89/90` — each leveraged a pre-typed consumer edge in the semantic map. Extending to other wrappers would either require first typing new consumer edges (which is the map-work step the landed bridge already inherited) or would introduce disclosure at wrappers whose current evidence profile does not match the progress/resume re-entry surface's role. Hold until at least the fixture-exercise move has lifted the five-slice chain's evidence floor.

- **Broader audit-open consumer widening.** Audit-open already carries richer seed rows at milestone-close through `83/84`. Migration-candidate disclosure belongs to re-entry lifecycle, not milestone-close lifecycle. Folding them onto the same helper would overload the milestone-close judgment path with a different lifecycle concern. This was output-04's reasoning; the bridge preserves the separation; widening audit-open now would reopen it.

- **Rewrite or normalization automation.** The current chain has no real-corpus exercise. Designing rewrite against synthetic fixtures plus the helper's frozen shape-list freezes rewrite semantics against test scaffolding rather than observed corpora. Hold until at least one of Section 4's narrow sharpenings plus the fixture-exercise move above has lifted the foundation, and ideally until another repo importing the overlay has exercised the specialist packet against a real mixed corpus.

A second, companion adjacent move worth naming: **a narrowed version of Section 4 item 1 (the inspect/write pointer split) landed alongside the fixture-exercise move.** The inspect/write split corrects a compression the bridge itself introduced against the harden slice's split; the fixture-exercise move raises the evidence floor; together they preserve the harden slice's separation while converting paper chain into exercised chain. If one of the two has to lead, the fixture-exercise move is larger in leverage because it carries forward into whichever family opens next; the inspect/write split is cheaper (one helper function, two workflow lines, one test extension) and can ride alongside.
